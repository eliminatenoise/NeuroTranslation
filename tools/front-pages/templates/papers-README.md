# Two papers, each written twice

This page is the way into the project's two papers: what each one argues, which edition to read first, where each study sits in them, and what came too late to be in them.

**NeuroTranslation** asks what a recording of the brain or of a muscle adds to a forecast of a movement that already knows how the body has just been moving. It reports every study of the numbered table on [the studies page](../benchmarks/README.md), all {{ n_in_papers() }} rows, a macaque study in which two networks lowered the average error and still failed the rule set for them, and the project's earlier attempts at reading movement from recorded brain cells. Then it describes the general model being built, NeuroTranslate 0.0, and the recording study that would test it.

**Brain2Body** starts somewhere else: let a machine guide a hand through a tune, then stop the guidance. What has the person kept? The paper proposes three ways to connect to a body (a pad on the brain, a strip along the spine, and a suit of fine gold electrodes that prick the skin), argues for trying them first in a small marsupial, and sets out the experiment and the results that would sink it. None of the three is built, and it reports no experiment on that question.

Each paper has a Technical edition, for readers who want the method, and a Simple English edition, which explains the same things in ordinary words and takes more pages to do it. Start with a Simple English edition.

## Not in either paper: what came after them

The NeuroTranslation paper ends its account of the general model with its first {{ spell(C('general_learner', 'fits')) }} fits, in section 7.4. It then says, in one paragraph, that later fits are outside it: a release of 20 November with a fit on two datasets at once, and later releases with the model's first tests on new people, which it leaves to the repository's front page. ## Checked inside the project, and by nobody outside it

Each title page says "Working draft, {{ built_date() }}. Not peer reviewed.", and both are true.

Inside the project the papers have been read by checkers who had not written them: one re-read every number at the result file it came from and checked every statement against its source, the other read every page for plain words and the voice the papers are meant to have. They read the whole of two editions, then every change made after that, the licences included. Every fix in this edition has been checked: the last few were made in the words the last check gave. One kind of finding could not be settled without the cited papers themselves, and each paper says so above its references: figures quoted from other groups' papers were not re-read in the full papers. Nobody outside the project has reviewed the papers.

Where a paper and a result file disagree, the result file is right: [the studies page](../benchmarks/README.md) is written from the result files.
