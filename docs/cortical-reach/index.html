When the arm
is moved

A macaque holds a robotic handle. The apparatus nudges the arm. Later, a target appears and the animal reaches. If you already know the recent movement, do spikes from the brain’s sensory cortex help predict where the handle will go in the next tenth of a second?

Where the measurements came from

Chowdhury, Glaser and Miller recorded activity in area 2, a part of the sensory cortex that receives information about the body. A robot’s encoders recorded the handle position. A camera separately tracked ten points on the arm. Joint angles and muscle lengths were estimated from those markers with a musculoskeletal model.

The recordings come from one animal on one day. The archived handle and marker positions were processed offline. A prediction of a later stored value does not establish when that information would be available during a live reach.
