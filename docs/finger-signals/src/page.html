What will the glove read next?

A glove records a number for each finger as it bends. Give a model four readings and a cue to move one finger. Does adding electrical activity from the surface of the brain improve its guess 120 milliseconds ahead?

The last glove reading won.

Researchers recorded five glove sensors while one person followed finger cues on a screen. We fitted the models on __FIT__ earlier trials and checked their guesses against the recorded glove readings on __LATER__ later trials from the same recording.

Adding the brain signal increased the average error for all five fingers. The gain over glove and cue alone was __GAIN__ (95% interval __INTERVAL__). Giving the same model brain activity from an earlier trial with the same cue gave a small advantage to the correctly paired signal, __DONOR__ (95% interval __DONOR_INTERVAL__). That interval includes zero.

How the recorded trials were scored

Each trial paired a finger cue with the blank screen that followed. Each finger’s average absolute error was divided by the spread of its target readings during training (one standard deviation). Each trial and finger received equal weight. A gain is the comparison model’s error minus the model’s error with the correctly paired brain signal.

The 95% intervals resample consecutive blocks of three whole trials. The 68 forecasts per trial overlap. The study tested a single recording and did not establish transfer to another person. The glove and cortical recordings share a file grid. Their independent hardware timing and live availability remain unknown.

Methods, sources and rights

The two fitted linear models use ridge regression to predict the change from the latest glove value. The screen cue names the requested finger. Four input times span 120 ms. Each surface-electrode summary covers the preceding 160 samples at 1 kHz. The high band excludes bins around 120 Hz. This page uses the study weights and only invented inputs for the interactive forecasts.
