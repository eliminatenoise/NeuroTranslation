The hand’s own history still beats the brain signal

NeuroTranslation tries to predict one recording of a movement from another: brain, muscle, force and motion, all from open datasets. In 25 small studies so far, each a numbered row of one table on the studies page, what the hand had just done was the forecast to beat. Adding a brain recording taken at the scalp (EEG) made that forecast worse in 8 of 13 comparisons. Every study, with its numbers.

The pages on this site run the studies’ forecasts in your browser. Try an invented example, or open a recording from the dataset a study used.

    Can a recording from the surface of the brain beat the last glove reading?

    Give two fitted models of one person's glove the same invented finger movement, one of them with a signal from electrodes on the surface of the brain as well, and compare their forecasts of the five fingers.   • Katmah and colleagues · Treadmill joint angles

    Can four seconds of walking beat the last stride?

    Follow both knees through four seconds of a walk and forecast both angles a quarter of a second later. On 12 walkers, copying the matching moment of the previous stride missed by 3.535° on average; a small transformer trained to correct that copy missed by 2.679°, closer for every one of them; with ten walkers in a second laboratory, it was closer for only six. The general model, given the same four seconds, missed by 3.465°, and a copy of it with its shared part scrambled did better.

  • Katmah and colleagues · Treadmill muscle and knee recordings

    Do the muscles and knees repeat at the same rate?

    For 11 held-out walkers at 0.5 m/s, the strongest rhythms in the leg-muscle recordings and knee angles differed by 0.027 cycles per minute on average. Pairing each walk with another walker’s muscle recording raised the difference to 3.055. Each instrument supplied its own 40-second window. Agreement in rate leaves the timing of individual steps unresolved.

  • Katmah and colleagues · Treadmill joint angles

    Where will the knees be 15 stored frames later?

    Five snapshots of both hips, knees and ankles predict the two knee angles 15 frames later in a stored 60 Hz treadmill recording. On 40,201 forecasts from 12 held-out walkers, replaying a stride from four seconds of knee history gave 3.54° mean absolute error. The transformer, with 0.4 seconds of joint history, gave 4.46° on the same windows. Six of the 51 recordings used whole-file Xsens HD processing, including test recording S48. The other 45 carry the Live label. Physical prediction latency was never measured. Only reconstructed joint angles entered the forecast.

    Where will the next step land?

  • Shin & Jadhav · W-track recordings

    Can brain activity sharpen a rat’s movement forecast?

    Step through one recorded trip and compare how far a rat moved with forecasts that use its earlier movement alone or also use firing from two brain regions. Across three held-out rats the average miss fell from 2.898 to 2.870 cm when firing was added. Only one rat improved against both movement alone and firing from an earlier trip. For that rat, continuing at the last observed speed did better still.

Run two measured recordings with Python

Both run on your own computer, not on this site. The general model’s five-sensor walking fit opens a page in your browser that forecasts five sensors on a walking person, on a walk it was fitted on, so it shows the model running, not how well it would forecast someone new. One wrist trial shows how a recording is labelled, channel by channel, for the general model. No model is fitted to it.

Two papers, each written twice

Two working drafts, each in a Technical and a Simple English edition. Nobody outside the project has reviewed them. Every result in them comes from stored recordings, and nothing is live. They report every study of the numbered table, the general model’s first five fits among them. What came after them is not in them: the general model’s shared fit, the studies published after the numbered table, the walk/run generator, the knee forecasts and the rhythm comparison, the two five-finger studies, and the separate supplements on them.

How precisely do words identify a recorded movement? A separate four-page supplement, not one of the two papers, on the two studies that search recordings of movement with words, one of the whole body and one of the hand. Open the PDF.
