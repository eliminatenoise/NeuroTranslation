What changed across the study?

The frozen motion models had a two-axis error of 4.053°. Correcting their forecasts using earlier wrist motion brought the error to 3.957°. Adding the paired forearm signal brought it to 3.853°. Giving the same correction forearm activity from the next trial instead raised it to 4.140°.

Six people supplied the training movements. Three previously evaluated development participants supplied 960 trials. Each person contributes equally to these root mean square errors. The forecasts look 250 ms ahead on the stored clock. The instruments’ physical delay remains unmeasured.
