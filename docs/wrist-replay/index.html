Scalp EEG made the wrist forecast worse

A person sees a target and turns their wrist. Given half a second of movement, where will their wrist be a quarter of a second later? A small learned network made that forecast from wrist motion alone, and again with electrical activity measured at eight scalp electrodes (EEG).

Over both wrist axes and all six moments, it missed by 4.053° from motion alone and by 4.523° with EEG added. The error is root mean square (large misses count more), with each of the three development participants counting equally. Holding the last angle missed by 7.925°.
