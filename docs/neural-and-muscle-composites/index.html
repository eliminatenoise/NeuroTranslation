Which model best forecasts a reach and its cortical activity?

A macaque moved a handle. Electrodes counted spikes in somatosensory area 2 while the handle and a marker on the hand traced the reach. Can a model use the last 200 milliseconds to forecast where the hand will go and how many spikes will follow?

Three Transformers learned from the same reaches on September 7. Each target had its own fit. The autoregressive model draws the first interval, then the next. The diffusion model refines a noisy guess for both intervals together. The hybrid refines the first interval before drawing the next. Each sees four earlier blocks of movement and counts. Each gets 180 updates per seed and makes 16 draws per example. The fits use three seeds.

Ridge regression gave the closest handle forecast. Extending the last velocity gave the closest forecast for the three-dimensional hand marker. Repeating the last spike count beat every fitted architecture for predicting counts with either movement input. Each error averages the first and second 50 ms horizons. Movement error is the Euclidean distance in centimetres. The counts column gives mean squared error per electrode contact per 50 ms, in squared counts. The labelled contacts pool spikes across sorting labels. They identify recording sites across days. A contact can contain activity from more than one neuron.

Handle forecasts used 2, 8 and 16 sequential decoder calls for autoregression, diffusion and the hybrid. On a two-thread CPU these took 0.081, 0.266 and 0.550 ms per window with 16 draws, in batches of up to 32. The times cover model computation. The complete handle scoring process took 14.57 s including evaluation of both targets and all controls.

The 493 later windows come from 274 trials, two workspaces and two recording blocks on September 14 in the same animal. Trials carry equal weight. The investigators had used the later day before. The marker analysis was added after scoring the prespecified handle analysis. An animal, a new day and a new species would be different tests. Which signals should meet first?

A person stands or walks on a narrow beam while the visual scene tilts. Scalp electrodes record voltage. Electrodes on both lower legs pick up electrical activity from eight muscles. Cameras follow a marker over the sacrum. Every input and target comes from the same person, event and sampling clock.

Electrical pair. Combine three scalp-site histories with eight leg-muscle EMG histories.

Movement pair. Combine the measured sacrum path with four projections of where it would go if recent velocity held for 125 ms.

Join. The two branches each yield 16 learned features. Combine both outputs, their elementwise products, posture and cue direction. Predict the eight future RMS muscle voltages and 32 future marker displacements.

Two alternatives use the same parameter count and training schedule. Early mixing combines all four inputs before either branch. Crossed pairing groups scalp with measured position and leg EMG with projected movement. All three learn the same two targets from the same 18 development participants and are scored on six other, historically examined participants.

The proposed pairing gave the smallest average path error of the three groupings. Early mixing gave the smallest muscle-voltage error. The four prespecified comparisons required improvement on both outcomes against both alternatives. The paired design missed that threshold. Its movement projection assumes zero net acceleration and draws entirely on the measured path. It adds a way of representing the path, without an additional sensor.

Omitting the scalp voltages lowered both errors. While standing, simply continuing recent velocity gave 0.674 mm path error, ahead of the paired model’s 0.833 mm. During walking, the paired model gave 3.278 mm against 4.864 mm for continued velocity.

The six participants supplied 879 standing and 880 walking events, 1,759 in all. Average across training seeds, then give equal weight to cue direction, posture and participant. Muscle error is mean absolute error across the eight channel RMS amplitudes. Path error is mean 3D distance across 32 samples. Both cover 125 ms. Six reserve participants were untouched. What does simulated muscle geometry add?

The reaching archive contains another description of the arm. OpenSim estimates the lengths of 39 muscle–tendon paths from measured pose. Does supplying those lengths at the last observed instant help predict where the handle will be 100 ms later?

Use the same later measured handle displacement for each comparison. A ridge model reads recent body positions and 37 cortical contact histories. What was physically measured?

Surface EMG measures the electrical activity associated with muscle excitation. Contraction and shortening concern mechanical changes in muscle tissue. A camera measures marker position. OpenSim estimates a path length from geometry. An inertial projection extends measured velocity. Each answers a different question about the same movement. Neither experiment records individual muscle force. The balance recording supplies electrical activity and displacement as separate targets.

There is one force that motion can give directly in principle. For a body of known, constant mass, the net external force equals mass times the acceleration of its centre of mass. Estimating that acceleration requires a calibrated trajectory on a known time grid. A single hand marker tracks a different point. A segment model with known inertias and external loads can estimate net joint moments by inverse dynamics. Several muscles can supply the same moment, even pulling against one another. Individual tension needs additional constraints and its own test. OpenSim inverse dynamics

Area 2 receives information about the arm. A forecast of its later contact counts from earlier movement is compatible with sensory feedback and with other common causes. No afferent nerve or intervention was recorded here that would identify the path. A physically measured muscle-length or force target, alongside synchronized neural measurements, would test a fuller contraction model.

For the geometry study, 519 training windows came from the earlier day. The later blocks supplied 493 windows. The earlier-trial control is available for 485 of them.
