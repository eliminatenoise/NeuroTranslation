Walking force

What will the floor feel?

Two plates in a walkway measure how hard a walker presses down on them. Can a model predict their combined reading a tenth of a second ahead from the motion of the feet and pelvis, and the electrical activity of eight right-leg muscles?

What goes into each prediction?

Each point uses eight consecutive 30 ms bins. At the end of each bin the model can see six marker positions, three coordinates each, and eight right-leg muscle signals, each recorded by a pair of skin electrodes. The electrical input is the root mean square voltage over that bin, in microvolts (µV). A larger voltage can have many causes. Changing it here tests how a fitted equation responds to an invented input.

The reference always uses the original muscle pattern. A timing shift moves the edited pattern in milliseconds. A strength of 1× and a shift of 0 ms make the signals identical. Changing position or speed moves the markers in both versions together. The Transformer learns which earlier bins to weight for this task. Ridge fits a weighted sum of the same inputs. Each choice of signals has its own fitted model.

The horizontal axis is time in the invented example. This graph links separate predictions, one every 30 ms. Each prediction is plotted at its target time, 100 ms after the centre of the last recorded input block. The study files already included smoothing in both time directions, marker-gap reconstruction and whole-trial electrical processing. Timing and accuracy in a live system are unknown.

Sources and scope

Two plates in one walkway

The plates measure the combined vertical load where feet touch their surfaces. A foot can also land on the surrounding floor. A zero plate reading can occur while the floor still supports the walker. The experiment does not assign force to a particular leg. The electrodes recorded the right leg only.

Florent Moissenet and Céline Schreiber collected the original walking data. The models, predictions and figures here were made from their published data. Only averages from the measured assessment appear in the viewer. Every editable walk is invented.
