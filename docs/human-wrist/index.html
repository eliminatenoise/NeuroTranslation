Where will the wrists move?

A camera follows the wrist, elbow and shoulder on each arm. Electrodes on the surface of the brain record voltage. Give the model half a second of both histories. Where does it place each wrist eight and fifteen camera frames later?

Two clocks, one movement

The AJILE12 recording pairs eight cortical contacts sampled 500 times a second with arm positions estimated from 30 video frames a second. A movement label on the right wrist sets the cutoff. The model forecasts both wrists at that event, even if the left wrist stays still.

Five consecutive windows each contain 50 voltage samples and three camera frames. Each wrist has its own model of that arm. With voltage enabled, both models receive the same eight cortical traces. Eight and fifteen frames are counted from the final input frame. The movement label was assigned after recording, and the released signals underwent offline processing. This experiment measures offline prediction in the camera plane.

What the model learns

A simple guess extends the observed wrist velocity. The fitted prior scales that velocity using earlier training events. A temporal Transformer weighs the five windows against each other and can add a small correction to the prior. Switch to the direct forecast to see the model predict displacement on its own.

The built-in examples and weights come from generated motion and voltage. Their reference points come from the same simulator. They let you operate the model and inspect its arithmetic. The recording-day test below uses AJILE12.
