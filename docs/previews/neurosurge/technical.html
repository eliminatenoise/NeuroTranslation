Capture and command controls
  1. Press C to open the motion recorder. Choose Start camera to request video access. Under Source, you can also choose a local video or timed pictures. The images stay on your device.
  2. Choose Movement forecast in the game toolbar, or press F and enter “forecast a tracked point”. Set Body follows for the arms or knees and Point to forecast for the marker. Brackets identify the tracked selection and a prompt appears when tracking misses it. The ring shows its forecast position 120 ms after the last of five recent images. The stay-put prediction uses the last tracked position. Both predictions use the same later tracked position for scoring.
  3. Use Forecast method to select the browser model, the blend based on past tracker feedback, or a named movement. The movement options use rules fixed on an earlier sample.
  4. In the forecast view, choose Save take and Open Command box to stop capture. Enter replay my take. F opens the Command box for supported moves such as “bend my right elbow”. In a browser with on-device dictation, choose Use voice and confirm the transcript.
  5. In Named takes, use Save last take and Download to export a version 1 JSON take file with the name, timestamps, tracked movement values and tracking gaps. In another tab, press F and use Open a take file under Named takes. Resolve a matching name with Keep existing take or Replace take. Start playback with replay take named warm up, using the saved name.
  6. Join two to four named replays with then, as in replay take named warm up then replay first 1 second of take named reach. A comma before then marks the end of an ambiguous name. The game checks every step before playback. Each take keeps its original sample clock. A requested interval includes its start and excludes its end. Between takes the game releases the previous movement controls for a render frame. Stop replay or Esc cancels the remaining steps.

Earlier camera forecast test on 11 people

Open the 86-person camera forecast study.

Supplying the action gave 13.42 px mean endpoint error. The camera-only blend gave 13.55 px and NeuroTranslate gave 14.60 px. The confirmation sample includes 11 reserved participants and 44 activity recordings with two camera views each, for 88 views. Means give equal weight to each view of an action, each action per person, and each person.

All methods forecast at the same times with a 120 ms lead. Each uses the last point, the last two points or up to five recent readings. Endpoint error is Euclidean distance to the interpolated Vicon joint position projected into the rescaled image. Displacement error compares forecast movement from the detector's issue point with measured movement from the optical issue point.

The action map was fixed using eight earlier participants. It uses two-point velocity for walking, a penalized curve for squat and shoulder press, and persistence for jumping jacks. Each camera-only estimator starts with persistence until feedback is available. It compares prior predictions against their resolved tracker points, divides the errors by body scale, and averages with a one-second half-life. A resolved point lies within 40 ms of the earlier target. The selected estimator takes the least historical loss. The blended estimator applies exponential weights with a scale of max(0.01, hold loss / 4). The preview exposes the blend.

Both named and blended forecasts beat holding position on mean endpoint error for all 11 people. Mean paired differences were −0.653 px and −0.527 px. The 95% percentile intervals were −0.840 to −0.484 px and −0.682 to −0.367 px, based on 10,000 participant resamples.

Of 14,802 scheduled slots, 11,305 supplied an image. The matched sample includes 31,523 of 64,815 predictions, with 29,394 after camera feedback and 2,129 at startup or reset. Missing adjacent reference rows explain 33,200 exclusions and missing optical points 92. The 60 Hz grid uses alignment after capture. Physical exposure times remain unverified. On the pooled 1,709 common forecasts from 66 views, named movement and the blend beat NeuroTranslate at each of the five shifts from −2 to 2 camera slots (about ±33 ms). The live comparison uses its own tracker’s later points.

The preview requests a CPU capture canvas. Default and Vulkan graphics in Chromium 155.0.8059.84 on Linux reproduced the archived tracker coordinates for all 1,003 tested images across eight clips, covering four movements and two views of one participant.
