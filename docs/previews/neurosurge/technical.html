Movement preview

NeuroSurge plays the same simulation in Full and Lite. Both run the released NeuroTranslate 0.0 movement forecast in the browser. Lite transfers smaller anatomy files.

Use a desktop browser with WebGL, a keyboard and a mouse or trackpad. Open the standalone Movement mirror to run the forecast on its own.

Capture and command controls
  1. Press C. Choose Start camera to request video, or choose a local video or timestamped stills. Images stay on the device.
  2. Set Body follows for the arm or knee pose channels and Point to forecast for the marker. The ring shows its forecast position 120 ms after the last of five usable frames.
  3. Choose Save take and Open Command box to stop capture. Enter replay my take. F opens the Command box for supported moves such as “bend my right elbow”. In a browser with on-device dictation, choose Use voice and confirm the transcript.

Forecast accuracy on recorded movement

Holding the latest position had the lowest mean endpoint and displacement errors. Eight previously unused participants each did a walk, squat, shoulder press and jumping jacks. The C1 and C3 views of each trial show the same event. Average each view separately, then both views per action, the four actions per participant and the eight participants.

The exact released still-image detector feeds five-row windows. The analytical forecasts use the selected joint's history. With lead h = 120 ms and issue time t, the two new fits fix the intercept at the last position and weight earlier times tᵢ by 2(tᵢ − t)/h. The curve adds a squared time term and penalizes its coefficient by that column's weighted sum of squares. The attention model also uses summaries of the other pose points. We froze all five predictions before reading the optical coordinates.

Endpoint error is Euclidean distance to the projected Vicon position interpolated at the target. Displacement error compares the change from the tracker’s issue position with the change from the Vicon issue position. Lowest mean endpoint errors by action were constant velocity for walking (12.61 px), the curve for squat (7.98 px) and shoulder press (7.68 px), and holding position for jumping jacks (30.18 px).

Holding reduced mean endpoint error by 0.411 px against constant velocity and had lower error for five participants. Its paired 95% percentile interval for the difference was −0.794 to +0.0008 px based on 10,000 resamples of participants. The interval includes zero. The comparison uses the same qualifying forecasts for all methods.

The run decoded 8,068 images for 10,718 time slots and scored 22,266 of 45,117 forecasts across the 64 views. Missing adjacent reference rows explain 22,652 exclusions and missing optical points the remaining 199. The publisher aligned RGB and Vicon after capture. The 60 Hz grid includes drop indices and physical exposure times remain unverified. On 1,457 common forecasts in 50 views, holding led in pooled endpoint error across shifts of −2, −1, 0, 1 and 2 slots (about ±33 ms). All eight participants contributed and one had all eight view and action combinations in this subset. The live comparison uses its own tracker’s later positions.
