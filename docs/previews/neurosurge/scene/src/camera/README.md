# Camera tracking for arms and legs

In the connected Movement mirror, select **Save take** to keep recent tracked movement from the active camera, a video, or timed pictures. Open the Command box and enter **replay my take**. The standalone recorder below offers its own capture and replay controls.

The standalone recorder also accepts saved footage. Open it with **C**, set **Source** to **Recorded video**, choose limbs and a local file, and select **Play video**. The recorder uses presentation times for pose updates and releases tracking on pause or seek. **Save video take** keeps up to eight seconds of recent movement on that same clock. Seek, limb and file changes clear the unsaved window. The latest saved take and named takes use the same F controls for both sources. Follow the [Simple player guide](../../../PLAYER-GUIDE.html#follow-a-video-file) or [Technical player guide](../../../PLAYER-GUIDE-TECHNICAL.html#follow-recorded-video).

**Timed pictures** reads local images at user-assigned times. Track them in order, then save a picture take to replay from F. Positions between two tracked poses are calculated only during replay. Saved sample times and positions still come from the individual images. Follow the [picture take instructions](../../../PLAYER-GUIDE.html#make-a-take-from-timed-pictures).

Press **C** or select **Follow movement** in the game toolbar. In the local model version, **C** opens the Movement mirror. Choose **Record a limb take** from the toolbar to open this recorder. Under **Follow**, choose your own left or right side before or during capture. The arrow keys can move through multiple choices while **Follow** has focus. Select **Start camera** to request video. The preview shows a mirror image. If the device already flips its outgoing video, disable that setting so the tracker receives the original view. If the mouse is captured, the usual limb keys also switch the camera. **1** selects your left arm, **2** your left knee, **3** your right knee and **4** your right arm.

**Following** appears with a green line for each limb that meets the tracking checks. **Show a bend** means a knee is visible and still needs its starting bend. **Bring into view** means the tracker could not use that limb in the current image. Each status describes a single selected limb.

| Choose | What to show the lens | What moves on the avatar |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Right arm | Face the lens. Keep both shoulders, your right elbow and wrist in sight. Lift the arm out to the side and bend it across the picture. | The shoulder lifts sideways and the elbow bends. |
| Left arm | Face the lens and repeat with your left arm. | The hand reaches in the direction of your wrist from your shoulder. The game bends the elbow to fit that reach. |
| Both arms | Show both shoulders, elbows and wrists. | Each visible arm follows its own movement. |
| Left or right knee | Keep your shoulders, hips, selected knee and ankle in sight. Turn partway sideways so the thigh and shin make a visible angle. Bend once to start, then straighten and bend again. | The selected knee bends and straightens within its game limits. You can leave the other leg in place. |
| Left hip lift or Right hip lift | Face sideways with the shoulders, hips, face and selected knee in frame. | A signed image-plane angle drives forward and backward motion at the selected hip. |
| Left leg from the side or Right leg from the side | Turn the selected leg towards the camera. Keep the face, hips, knee, heel and forefoot in frame. Hold a clear knee bend briefly. | The hip, knee and ankle have separate tracking checks. |
| Both knees | Turn partway sideways. Keep both feet in view and bend each knee in turn. | Each knee starts following once the camera sees it bend. |
| Both arms and knees | Step back until the lens can see your wrists and feet. Keep your shoulders toward the lens and bend each knee where the bend shows in the picture. | Each visible arm and knee drives its own movement in the same frame. The panel names the limbs it can follow. |

The camera estimates points in a flat picture. A knee bent towards the lens can look straight. An ankle hidden behind the other leg can disappear. If the tracker loses the limb, updates stop and the ordinary controls remain available. A brief missed frame releases control immediately. After a longer gap, show a clear knee bend again. Use **Stop camera** in the recorder. In the Movement mirror, choose **Stop** to release video or **Close** or **Escape** to return to the game. Closing or hiding the game also releases the video track.

The angle between the thigh and shin drives a simulated knee. Before updating it, the tracker asks for two successive views with at least 45 degrees of *visible* bend. It drops reversed hip order, short or foreshortened segments, and points whose visibility is low. The tracker also checks the knee and ankle for visible detail. A dark cover across the lower picture can hide the ankle even when the model supplies a confident estimate. These checks depend on the image projection. The arm controls use the shoulder line to allow for a camera tilted slightly to one side. The left arm uses the game's own arm lengths and reach limits.

Hip lift uses the shoulder-to-hip and hip-to-knee directions in the picture. Face projection establishes the forward direction. Torso breadth and the image detail along the thigh gate updates. Each accepted frame sends one hip angle within −30° to 120°. Video, timed pictures and saved takes carry the same selected joint. The angle is a target for the simulation derived from a single camera view. [Technical hip tracking instructions](../../../PLAYER-GUIDE-TECHNICAL.html#hip-camera)

For **Both arms**, keep both shoulders, elbows and wrists in the picture. Try raising one hand and lowering the other. When the tracker loses one arm, it releases that arm. The other can still move. For **Both knees**, the tracker finds the legs afresh in each picture and checks for visible detail at each foot. When one foot goes out of sight, the other knee can keep moving. If the two shins line up in the picture, the game can lose both. Turn slightly so the camera can tell them apart and show each bend again. Holding a movement key gives you manual control of the limb selected in the game. Release it to follow the camera again.

**Record take** becomes available when a selected limb starts tracking. Choose a single limb, a pair, or **Both arms and knees**. Each take lasts up to eight seconds. **Finish take** keeps it and switches off the camera. **Stop camera**, a limb change or leaving play cancels an unfinished take. A successful new take becomes the latest take. A named copy of the earlier movement stays available. **Replay take** works with video off and sends the saved poses to the original selection. A single limb or pair needs two visible frames per selected limb. **Both arms and knees** keeps a take once any one limb has two visible frames. The four counts tell you what the lens picked up. A zero means that limb contributed no movement. During replay, a side that went out of view releases its controls until it reappears. Other visible limbs can keep moving. **Dismiss** clears the unnamed take.

From **F**, type **replay 0.5 to 1.5 seconds of take named warm up** to watch that one-second passage, or **replay last 1 second of take named warm up** to see the finish. Each side waits for its first visible sample within the passage. A frame at the exact end belongs to the next passage. If neither side was visible in the requested frames, F stays open so you can choose another range. The original take remains in the list.

To compare two named recordings, press **F** and enter **compare take named warm up with take named second try**, or choose both names under **Named takes** and press **Compare takes**. Under **Joint**, choose an angle present on the same side in both recordings. Elbows and knees show the avatar’s bend target, with 0° straight. The right shoulder shows the stored sideways lift target. Left upper-arm lift comes from the saved shoulder-to-elbow direction in the plane of the picture. On each side, 0° points down, 90° lies along the shoulder line, and 180° points overhead. The left angle gives the size of the lift and cannot distinguish an inward lift from an outward one of the same size. Each column gives its own sample times in seconds since **Record take**, rounded down to the millisecond. Half-second rows group the readings without shifting either movement to match. A timestamp beside **No reading** marks a saved frame with no angle for this side. **Tracking expired** marks a tracking timeout, **No saved sample** an empty time window, and **Take ended** the end of the shorter recording. Angles are rounded to a tenth of a degree. The range uses the lowest and highest visible readings on the selected channel. Its denominator counts saved camera-frame entries in that take, including the first stored loss of tracking. The recorder can skip consecutive missing frames. A timeout has its own timestamp and adds no camera frame. Motion through a gap remains unknown.

**Change per second** gives the smallest and largest signed interval rates in degrees per second. For each adjacent pair, the game divides the difference in the two saved angles by the time between the frame grabs. A positive rate means more bend or lift. Open **Show intervals** for the start, end and rate of each pair. The count and covered seconds include only pairs 50–250 ms apart, endpoints included. These limits apply to this preview. They omit very short intervals that amplify timing noise, and long gaps that can hide another movement. A missing angle, invalid time, or tracking timeout breaks a run. Duplicate or reversed capture times make rates unavailable for that take. Intervals can cross a half-second display row. Camera perspective, foreshortening, tracker jitter and any limits on avatar targets affect the estimates. Clinical angular velocity requires separate calibrated measurements. Recordings stay in the tab and comparing them leaves the avatar still.

A take contains the browser timestamps of the video frame grabs and the selected avatar angles in degrees. For the left arm it also keeps the two image-plane directions used to draw the reach. The source is a 2D picture. Clinical joint-angle measurements and optical-marker positions in millimetres require separate calibrated measurements. For an arm, the take also keeps each visible wrist’s horizontal and vertical offset from its own shoulder, in image heights. **F → Match my arm reach** compares that path with eight measured macaque hand trajectories and can replay the selected animal trial. **F → Forecast my wrists** can use the earlier projected arm positions in a fixed shoulder frame. Its ridge coefficients come from generated motions. The camera yields no depth or neural measurement. Frames and audio are never saved. [Study data and comparison rule](reach-study/SOURCES.md)

With a finished take in this tab, press **F**, type **replay my take** and press Enter. To check the recorded selection, enter **replay my left arm take** or **replay my take of both arms**. For **Both arms and knees**, you can say **replay my whole-body take**. The command replays the arm and knee channels the camera saw. Fingers and spine have no channels in the take. Only a matching take will play. This uses the same replay clock and avatar controls as **Replay take**. On browsers with English on-device dictation, **Use voice** fills the field. Check the words and choose **Move** to start. Opening F stops a live camera or replay and cancels an unfinished recording. To bookmark a finished take, open **Named takes** in F, enter a name and choose **Save last take**. The list shows each name alongside the recorded limb selection and duration. Type **replay take named warm up** to replay a take saved as **Warm up**, even after recording a different one. The command uses the whole name, ignoring capitalization and extra spaces. If the name is missing or ambiguous, F stays open. A collision asks you to confirm **Replace take** or choose another name. To delete one, enter its name, choose **Forget by name** and confirm **Forget take**. Named takes survive dismissing the camera panel. Closing or reloading the page clears them all.

Frames pass one at a time to a worker in the browser. The worker returns the selected avatar movements. During an arm take it also returns both tracked shoulders and the selected visible elbow and wrist positions in the unmirrored image. Each accepted arm needs a visible shoulder line, elbow and wrist, and visible detail along the forearm. Nothing records or uploads the video. The model, JavaScript and WebAssembly are bundled locally and load after opt-in. Use the localhost launcher or a secure HTTPS origin that permits camera access. The tracker needs video capture, Web Workers, OffscreenCanvas, ImageBitmap, WebAssembly and WebGL.

For forward shoulder motion, select **Left arm from the side** or **Right arm from the side** in the recorder. The side-view input uses the visible arm, the shoulder-to-hip direction and which way the face points. The selected pose is an image-plane estimate. Save it from live capture, local video or timed pictures and replay it through **F**. The file stores the selected side and pose times. Use a front-view arm choice for wrist-path forecasts and the reach study.

## Side-view leg tracking

In **C**, select **Left leg from the side** or **Right leg from the side**. Turn the selected leg towards the camera and keep the face, hips, knee, heel and forefoot visible. Start camera capture. The projection uses the nose and ears to find forward and the shoulder and hip width to require a side view. It measures thigh relative to torso, shin relative to thigh, and heel-to-forefoot relative to shin. The target ranges are −30° to 120° at the hip, −135° to 0° at the knee, and −50° to 20° at the ankle. Zero ankle target corresponds to a 90° angle between shin and foot.

Image detail and landmark checks gate each target. The knee starts after two consecutive accepted bends of at least 45°. The hip and ankle can follow independently of that starting check. Read **Tracking by limb** for each joint. A missing foot can release the ankle while the hip stays visible. The game view stays under player control.

Before opening the recorder, select the game leg with **2** or **3** if you plan to use manual movement keys. While **W**, **A**, **S** or **D** is held, it takes precedence for that leg. Capture resumes joint control on the next accepted frame after release.

Local video and timed pictures use the same projection. **Finish take**, **Save video take** and **Save picture take** require two samples of at least one common joint for a side-leg take. The take stores accepted angles and sample times. The source images stay on the device. Use **F** and **replay my left leg take** or **replay my right leg take** to replay the last saved take when its side matches. Playback keeps capture off. Use **Named takes** to retain earlier recordings in the tab.

In six SUMediPose walking recordings set aside for evaluation, the near-left side view supplied 630 eligible images. Before the game’s two-picture knee readiness check, the per-image tracker returned all three targets on 150. On the 203 matched images with an ankle target, mean absolute error was 7.04° against independent optical markers projected into the image and limited to the game’s target range, compared with 15.69° for a constant 0° ankle target. These averages weight each person equally. The forefoot reference marker was over the second metatarsal head, at the ball of the foot. Right-side testing used recorded footage to check game response. [Walking recordings and marker setup](https://doi.org/10.7910/DVN/GRRROM)

## Verification

Open the Full or Lite link from the game menu, or use the desktop launcher included with Lite. Press **C**, choose a **Follow** option, and start the camera. Check the joint names under **Tracking by limb** as you move. Stop capture or finish a take, then use **F** to replay with the camera off. You can check the same controls with a local video or timed pictures.

The side-leg release was checked in Linux Chromium using recorded footage as a local camera stream. Both editions followed the selected side, dropped tracking from a hidden foot, and left the view steady. The checks also saved and replayed takes from the camera, a local video and timed pictures. Each source used the bundled worker. Camera access and device compatibility depend on the browser and computer.

The worker checks one frame at a time on the CPU. Each image is at most 640 pixels along its longer side. With video-frame callbacks available, the recorder waits for a newly displayed image and leaves at least 50 ms between grabs. The saved timestamp is the browser time when the recorder grabs the image. It is separate from the video's playback clock. [Video-frame callbacks](https://wicg.github.io/video-rvfc/)

The polling fallback waits 90 ms after a result or unsuccessful check and uses the displayed-frame count when available. With either frame API, a gap in video frames releases tracking and clears the green lines after 1.2 seconds. The recorder shows **Camera on. Waiting for video.** and shuts off the camera after about 15 seconds without a readable frame. A stationary limb can keep tracking while fresh frames arrive. Clock-only browsers may keep accepting a held image as the playback clock advances. The recorder can be restarted with **Stop camera** and **Start camera**. Chromium and MediaPipe control CPU threads, image transfers and instruction selection. These checks used virtual video on Linux Chromium. Physical devices and other browsers need their own checks. [Video playback quality counters](https://w3c.github.io/media-playback-quality/)
