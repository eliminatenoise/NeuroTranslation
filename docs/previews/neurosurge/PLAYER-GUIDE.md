# NeuroSurge player guide (simple)

[Open Full online](https://eliminatenoise.github.io/NeuroTranslation/previews/neurosurge/scene/example/preview.html) · [Play Lite](https://eliminatenoise.github.io/NeuroTranslation/previews/neurosurge/scene/example/preview.html?edition=lite) · [Technical guide](PLAYER-GUIDE-TECHNICAL.md)

## Move a body part from the brain

1. Open either edition in a desktop browser with a keyboard and mouse or trackpad. Press and hold on an empty part of the picture, then drag to look around. Let go to stop turning.
2. Look at a coloured piece of the brain. The coloured pieces show the **motor strip**, a region of the brain that helps control movement. A label near the middle of the screen names the part the game will move. For example, a shoulder area moves the shoulder. Click on the area to repeat the move. Press **Left Arrow** or **Right Arrow** to aim at the next area in that direction.
3. Watch your body and the mirror body. Both make the same movement. The mirror body gives you another view of it. Press **E** to change between the eye view and the outside view. In the eye view, moving the mouse turns the camera from one fixed place. From outside, moving the mouse takes the camera around the body at a steady distance. The game leaves the view where you set it until you move it again.
4. Press **Escape** to open the controls. Open **View tutorial** to follow the steps. The tutorial can mark a visible motor area and the part of the body it controls. You can resume or restart the tutorial in the same menu.

The **Resume** button keeps the pointer free. To turn the view with every mouse movement, choose **Capture mouse** in the controls. Press **Escape** to release it and open the controls again. If capture is unavailable, drag on an empty part of the picture to keep playing.

If you turn far enough that the brain and both bodies leave the picture, a message says you have turned away. Drag the empty space back toward the brain, press an arrow key to aim at a motor area, or press Escape and choose Reset brain view. While the brain is out of view, Reset brain view returns the camera to its starting direction. The tutorial and information cards receive your clicks, so you can read them without pressing a motor area behind them. When the tutorial shows a Brain ring, aim at it and press Space to pick up the brain.

A game link can still open when the app that shared it adds tracking information to the address. If the game shows an address error, select **Start the game** to open the usual address.

Drag on an empty part of the picture to turn your view. Finish the drag before aiming at a coloured area to start a movement. When the tutorial asks you to drag a limb, start at the centre of the ring and move the pointer while holding the button. If you hid the information cards, press R to show them again. If the ring stays hidden, turn the view until the limb is clear. If you turn away from the whole scene, the game shows how to look back, even with the information cards hidden.

## Move one limb yourself

During play, press **1** for the left arm, **2** for the left leg, **3** for the right leg or **4** for the right arm. Use **W**, **A**, **S** and **D** to move the selected limb. Release the key when it reaches the position you want.

While looking through the character’s eyes, look a little up or down to raise or lower the selected arm at the shoulder or the selected leg at the hip. You can do this without looking straight up or at the floor. After you select a limb, the aiming mark for this control sits about a quarter of the way down from the top of the screen. The mark stays there. The drawn wrist or ankle follows the joint, so it can move away from the mark when the limb reaches the end of its movement or the camera looks elsewhere.

Step 4 lets you practise this movement. Drag up or down on an empty part of the picture. If you chose **Capture mouse**, move the mouse up or down. Watch the hand or foot on the limb you chose. The tutorial advances when the wrist or ankle moves at least 2.5 cm compared with its position beside the shoulder or hip just before you looked. The game gives it up to 0.35 seconds of game time to move. If you are looking from outside the body, press **E** to return to the eye view first.

Press and drag on a limb to move the joint you pressed on. Press **Space** to grip. Aim at the brain or mirror body and hold Space to carry it, then release to put it down. You can drag the brain or mirror body to turn it. Hold **Shift** on the initial press to reach the item behind the one you would usually select.

## Give the body a movement to make

If you use **Tab** to reach a button at the top of the game, you can press **F** to open the Command box or **C** to open the motion recorder from there. Press **Escape** to close either panel and return to the game view.

1. While playing, press **F** to open the **Command box**. Type a direction such as **bend my right elbow**, then press **Move** or **Enter**.
2. Join up to four supported movements with **then**. For example, enter **bend my left knee then bend my right knee**. The game shows which step it is doing. A movement key or a new command gives you control back.
3. Where the browser offers on-device dictation, choose **Use voice**. Check the words that appear in the box before you move the body. You can always type instead.

## Follow a movement or save a take

1. Press **C** to open the motion recorder in Full or Lite. The camera starts after you choose **Start camera** and allow access. For a point forecast, choose **Movement forecast** in the game toolbar. You can also press **F** and enter “forecast a tracked point”.
2. In the recorder, use **Follow** to choose an arm, a knee, a hip, a leg from the side or a group. Choose your own left or right. You can make this choice before or during capture. If you are recording, choose **Finish take** before switching limbs to keep the take. Use the arrow keys while **Follow** has focus. Press **Tab** to reach **Start camera** when you are ready.
3. Select **Start camera** and allow video if the browser asks. Video stays on your device. The recorder shows your own image as a mirror. **Following** and a green line show which limbs the recorder can use to move the virtual body. An arm line joins the estimated shoulder, elbow and wrist. A leg line joins the estimated hip, knee and ankle. If you see **Show a bend**, turn partway sideways and bend that knee so the camera can see the angle. If you see **Bring into view**, make room for that arm or leg in the picture and check that its joints are visible.

   In the Movement mirror, step back so your feet fit in the picture. When it asks for a clear bend, turn partway sideways and hold that knee bent briefly. Both live camera views start following a knee when two pictures in a row show a bend of at least 45 degrees. That is half a right angle in the picture. If the camera loses a knee for six tenths of a second, show its bend again when it returns. Visible arms can keep moving while a knee waits. The Movement mirror can still forecast where a visible point will move, using the places and times it saw that point in the pictures.

4. Save any movement you want to keep before stopping capture. In the recorder, choose **Record take** and **Finish take**. In the Movement mirror, choose **Save take**. Open the Command box and enter **replay my take** to move the simulated body through the saved poses.
5. Choose **Stop** in the Movement mirror or **Stop camera** in the recorder when you finish. Use **Close** or **Escape** to return from the Movement mirror. Your own mouse still controls the view of the game while the body follows the camera.

For a forward arm lift, choose **Left arm from the side** or **Right arm from the side** under **Follow**. Turn sideways with that arm nearer the lens. Keep your face, hips and the whole arm in view. As you raise the arm in front, lower it or bend your elbow, the virtual arm follows the movement it can see. If your other arm or clothing hides the elbow or wrist, turn until the recorder can see both again. For sideways lifts, face the camera and choose **Left arm** or **Right arm**.

The side-view choices also work with **Recorded video** and **Timed pictures**. Save the result as a take before changing **Follow**. Press **F** and enter **replay my take**, or name the side, as in **replay my left arm take**. You can compare the forward shoulder lift and elbow bend with another side-view take for the same arm. The chart reads 0° when the arm points down. The right chart shows the angle sent to the game, from 60° back to 180° overhead. The left chart shows the angle in the picture, with positive values towards the front. To calculate speed, it uses the shorter turn if the arm passes overhead between pictures. For a wrist-path comparison in the reach study, record an arm while facing the camera.

In the game toolbar, choose **Movement forecast**. Under **Body follows**, choose the part you want to move. The default is both arms and knees. You can choose one arm or knee, both arms, both knees, a forward hip lift, an arm from the side, or a leg from the side. The whole-leg choice follows the hip, knee and ankle. For a side view, turn your body sideways with the selected limb nearer the camera. Keep the points listed in the instructions in view. For the knee, hold a bend of at least 45° for two pictures in a row. The other joints can start moving while the knee waits.

Use **Point to forecast** for a separate choice. For example, the body can follow your left leg while the model forecasts where your right shoulder will appear 120 ms later. Both use the same pictures. Keep the part you want to forecast in the picture as well as the limb you want to move.

Choose **Controls** if the Movement mirror is compact. Change **Body follows** to switch parts or choose **Forecast only** to use the game controls. The forecast keeps its recent pictures when you change what moves. Newly selected joints wait for a usable picture. Changing **Point to forecast** starts a fresh forecast for that point and lets the selected body parts keep following.

The same choices work with **Recorded video** and **Timestamped stills**. Pausing, seeking or finishing a video releases the tracked joints. Timed pictures release them when the sequence ends. The last comparison stays on screen if one is available. If the leg is hard to read, the forecast can still run when its point is visible. A whole-leg take needs two accepted readings from the same joint before you can save it. Choose **Save take** to keep the movement you can see. Then open the Command box to replay it with the camera off.

Choose **Save take** once the mirror has tracked the limb you want. It keeps up to eight seconds of recent movement from the camera, video, or timed pictures. For a video you can pause and save movement from just before the current position. For timed pictures, wait until the mirror has read the images. Replay keeps the timing you supplied. The take keeps the estimated joint movements, their times and available arm marker positions in this tab. The camera image or file stays on your device.

Choose **Open Command box** after saving, or return to the game and press **F**. The camera stops when the Command box opens. Type **replay my take** to play back the movement, or use **Named takes** to give it a name. Save before stopping the mirror. Changing the limbs, moving to a new time in a clip, or choosing a different source clears the movement waiting to be saved. A saved take stays available while you use the same tab. Changing the forecast point keeps the movement you can save.

The Movement mirror uses the five most recent pictures to forecast where a point will be 0.12 seconds later. “Forecast from frame 5” means the fifth picture was the last one used for that prediction. The score uses the point found in the image closest to the target time, up to 0.04 seconds either side. It measures the difference in pixels, the small units that make up the picture. The second score uses the same later point and guesses that the tracked point stays at its most recent position.

The score says when the tracker finished processing the matching image before the model replied. It still uses the same match. If the tracker finds no matching point, the score says it is unavailable. A virtual movement is scored against a simulated point. Switching points, seeking a clip, or losing track starts a fresh comparison.

The recorder checks one picture at a time. When the browser reports new pictures, the game waits at least one twentieth of a second between grabs. On older browsers it waits about nine hundredths of a second after each check before looking again.

When the browser can tell the game that new pictures have stopped arriving, the green lines disappear after about 1.2 seconds. The recorder says **Camera on. Waiting for video.** You can still move the virtual body with the game controls. The recorder can follow you again when new pictures arrive. It turns off the camera after roughly 15 seconds without a new picture. Select **Start camera** to try again. A motionless arm can keep tracking as long as the camera keeps sending pictures. Some older browsers only report how long the video has played. If their camera picture freezes and the lines stay on, select **Stop camera**, then **Start camera**.

The browser uses the saved NeuroTranslate 0.0 model for these point forecasts. The model learned from video tracks of animals. [The recorded-movement test](index.html#camera-test) compares the model with two simple forecasts using pictures of people. You can also press **F** and type **raise my right hand above my shoulder**. The game moves the virtual arm, reports where it finished and shows a separate forecast. The **Virtual brain** section uses invented spike histories to show how the model ranks the next possible spike source. The separate fingertip touch study uses the [local model launcher](START.html#local-model). [Forecast sources and reuse](scene/src/joint/model/NOTICE.html)

### Follow a whole leg from the side

Press **C** to open the motion recorder in Full or Lite. Under **Follow**, choose **Left leg from the side** or **Right leg from the side**. Choose your own left or right. Turn sideways with that leg nearer the camera. Step back until your face, hips, knee, heel and toes fit in the picture.

Choose **Start camera** and allow video. Bend the knee you want to move and hold it briefly. The recorder needs to see a bend of at least 45°, half a right angle, in two pictures in a row before it follows the knee. The hip and ankle can start moving while the knee waits.

Lift or lower your thigh to move the hip. Bend or straighten your knee. Tip your foot up or down to move the ankle. Watch **Tracking by limb** in the recorder. **Following** tells you that the game can use that joint. **Bring into view** tells you that it needs a clearer picture. If your foot is hidden but your hip is clear, the game can keep following the hip. Your view of the game stays where you set it.

If you want to use the keys during capture, press **2** for the left leg or **3** for the right before opening the recorder. Hold **W**, **A**, **S** or **D** to move that leg yourself. Release the key so the next readable picture can move it again.

To keep the movement, choose **Record take**, then **Finish take**. From **Recorded video** or **Timed pictures**, choose **Save video take** or **Save picture take**. Two tracked pictures of the same joint are enough to save a whole-leg take. The take keeps the movements the tracker could read and the times it read them. Your pictures stay on the device. Press **F** and type **replay my left leg take** or **replay my right leg take** to repeat the last saved take if it matches that side. The camera stays off. To keep an earlier movement, save it under **Named takes** before you record another.

The game keeps each estimate within the virtual joint’s range. The hip target reaches 30° back or 120° forward. The knee can bend up to 135° from straight. The ankle target ranges from 50° with the foot pointing down to 20° with it tipping up, measured from the position where the foot and shin make a right angle.

The recorder estimates how the leg bends in the picture. We checked 630 individual side-view pictures of six people walking. This check estimated angles one picture at a time, before the game’s two-picture check to start following the knee. The tracker found all three angles in 150 pictures and an ankle angle in 203. In those 203 pictures, the average ankle error was 7.04° with the tracker and 15.69° with the ankle held at 0°, where the foot and shin form a right angle. Each person counted equally. The comparison used markers measured by separate equipment and placed over the same picture. We kept the reference angles within the same limits as the game. The foot marker was at the ball of the foot behind the second toe. These measurements cover the near left leg. We checked that the right leg moved in Full and Lite using other recordings. [Walking recordings and marker setup](https://doi.org/10.7910/DVN/GRRROM)

## Follow a video file

1. Press **C** and select **Recorded video** under **Source**. Under **Follow**, select the arm, knee, hip or side-view leg you want the virtual body to follow. You can also select both arms, both knees, or both pairs.
2. Choose a file under **Local video**, then select **Play video**. Use a file up to about 537 MB. Your browser must be able to play it and tell the game when each new picture appears. The file stays on this device. The game shows the picture as it was saved, so a mirrored recording can reverse left and right.
3. **Following** and a green line show which limbs the game can use. **Waiting for a bend** means the game can see the knee and needs a clearer starting bend. **No usable pose** means the joints in that picture could not be used. Try a part of the video where you can see more of the limb.
4. Pause or move to another time with the video controls. The previous tracking clears. The game also clears a result that falls 1.2 seconds or more behind playback. Choose **Stop video** to stop early or press **F** to stop and open the Command box. You can play the selected file again after it ends or you choose Stop video.
5. Choose **Save video take** while the video plays, after pausing, after stopping or at the end. It keeps tracked movement from up to eight seconds before the current position in the video. The timing follows the source even if you played it slowly. Gaps in tracking still take up time.
6. Press **F** and enter **replay my take**. To keep the take under a name, open **Named takes**, type a name and choose **Save last take**. For a take saved as **side reach**, enter **replay take named side reach**. Named takes and the latest take stay in this tab.

When you seek, change limbs or open a new file, the game starts a fresh window for the next take. Your earlier take stays until you choose **Save video take** again or finish a live take. The game keeps the tracked joint movement in memory. The picture and sound remain in the original file.

To save one limb, it needs to be tracked in at least two pictures. For **Both arms** or **Both knees**, each side needs two. For **Both arms and knees**, one limb appearing in two tracked pictures is enough. The take can include the other limbs when the tracker sees them.

You can also choose **Movement forecast** and use **Save take** in the Movement mirror while following a saved video or after reading timed pictures.

The tracker estimates joints in the picture. The virtual body moves from those estimates. The game simulates its electrical activity.

## Make a take from timed pictures

1. Press **C** and choose **Timed pictures** under **Source**. Choose the limb or pair you want the body to follow.
2. Add two to 256 local pictures. Use pictures up to 32 MiB each. Enter one time in seconds per picture in the displayed order. Use zero or positive times. For example, `0, 0.5, 1.25` gives a half-second first step and a longer second step. You can leave the times field blank and use filenames such as `0.jpg`, `0.5.jpg` and `1.25.jpg`. Use different times for each picture and keep the first and last within eight seconds of each other.
3. Choose **Track pictures** and wait for the result. A take needs two tracked pictures for a single limb, two per side for a pair, or two from any one limb in **Both arms and knees**. For knees, start with two pictures that show a clear bend of at least 45 degrees and add another clear view so there are enough poses to save. Use **Save picture take** once it appears.
4. Press **F** and type **replay my take**. Save a name under **Named takes** to call the movement back later in this tab. The virtual body moves between the poses from the pictures. If a timed picture loses a limb, the game releases that limb at that time. Comparisons and forecasts use the tracked pictures at their assigned times.

Changing the pictures, times or selected limb starts a fresh attempt. An earlier saved take stays available until you save another. The pictures stay on your device. The take keeps joint estimates and their times in the browser tab.

## See what moved

Press **R** to show more information about the area you are looking at. Press it again to hide the cards, then once more to return to the short cards.

In the normal view, faint lines of light travel across the brain and through the motor strip. Press **M** for the detailed electrical map and again to return. In the Escape menu, untick **Electrical effects in the normal view** if you want a quieter picture. If you want to return the body or brain to its starting place, use the matching reset button in that menu.

The game generates the brain and muscle activity shown in colour. With camera permission, it estimates where visible joints are in the picture. Those points can move the virtual body. The electrical activity still comes from the simulation.

## Choose a download

Full and Lite have the same controls, body shapes and brain. Lite uses smaller files to deliver the same shapes. The five groups of anatomy files, their contents list and the body rig save 996,262 bytes, or about 1 MB. If your browser needs the 142,385-byte decoder to open them, the total saving is 853,877 bytes, or about 0.85 MB. For a local copy, follow the [launcher instructions](https://eliminatenoise.github.io/NeuroTranslation/previews/neurosurge/START.html).
