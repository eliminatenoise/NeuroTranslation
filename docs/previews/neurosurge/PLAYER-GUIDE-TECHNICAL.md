# NeuroSurge player guide (technical)

## Keyboard focus and aiming

Full and Lite use the same game controls. Lite uses Brotli compression for the same anatomical meshes as Full. The downloadable Lite package contains the local Node.js launcher and the compressed Lite packs. Extract it before opening the launcher inside **lite**. Node.js 18 or later must be available on the computer.

When the game canvas has keyboard focus, **Left Arrow** and **Right Arrow** step through the motor areas. **Up Arrow** and **Down Arrow** change the vertical look angle. In the eye view, this input also moves the selected arm at the shoulder or the selected leg at the hip. Press **4** and then **Up Arrow** to test that response on the right arm.

The scrollable information card accepts focus with **Tab**. While the card has focus, the vertical arrows scroll its text. Selecting the canvas returns the keyboard to the scene. To carry the mirror body with the keyboard, use the arrows to place the aiming mark on its head or trunk and hold **Space**. Release it to finish the hold.

Press **E** for the outside view. When the layout has room, the limb selector and hint move to the side of the simulated head and trunk. On narrower screens their position depends on the space left by the information cards. The camera position stays under player control.

## Input and movement

- Start and **Resume** use a free pointer. Drag on empty space to turn the view. **Capture mouse** in the Escape menu enables browser pointer lock, so each mouse movement turns the view. Escape releases it. If the browser declines capture, drag on empty space to keep playing.

- The central aiming mark picks a motor area. Aim to trigger a movement or click to repeat it. Left and Right Arrow step across motor areas.
- During play, 1 selects the left arm, 2 the left leg, 3 the right leg and 4 the right arm. W, A, S and D move that limb. In the eye view, a small deliberate pitch change commands shoulder or hip movement within the current joint limits. The aiming cue stays 25% from the top of the screen. The actual wrist or ankle can move away from the cue when the camera moves or the joint reaches its limit.
- Space grips or carries the targeted object. Drag an object to turn it or a limb to move its joint. Shift selects the item behind the default target.
- E switches modes. The eye view rotates about a fixed position. The outside view orbits a fixed point at a constant distance. Each mode has a fixed field of view for the current window size. The camera does not follow the simulated body.
- R changes the information shown. M toggles the detailed electrical map. The normal view has a separate, lower-opacity electrical effect on the cortex and motor strip, with an off switch in Escape settings.
- Press Escape and open **View tutorial** to review its steps or resume or restart the sequence. Markers identify game regions or joints in view.
- In step 4, a vertical look in the eye view starts the measurement. The tutorial advances if the simulated wrist or ankle moves at least 2.5 cm relative to its shoulder or hip within 0.35 seconds of game time. The reference is its position just before the look. Press Up Arrow or Down Arrow, drag on empty space or move the captured pointer. Press **E** to return from the outside view.

When the scene is out of view, the recovery hint offers drag, arrow-key aiming and Escape → Reset brain view. With the brain out of view, the reset restores the initial look. Tutorial and information panels take pointer input, and the Brain ring marks a valid Space pickup.

Extra sharing parameters leave the selected edition intact. An unrecognized edition opens the address error panel. It offers the default game address through **Start the game**. Camera drag suppresses motor-area activation by the aiming mark. With the information visible, the ring in the limb drag step appears when a press at its centre can select the chosen limb. The view recovery message is available at every information level.

## Command box and camera

During play, **F** and **C** work on the game canvas and toolbar buttons. Text fields, selectors, links and other dialogs keep their keyboard controls. **Escape** dismisses the Command box or recorder and returns focus to the canvas.

In the motion recorder, choose **Left arm from the side** or **Right arm from the side** for forward or backward arm movement. Face sideways with the chosen arm nearer the lens. The local tracker checks the face direction, torso width, landmark visibility and image detail. It measures elbow bend and the direction of the upper arm relative to the shoulder-to-hip line in the image. The virtual right shoulder uses a target from −60° to 180°, with 0° pointing down. The virtual left arm uses the upper-arm and wrist directions in the same picture plane. Live capture, video and timed pictures use the same estimate. Press **F** and enter **replay my take** or **replay my left arm take** after saving. The left comparison shows the signed image angle. Its rate uses the shorter turn across the overhead boundary. The right comparison shows the avatar target. The wrist-path study uses front-view arm takes.

- **F** opens the Command box. Enter a supported movement or a sequence of up to four movements joined by `then`. Choose **Move** or press **Enter**. Where supported, **Use voice** fills the field through on-device dictation. A new command or a direct movement input ends the queued sequence.
- **C** opens the motion recorder in the hosted Full and Lite game. **Movement forecast** opens the Movement mirror. You can also open it in the **F** Command box with “forecast a tracked point”. In the motion recorder, the **Follow** chooser is available before **Start camera**. Arrow-key changes leave focus in the chooser. Before capture, **Tab** moves to **Start camera**, which requests video only. The local tracker estimates arm and knee positions and drives the selected joints while the player controls the view. Use **Stop** in the Movement mirror or **Stop camera** in the recorder to release video. **Close** or **Escape** closes the Movement mirror.
- In the game’s Movement mirror, **Body follows** selects the body targets and **Point to forecast** selects the image point to predict 120 ms ahead. The default is both arms and knees. The recorder’s single-arm, knee, pair, side-arm, hip and whole-leg choices are available. For a side view, face sideways with the selected limb nearer the camera. Keep the required body points and forecast point visible. Across camera, local video and timestamped images, a side-view knee needs two consecutive accepted bends of at least 45°. Other available targets can follow while it waits.
- Each picture supplies both joint estimates and forecast coordinates. Wrist coordinates retain the same visibility and image-detail checks across body choices. Open **Controls** in compact view. Changing **Body follows** preserves the forecast history. Switching between front and side choices releases the old pose until a fresh picture is ready. Changing **Point to forecast** starts a fresh forecast window while body following continues. **Forecast only** returns body control to the player. Video pause, seek or end and the end of a picture sequence release the tracked joints. The point forecast can continue when tracking supplies too few joint readings to save the take. A side-leg take needs two accepted samples of the same joint. **Save take** retains the readable targets and timing for playback from **F** with the camera off.
- In the Movement mirror, **Save take** keeps up to eight seconds of selected pose estimates and available arm marker positions on the source clock. It accepts live camera, decoded video and timestamped stills. Video uses the current playback position. Stills retain their assigned spacing, including time before the first usable pose within the saved interval. Pause or source end retains the eligible window. Source, file, limb and seek changes clear the unsaved window. **Stop** and closing the mirror discard unsaved values. Changing the forecast point preserves the take window.
- **Open Command box** stops capture and opens the same **F** interface as the game. Use **replay my take** or **Named takes**. The snapshot is held in this tab and contains movement values and times. Full and Lite use the same capture and replay controls.
- A video pause, seek or ending releases tracked joint control. The still sequence releases it at the end. The last matched forecast comparison can remain on screen. The separate virtual goal continues to drive its own simulated arm.
- The recorder reports **Following** for an accepted limb, **Show a bend** for a visible knee waiting for a clear starting bend, and **Bring into view** when tracking cannot return an accepted image estimate for that limb. Green lines join the estimated shoulder, elbow and wrist or the estimated hip, knee and ankle for accepted limbs. The image is mirrored. Labels refer to the player’s own sides.
- Live tracking in the recorder and Movement mirror starts each knee after two consecutive accepted images with at least 45° of estimated bend. A missing knee releases its control. The starting check expires 600 ms after the first missing-knee observation if the knee stays out of view. Visible arms can keep moving independently. Movement mirror forecasts still use the tracked image positions and their capture times.
- The Movement mirror predicts the selected point 120 ms after the last of its five input frames. It compares the forecast with the nearest usable point within 40 ms either side of the target on the same source clock. The scores give pixel distance for the forecast and the stay-put guess against the same matched position. The display names the last input frame. It indicates when the matching frame was processed before the reply. A missing comparison is shown as unavailable. Virtual movement is scored against simulated points. Source, point and clock resets clear prior comparisons and cancel pending requests.
- The recorder submits one image at a time. Frame callbacks identify new images and space grabs at least 50 ms apart. Polling waits 90 ms after a result or unsuccessful check and uses the displayed-frame count where available. With either frame API, a gap releases tracking, hides the overlay and shows **Camera on. Waiting for video.** after 1.2 seconds. A gap of about 15 seconds stops the camera. A stationary limb still tracks if new images arrive. Clock-only browsers may retain tracking on a held image. Use **Stop camera** and **Start camera** to recover. Manual movement stays available.
- A take retains time-stamped pose samples in the current tab. In the motion recorder, select **Record take** and choose **Finish take** before changing limbs to keep the take. A limb change cancels an unfinished recording. Use **F** and **replay my take** to replay the saved poses with video off.
- The browser runs the original 32-channel, four-head attention block with the stored FP32 weights for landmark movement and next-unit probabilities. Point forecasts use a model fitted to optical animal tracks. [The recorded-image test](technical.html#camera-test) scores predictions against projected optical joint positions. Type **raise my right hand above my shoulder** for a virtual goal, or use **Virtual brain** to score invented spike histories. The separate fingertip task uses the [local model launcher](START.html#local-model). The camera estimates positions in the image plane and the game simulates its brain and muscle activity. [Checkpoint and source details](scene/src/joint/model/NOTICE.html)

### Side-view leg tracking

In **C**, select **Left leg from the side** or **Right leg from the side**. Turn the selected leg towards the camera and keep the face, hips, knee, heel and forefoot visible. Start camera capture. The projection uses the nose and ears to find forward and the shoulder and hip width to require a side view. It measures thigh relative to torso, shin relative to thigh, and heel-to-forefoot relative to shin. The target ranges are −30° to 120° at the hip, −135° to 0° at the knee, and −50° to 20° at the ankle. Zero ankle target corresponds to a 90° angle between shin and foot.

Image detail and landmark checks gate each target. The knee starts after two consecutive accepted bends of at least 45°. The hip and ankle can follow independently of that starting check. Read **Tracking by limb** for each joint. A missing foot can release the ankle while the hip stays visible. The game view stays under player control.

Before opening the recorder, select the game leg with **2** or **3** if you plan to use manual movement keys. While **W**, **A**, **S** or **D** is held, it takes precedence for that leg. Capture resumes joint control on the next accepted frame after release.

Local video and timed pictures use the same projection. **Finish take**, **Save video take** and **Save picture take** require two samples of at least one common joint for a side-leg take. The take stores accepted angles and sample times. The source images stay on the device. Use **F** and **replay my left leg take** or **replay my right leg take** to replay the last saved take when its side matches. Playback keeps capture off. Use **Named takes** to retain earlier recordings in the tab.

In six SUMediPose walking recordings set aside for evaluation, the near-left side view supplied 630 eligible images. Before the game’s two-picture knee readiness check, the per-image tracker returned all three targets on 150. On the 203 matched images with an ankle target, mean absolute error was 7.04° against independent optical markers projected into the image and limited to the game’s target range, compared with 15.69° for a constant 0° ankle target. These averages weight each person equally. The forefoot reference marker was over the second metatarsal head, at the ball of the foot. Right-side testing used recorded footage to check game response. [Walking recordings and marker setup](https://doi.org/10.7910/DVN/GRRROM)

### Follow an arm and leg together

Select **Left arm and leg from the side** or **Right arm and leg from the side** in **C → Follow** or **Movement forecast → Body follows**. Frame the near side with the face, shoulder, elbow, wrist, hips, knee and foot visible. Select **Start camera** and approve access for a live source. The modes reuse the arm and whole-leg image-plane estimates from one pose detection. The knee starts after two consecutive accepted bends of at least 45°. It releases on loss and needs a new starting bend after 600 ms out of view. Each accepted shoulder and elbow, hip, knee or ankle target can follow independently across camera, video or timed pictures. **Point to forecast** remains independent and predicts 120 ms ahead. **Forecast only** releases body control.

A take needs two accepted observations of any one selected part. It keeps per-part counts and preserves side-view arm directions during replay. In the recorder, use **Record take** and **Finish take**, **Save video take**, or **Track pictures** then **Save picture take**. The Movement mirror uses **Save take**. Use **F → replay my take** or **replay my left arm and leg take** with the camera off. Compare only angles present in both takes. In a 37-image recorded check, 34 left and four right selections returned an arm and at least one leg target. Sample by sample, the returned targets matched the separate choices and the 74 coordinate sets for point forecasting matched the default mode. The check compares integration outputs on recorded images.

## Follow recorded video

- Press **C**. Set **Source** to **Recorded video**, choose limbs under **Follow**, select **Local video**, then choose **Play video**. The browser must decode the file and provide video-frame callbacks. The file limit is 512 MiB (536,870,912 bytes). Tracking runs locally.
- Playback uses each presented image's time from the decoder and processes one image at a time. The recorder displays the stored orientation and estimates two-dimensional joint positions. **Following** and a green line mark an accepted limb. **Waiting for a bend** marks a visible knee awaiting a clear starting bend. **No usable pose** means the image yielded no accepted estimate for that limb.
- A result 1.2 seconds or more behind playback is discarded. Pause, seek, end or changing the selected limbs clears tracking. **Stop video** closes playback and retains the selected file for a new start. **F** stops playback and opens the Command box.
- **Save video take** keeps pose estimates and projected arm points from the last eight media seconds up to the playback position. It uses the source frame times and includes tracking gaps in its duration. A gap longer than 1.2 seconds releases the pose at that limit. Save while playing, paused, stopped or ended. A single limb needs two accepted samples. The paired modes require two per side. **Both arms and knees** requires two from any one limb and keeps the other visible limbs where available.
- A seek, limb change or new file clears the unsaved window. An explicit save replaces the latest take. Press **F** and enter **replay my take**, or use **Named takes** and **Save last take** to replay it by name. The take retains projected data. Picture and sound stay in the source file.
- Select **Start camera** under **Live camera** to grant video access. **Movement forecast** opens the Movement mirror, which also accepts video and timed pictures. Electrical activity is simulated.

## Make a take from timed pictures

- Press **C** and select **Timed pictures**. Select 2–256 local images up to 32 MiB each. Enter one distinct time in seconds per listed file or give each file a numeric name such as `1.25.jpg`. Times must be zero or positive. The images are processed in time order within an eight-second span. Replay time starts at the earliest assigned time.
- Choose **Track pictures**, then **Save picture take**. One limb needs two accepted poses. The paired modes need two per side. **Both arms and knees** needs two from at least one tracked limb. A knee first needs two consecutive visible bends of at least 45° to calibrate. The second bend can count toward the two accepted poses.
- Press **F** and enter **replay my take**. Use **Named takes** to save a name. Replay blends joint angles and left-arm directions between adjacent accepted poses on the render clock. An absent limb releases at its stated image time. The stored poses and times used for comparisons or forecasts come from the individual images. An unsaved take clears when its inputs change. The local images remain on the device and the saved poses remain in tab memory.

## Shared engine and assets

Both editions use the same simulation, region picking, body rig, materials, tutorial and camera. Lite’s compressed files reduce transfer by 996,262 bytes across the five mesh packs, index and rig. The separate decoder adds 142,385 bytes when the browser lacks built-in support. The decoded bytes are identical. [Pack checks and local launchers](lite/README.md).

Joint and muscle behaviour approximates the displayed anatomy. The electrical activity is illustrative and unitless. It comes from the simulation and is not a recorded brain signal. [Credits and licences](CREDITS-AND-LICENCES.md).
