What the clock measures

From the LED go cue, the first force sample must be below 0.5 N. Confirmation is the tenth consecutive reading at or above 0.5 N. It comes 18 ms after the first reading in that qualifying run. Shorter excursions reset when force falls below threshold. This is a force proxy for contact. First anatomical touch and live sensing delay were outside the experiment.

Each forecast uses the latest 200 rows through issue on the 500 Hz clock, a span of 398 ms from first to last row. Its target is the compression force 60 rows later. The cue sets the trial clock. An earlier NeuroTranslation study used a separate cue-aligned forecast.

Open your own signals

Use the aligned sensor arrays described in the input guide. A clocked capture needs LED onset, grip and load in N, and the four XYZ trackers in the released source scale. Supply synchronized scalp EEG or surface EMG to inspect those branches. A prepared 200-row window supplies its own confirmation offset and time since the go cue. The file stays in this browser tab. Loading another input or closing the tab clears it from the viewer.

Study, weights and limits

WAY-EEG-GAL recorded a pinch lift with two force plates, four position trackers, scalp EEG and surface EMG. The browser replays the frozen ridge and causal CNN weights fitted on six separate training volunteers. Source and package checksums are recorded in the guide.

Matthew D. Luciw, Ewa Jarocka and Benoni B. Edin recorded the study, supported primarily by the European Commission’s WAY project (FP7-ICT-288551).

The synthetic examples illustrate the calculation. The development table uses measurements from the study. The CNN and ridge differ in how they represent history and how they were tuned. Neither the comparison nor scalp EEG establishes a cortical mechanism. Surface EMG records muscle activity through skin electrodes.
