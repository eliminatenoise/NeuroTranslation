How close were the forecasts?

For each eligible recording we can set the forecast beside the next 32 measured marker positions and the measured electrical amplitude of each usable muscle. The two studies followed different people, tasks and coordinate systems. Only the time scale is shared.

What did scalp signals add?

Adding earlier voltage from three scalp sites reduced the average balance muscle error by 0.0143 µV. Replacing that voltage with an earlier trial from the same person gave almost the same muscle error. Average movement errors increased in both studies. Each difference below subtracts the reference model’s error from the model that used the scalp signal. Negative values mean a smaller error.

Explore the recordings and methods

Luciw, Jarocka and Edin recorded the right wrist and arm during grasp and lift. Peterson and Ferris recorded lower-leg muscles and a marker over the sacrum during standing and walking. An electrode measures electrical activity at the skin. These passive recordings support forecasts within each task. They cannot establish how to control a person’s movement.
