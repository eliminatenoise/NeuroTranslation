A practice pinch from an earlier day did as well as a fresh one

Four adults followed the same pinch-force cue on two recording days. For each target, we kept the correction from the earlier day and fitted another from the new day’s first pinch. All eleven forecasts faced the same second pinch.

The earlier day’s practice did as well as a fresh one: at every target, the two corrections’ average misses were within 0.001 percentage points of the hardest pinch (maximum voluntary contraction, MVC); the largest gap was 0.00065. A simpler rule beat both at 10% and 50% of the hardest pinch: there the current force plus the change in the on-screen cue missed by 0.38 and 0.58 points, against 0.56 and 0.71 for the best fitted forecast. At 30%, the fitted forecasts did best (0.45 against 0.48).

Reading the comparison

The force gauge measured the squeeze between two digits. The forecast uses force history and the scheduled cue. The five-grid version also uses forearm surface EMG. Each correction changes the forecast when the cue changes over the next 24 force samples, a nominal 120 milliseconds.

Each person contributes equally. Trials share a fixed order, and nearby cutoffs overlap. The gain range describes the four people who took part. The gauge readings use each day’s own maximum, so equal percentages on different days may stand for different absolute forces.

Signed error is forecast minus gauge. A positive value means the model predicted too much force on average. Target-relative error uses the prescribed target as its denominator.
