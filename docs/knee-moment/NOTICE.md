# Walking study and model credits

Emma Reznick, Kyle R. Embry, Ross Neuman, Edgar Bolívar, Nicholas P. Fey and Robert D. Gregg released the continuous treadmill recordings under CC0 1.0. The walking study fitted knee moment forecasts from prior optical angles, using inverse-dynamics moments as references. The browser includes fitted weights, aggregate results and an invented walk. [Versioned recordings](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.14558181.v1) · [Acquisition and processing](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-021-01057-9)

The movement encoder was originally fitted on tracked movements from two sources. Xin Wang, Taein Kwon, Mahdi Rad, Bowen Pan, Ishani Chakraborty, Sean Andrist, Dan Bohus, Ashley Feniello, Bugra Tekin, Felipe Vieira Frujeri, Neel Joshi and Marc Pollefeys released *HoloAssist* (ICCV 2023) under the Community Data License Agreement – Permissive 2.0. Justin Shin and Shantanu Jadhav released *Single Day W-Track Learning*, version 0.240511.0307 (2024), under CC BY 4.0. The inherited spatial weights carry CC BY 4.0. [HoloAssist](https://holoassist.github.io/) · [Included CDLA agreement](CDLA-Permissive-2.0.txt) · [W-Track recordings](https://doi.org/10.48324/dandi.000978/0.240511.0307) · [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)

NeuroTranslation reused the spatial projection and attention block, permuted individual tensors for the shuffled control, fitted new angle-only linear readouts, and separately updated a copy of the encoder for a knee moment residual. This export flattens those tensors into JSON for the JavaScript reader. Retain the attribution and applicable terms for reused or adapted weights, and identify further changes. The new reader code, explanatory text and invented input generator carry the project's CC0 dedication.
