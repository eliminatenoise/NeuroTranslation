Edit marker positions and muscle readings

The 24 marker frames arrive 10 ms apart. Each frame contains a right heel and second metatarsal position, both in laboratory XYZ millimetres. Four consecutive 60 ms bins hold the root-mean-square surface muscle voltages in microvolts (µV), oldest first. All readings end at or before the cutoff.

Changing the sliders builds a new invented history. To keep your own marker and muscle arrays, edit the strike offset here or use the time selector.

What happened on recorded walks?

Errors compare predictions with camera and plate measurements. Smaller is better in each column. The heel error is the three-dimensional distance to the measured endpoint. The load error is the absolute difference from the measured upward load. Each person counts equally, each walk within a person counts equally, and each eligible cutoff within a walk counts equally.

Thirty people formed the original development roster. Ten others had already appeared in earlier assessments. A separate reserve of ten people remains unopened. Repeated cutoffs from one walk share observations.

What the instruments saw

Two dots on the right foot

A camera system follows skin markers on the heel and the head of the second metatarsal, near the base of the second toe. X runs along the plates, Y across them and Z upward in the laboratory. The arrow joins the last heel position to its predicted position ten frames later. The path between those two positions is unknown.

A plate under that foot

The reference is the mean vertical load across 15 samples at 1,500 Hz, centred on that future heel frame. It measures the net load at the assigned plate. A force reading alone cannot tell us what each muscle contributed. This reader predicts numbers on a screen. It sends no commands to a limb or device.

Which steps qualify?

Barefoot C2 walks in Luxembourg used a metronome to target 0.4–0.8 m/s. Source foot assignments, annotated strikes and foot-off, and the heel and forefoot positions on the plate at strike and target support right-foot contact. The entire future force block has to fall in stance. Those future checks select the outcomes on which the models are scored. They are unavailable to the model. A forecast here assumes that support without testing a future contact.

Which signals can the models use?

Four successive bins describe the two markers’ relative motion, their separation and heel height, plus time since the annotated right foot strike. The full models also use electrical activity from eight surface electrodes over right-leg muscles. Each specialist learns from the same full history as the full joint model. The markers-and-time joint model learned without muscle input. No model shown here receives earlier plate force or future measurements.

Surface electromyography (EMG) measures electrical activity at the skin over muscles. The recordings do not directly measure nerve commands. The archived signals used two-way filtering (6 Hz markers, 15 Hz force, 30–300 Hz EMG), reconstructed marker gaps, trial-wide EMG centring and event-based cropping. A cutoff on these processed signals does not establish the delay of a live sensor system. The force processing applies a 50 N floor, so a stored zero can also reflect processing.

Where do the measurements come from?

Florent Moissenet and Céline Schreiber released the v8 gait recordings under CC BY 4.0. NeuroTranslation paired heel-marker displacements with upward plate forces from the same foot and instant, fitted the predictors and calculated their errors. The editable examples above are generated from simple curves.
