Right arm / Movement forecast

The elbow after the cue

A person sees a cue to bend or straighten their elbow. A sensor in the arm support records what happens next. Once the first quarter-second has passed, how much will its reading change over the next three quarters?

Observed sensor outcomes · Development runs

Did scalp activity help?

Each method faced one entire run after fitting on the other two. The elbow sensor gave the answer on every trial. Take the distance between the forecast and that answer, then average it. A smaller mean absolute error is better.

How to read the controls

Keep the cue and elbow readings. Add C3, a scalp electrode over the left side of the head. An apparent gain could come from the visual cue, eye movements or the movement itself.

Replace C3 with eye recordings. Three electrodes near the eyes give a comparison. Another control substitutes the C3 summaries from the next trial with the same cue in the same run, wrapping back to the first trial at the end.

Change how the four scalp summaries are combined. The exploratory attention calculation gives each time bin a weight and averages them. It tests one pooling rule. Its mean error on these runs was slightly lower than the cue-and-elbow forecast, but it did worse on bend trials. This separate comparison had no matching attention-specific eye or swapped-trial test.

One bend trial from run 1 had been examined during an earlier source check before the forecast protocol was fixed. All 36 trials from these three development runs contribute to the scores.

Acquisition and attribution

What was measured?

The signalled task

One seated participant moved a supported right arm. Codes 0x600 and 0x601 identify elbow flexion and extension. “Bend” and “straighten” describe those cue classes. The release does not establish the exact words shown on the screen or what the participant intended.

Three kinds of reading

The exoskeleton elbow sensor sampled at 64 Hz. Scalp electrical activity (EEG) and eye-area electrical activity (EOG) sampled at 512 Hz. C3 identifies a scalp electrode. The signal cannot establish which brain structure produced it.

One signed outcome

Each elbow summary takes the middle of eight recent readings, averaged across the two middle samples. Subtract the summary at +0.25 s from the summary at +1.00 s. The result measures change on the stored sensor scale. The recording supplies no verified conversion into elbow degrees. Display latency, sensor delay and clock drift are unknown.
