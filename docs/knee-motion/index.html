What did the sensors measure?

Body-worn inertial sensors recorded motion. Xsens software reconstructed the stored ZXY joint angles. We use its signed flexion/extension estimates for walking on a treadmill at 0.5 m/s. All 51 study files report 60 Hz, so 15 frames span a nominal quarter of a second. Six files used Xsens HD, which processes the whole recording. The other 45 carry the label “Live.” Neither label establishes end-to-end sensing latency. This is a forecast within a stored recording.

The transformer learns how the six joint angles change together. The two fitted linear models use either knee readings alone or all six joints. The simpler rules keep each knee where it was or continue its last slope. Every forecast estimates two angles in a recording. The viewer sends no movement command. Brain and muscle electrical signals have no verified sample alignment to these frames and do not enter the model.
