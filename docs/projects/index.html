NeuroSurge

Move a virtual body and see a second body mirror your movements.

GitHub repository

Move from the motor strip

Aim at the motor strip, a part of the brain that helps control movement. Watch which part of the virtual body moves. The electrical activity and movements are simulated.

Both editions play the same. Lite downloads smaller anatomy files. Use a desktop browser with a keyboard and mouse or trackpad.

Play the Movement mirror preview

Open Full or Lite. Press C to open the recorder for your camera, a video or timed pictures. Choose Movement forecast to see where the model predicts a tracked point will be 0.12 seconds later. Press F to replay a saved take with the virtual body.

Compare four ways to predict where a body point will move. The test used recordings of 86 people and separate measurements of their movements.

Download the walking forecast comparison

Save the calculator and both blended forecasts for use offline. The included guides explain how each blend combines the physical forecast with part of a model's prediction.

Download the 0.1 walking checkpoint

The ZIP contains the saved changes from training and a runnable example with made-up readings for two force plates. Use the last 200 milliseconds of muscle and force readings to forecast whether a floor plate will start carrying load and how much vertical force will add up over the next 100 milliseconds. To run the example, use Linux on an Intel or AMD computer with Python 3.13 and at least 64 GiB of available memory. Get the separate 19.53 GB base model during setup.
