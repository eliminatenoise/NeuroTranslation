Where does the heel go when the belt speeds up?

A treadmill has a separate belt under each foot. After the right belt speeds up, we use earlier readings to forecast how far the right heel will move up or down in the next tenth of a second.

At the withheld timings, the conventional movement reference averaged 46.87 mm of error. The linear model using movement, muscle and force averaged 66.14 mm. Error fell for 1 person and rose for 4. The planned one-sided sign test gave p = 0.96875.

Two tests on people the models had never seen

The familiar timings appeared during fitting. The later timings were held out with the new people. Each person has equal weight in the final average, after averaging within each timing and trial.

How the comparison works

Cameras followed markers on the leg. Electrodes recorded electrical activity from muscles, and force plates measured contact with the treadmill. The models used readings available at forecast time. We saved the forecasts before extracting the later heel positions.

We chose settings using 392 events from four fitting participants. The strongest conventional movement-only reference was chosen on those fitting folds. The separate linear and attention comparisons pair the same model family with different sets of readings.

The results cover an offline forecast on one treadmill setup. Further recordings are needed to assess how well the same models work in other settings.
