Can a chair forecast predict a squat or a lunge?

The models had learned how the hips move during standing up, sitting down and the first steps away from a chair. We took those same models to a new archive and asked them to predict a rise or fall over the next tenth of a second. The new recordings also include squats and lunges.

The simple linear fit gave the smallest average error with full camera history across all three movements. For 107 people in the chair trials, the one-archive fit averaged 3.62 mm. For the movements held out of training, the two-archive fit averaged 3.01 mm in squats from 29 people and 3.51 mm in lunges from 36 people.

When the two-archive attention model had just two camera positions, adding measured floor force lowered error in all three tasks. The chair error fell from 6.32 to 5.74 mm. On lunges with sparse camera readings the two-archive linear fit still had lower error than that attention model.

The chair catalogue had 135 people. One supplied the technical sample. 121 other chair trial files lacked at least one required marker label. The results describe the people and trials with matching camera and force histories.

What the camera flags tell us

The captures name four markers on the left and right of the pelvis. A residual is a camera-file value that can record how well the contributing views agreed. The positive-only table uses trials and times where all the required earlier marker positions and both endpoints have positive residual flags. The file may retain those flags after the source team fills gaps or filters the tracks. The authors report both kinds of processing. The flags show what the published file records, while performance on unsmoothed or live cameras remains open.

Two calibrated plates measured how strongly the feet pressed against the floor. Two camera positions were 150 ms apart. The full history adds position and speed information at additional earlier times. The models were frozen before any test recordings were scored. Leeds supplied no muscle recordings for this comparison.

The study participants had undergone hip replacement. The test measures predictions of their recorded movements. Differences between the training and test archives could come from the people, equipment, movements or data processing.
