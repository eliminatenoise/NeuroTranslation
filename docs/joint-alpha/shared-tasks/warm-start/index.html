Can six forecasts share a model?

Reuse the trained layers?

NeuroTranslate already has a set of layers that read earlier measurements. We tested whether those weights help when fitting six forecasts across human balance, macaque reaching and macaque grasp.

The grasp spike forecast gained slightly

Continuing to train the reused layers reduced grasp spike-count loss from 0.70196 to 0.70095 nats per sorted unit per 20 milliseconds. Both fits favored reuse. Fresh weights gave lower average errors on both human balance tasks and on reaching movement. The two reaching movement fits differed in direction. Freezing the reused layers had higher average errors than starting fresh on five tasks.

Movement and muscle errors use the spread of values observed during training as their scale. Spike scores measure the penalty for how much probability the model assigned to the counts that followed.

Equal new training, different starting points

The fresh model began with new weights throughout. In the two reuse conditions, the middle layers started from earlier training. The input and readout layers start from the same new weights in all three conditions. Reuse and train updates every layer. Keeping the middle layers fixed trains just the input and readout layers.

Each fit received 480 new updates. The reused layers came from an earlier 1,920-update fit. Both repetitions used the same internal comparison groups. The three human comparison participants and original macaque trials were weighted separately within their tasks. These samples came from archives used before, with external performance still untested.
