# Rights, attribution and publication methods

Kai Joshua Miller deposited *A library of human electrocorticographic data and analyses* in the Stanford Digital Repository. Miller and colleagues reported the original experiment in *Human Motor Cortical Activity Is Selectively Phase-Entrained on Underlying Rhythms* (2012). [Stanford Digital Repository](https://purl.stanford.edu/zk881ps0522) · [Library descriptor](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-019-0678-3) · [Original publication](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1002655)

Stanford's version 11 catalogue licenses the source under Creative Commons Attribution–ShareAlike 4.0 International. Its use statement requires respect for privacy and confidentiality, including protection against identification. Preserve attribution, indicate modifications and use the same or a compatible licence for adaptations. [Stanford catalogue and terms](https://purl.stanford.edu/zk881ps0522.json) · [CC BY-SA 4.0 licence](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/)

This adaptation uses the 362,000-row development prefix of one recording. It derives two bands of cortical power, cue features and five measures of short-window glove movement, fits seven readouts and reports equal-trial and matched-repeat errors. The trained weights, training scales, study aggregates, model-derived example outputs and scientific account are distributed under CC BY-SA 4.0. The original Python code and invented input generator are dedicated to the public domain under CC0 1.0. Source measurements stay in the originating archive. Both licence texts are included. [CC BY-SA 4.0](licenses/CC-BY-SA-4.0.txt) · [CC0 1.0](licenses/CC0-1.0.txt)

For this browser, the six cue, cortex and combined fits are converted to an offline representation. Synthetic 24 and 90 Hz waves supply invented inputs. The interactive estimates and recorded aggregate results inherit CC BY-SA 4.0. Original browser code and the invented signal generator are dedicated under CC0 1.0.

## Original study ethics statement

The archive's task README requires the following statement in the Methods of publications using the data. Its reference identifies the original Miller et al. (2012) paper linked above.

Ethics statement: All patients participated in a purely voluntary manner, after providing informed written consent, under experimental protocols approved by the Institutional Review Board of the University of Washington (#12193). All patient data was anonymized according to IRB protocol, in accordance with HIPAA mandate. These data originally appeared in the manuscript “Human Motor Cortical Activity Is Selectively Phase- Entrained on Underlying Rhythms” published in PLoS Computational Biology in 2012 [Reference].
