Source and scope

What did the cameras see?

Schreiber and Moissenet recorded barefoot adults walking overground. Skin markers tracked the outside of each ankle and knee and the thumb side of each wrist. Two more markers on the front of the pelvis helped locate the moving body. Eight surface electrodes measured electrical activity in the right leg.

The model reads 40 frames at 100 Hz and predicts six changes in position 20 frames later. The recorded markers were gap-filled and filtered in both time directions. The muscle traces were also filtered in both directions. The 200 ms horizon refers to the stored recording clock. Live forecasting still needs a test with causal signal processing.

A marker follows a point on the skin. The six outputs say how far those points travelled along three laboratory axes. They do not measure nerve signals, joint angles, foot contact or forces.
