# Can a scalp recording improve a forecast of the arm?

A person sees an instruction to bend the elbow, straighten it, turn the forearm in either direction, close the hand, open it or rest. One quarter of a second later, a model receives the instruction, the short movement record so far, and six scalp readings. Its job is to predict what the elbow, forearm and index-finger instruments will read half a second, one second and one and a half seconds later. This is an offline test of the supported right arm in one cued task. [Experiment](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0182578) · [Data and codebook](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.834976)

## People and clocks

Use only S1–S7, motor-execution runs 1–3, for seven leave-one-person-out development fits and a final fit on all seven. S8–S12 each supply one fourth execution run for the prospective assessment. Other runs from these five people appeared in an earlier mechanical experiment. The assessment recordings are new to these fits. Each run should have six trials for each of seven instructions, in chronological order. Require original source checksums, known layout, unique onsets, a matching end marker at +1536 ticks and the three pre-cue markers at −1024 ticks. If the planned event roster or file layout differs, stop for source review. Report omissions of incomplete or nonfinite windows for all methods together. Retain flat channels, outliers, zero movement, and movements in either direction. Withhold a confirmatory gate if coverage is incomplete or a training output has SD below 1e−8. All inputs come from the same stored clock. No movement onset, display latency or physiological delay has been calibrated.

Use acquisition columns 13,17,28,32,43,47 for FC3,FC4,C3,C4,CP3,CP4 and columns 62,63,64 for the three eye signals. Preserve numerical eye-channel order where header labels vary. At 512 Hz the four consecutive 64-sample means cover cue-relative ticks −127…−64, −63…0, +1…+64 and +65…+128 inclusive. Subtract the first bin from each bin separately within each electrical channel. This removes static offsets without using another trial. The second bin includes the cue tick. Apply no new filter or common-reference subtraction. Electrical values retain their native file units.

Elbow, forearm rotation and proximal index glove use columns 93,94,68 sampled at 64 Hz. Let L=floor((cue+128)/8) in native rows. Divide L−31…L into four chronological eight-row bins. Retain each instrument's median and its last-four minus first-four median. Subtract the final bin's three levels from the four level vectors and retain these three final levels as separate anchors. Each target is the eight-row median ending floor((cue+128+h)/8) minus that last input-bin median, for h=256,512,768 ticks. Keep nine outputs in horizon-major then instrument order. Instrument units, directions and between-person calibration remain unresolved.

## Fits fixed before training

Balance people equally, runs within person, cues within run and trials within cue. Use only fitting people to learn scalers or predictions. For each mechanical and electrical coordinate, estimate its weighted mean and population SD over the four input bins. Estimate the three anchor scales and nine output SDs over fitting trials. Replace input SD below 1e−8 by one. Clip each standardized input to ±6. Retain every target at its original magnitude. No assessment-person normalization or adaptation. “Typical for the cue” is the training-weighted coordinatewise median (the midpoint across a central tie). The global median is computed in the same way.

Each Transformer has five width-eight tokens, four time bins plus one summary token. Every time token starts with a linear projection of 15 coordinates (six mechanics, six scalp, three eye). Every architecture uses identical parameter shapes, initialization and optimizer settings. Unused standardized coordinates become zero. To isolate the instrument source in the standalone eye comparison, place its three values in slots 6–8, which are also used by the three scalp contrasts in a second, equal-active-width comparison. For the combined model use the final three slots for eye. The summary combines a learned cue embedding and projected mechanical anchor. Each scalp-only or eye-only fit has a constant eighth cue and zero mechanical features and anchors. Add learned positions, one two-head scaled dot-product attention layer, a residual and layer normalization, a width-12 tanh feedforward layer, and a second residual and normalization. The summary feeds a linear nine-output residual head initialized at zero. Add its prediction times each target's fitting SD to that cue's training median (global median for scalp-only and eye-only). Mechanical-history alone means known cue plus mechanics.

Train history, scalp plus history, eye plus history, scalp plus eye plus history, scalp only and eye only from independent initializations of the same seed 29371. Also train (a) three bilateral scalp differences FC3−FC4, C3−C4, CP3−CP4 plus history, standardized with their own training scales, and (b) scalp plus history trained on a mismatched scalp trial. The latter cyclically moves each scalp record one position ahead within the same person, run and cue, using cue onset order; no target enters pairing. Keep separate ridge fits on scalp plus history and history alone. Ridge uses flattened standardized time bins, anchors and seven one-hot cue flags; fit the same cue-median residual by weighted squared error with penalty 0.25 on coefficients, unpenalized intercept. Include cue median, cue mean and zero change as simple baselines.

All neural fits use seed 29371, AdamW (learning rate 0.007, weight decay 0.025, default betas), 48 full-batch updates in float32, with weighted smooth L1 loss (beta=0.5) across standardized outcome residuals, no dropout or checkpoint search. Clip gradient norm at 1. Use deterministic PyTorch operations and verify independently implemented NumPy inference against the final PyTorch prediction. For each neural fit retain initial/final loss and parameter changes. The CPU implementation is the scientific reference.

## Assessment and decision

Compute absolute error in each of nine coordinates, divide by its own fitting target SD, then average the coordinates. Average trials within cue, cues within run, runs within person, then people equally. Report each method's overall standardized error and nine native-unit MAEs, equal-person errors and paired differences. Report an exploratory paired 95% percentile interval on each difference, resampling whole people 10,000 times with NumPy PCG64 seed 71293. Development folds share training participants. Five assessment people give a wide margin for uncertainty.

For the fitted scalp, scalp-plus-eye and separately mispaired-trained scalp models, also score replacing only the scalp values by each of the other trials for the same person, run and cue in turn. Hold each recipient's cue, mechanics, eyes and targets fixed. Average the absolute errors over these wrong pairings before aggregating. These retrospective pairing controls cannot issue live forecasts.

Each formal contrast requires overall error at least 5% lower than its comparator and strictly lower error in every assessment person. Give separate decisions for scalp over history, over its own wrong-pair and the separately wrong-pair-trained control evaluated over all wrong pairings (also show that fitted control on correctly paired assessment inputs descriptively), over eye, equal-width scalp differences over eye, combined scalp and eye plus history over the eye-plus-history fit and its own scalp shuffle, and attention over same-input ridge. Assess practical utility against the cue median, cue mean, history, history ridge and zero change. A broad engineering advantage requires every practical comparator plus the two scalp pairing checks. Any claim of incremental scalp information beyond the measured eye signals also requires scalp over eye and both combined-model comparisons. Always present equal-width and architecture checks separately. Even a measured advantage cannot identify a cortical generator or establish a gain in another laboratory, task or person. No formal superiority decision from development. No refits following the fourth-run comparison. S13–S15 and imagery are outside this protocol.

## Data and rights

Patrick Ofner, Andreas Schwarz, Joana Pereira and Gernot R. Müller-Putz released the recordings under CC BY 4.0. This study adds the clocked features, fits, portable prediction and comparisons. The earlier single-person C3 experiment found no consistent benefit. A development signal audit found that scalp and eye bins share trial-specific variation, especially at frontocentral sites. Neither check establishes a forecast gain. No raw recordings or individual trial arrays are included.
