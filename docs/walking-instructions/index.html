Did the cue help on recorded walks?

The researchers compared each forecast with the position of the same landmark 100 ms later. The error is the distance between those two points, in millimetres. Smaller is better.

For each landmark, the correct cue must beat both the withheld and swapped cues. We subtract each control’s error from the correct-cue error. Both paired 95% intervals must lie below zero. Intervals resample whole people 5,000 times. People count equally, as do instructions within person, walks within instruction and windows within walk.

Previously assessed participants

The fits and scoring rules were fixed before this assessment. These ten people belonged to earlier gait studies. Eight contributed eligible walks to the earlier C2 heel-and-force assessment and six also contributed later repeats.

10 of 10 people contributed both instructions. 60 walks yielded 216 of 240 candidate windows. 112 usual and 104 fast windows entered the paired comparison.

What the experiment measures

Two instructions

Schreiber and Moissenet describe barefoot walking along a 10 m walkway. C4 carries the instruction “as usual”, C5 “fast but not running”. The authors report these phrases in the protocol. The recordings supply the condition of each trial, with no speech track or timestamp for either phrase. C1–C3 used a different protocol and fall outside this comparison.

Two landmarks

The pelvis is the centre of four skin markers. A fifth marker tracks the right heel. Twenty-one samples at 100 Hz supply each history. The pelvis’s horizontal travel sets forward. Left and up complete the coordinate frame. The model also receives elapsed time since the first right foot strike. It estimates six displacements together.

What can change

Changing the text changes a two-value condition code. Swapping the cue in a recorded walk tests how the model responds while the measured outcome stays fixed. We do not know how that person would have moved under a different instruction on that same walk. General language comprehension, neural activity and the ability to command a body remain untested.
