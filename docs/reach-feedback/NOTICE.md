# Recordings, models and reuse

Raeed H. Chowdhury, Joshua I. Glaser and Lee E. Miller released the recording used in this reader. Rhesus macaque C performed a random-target reach task on 7 September 2017. The source member is reaching_experiments/C_20170907_TRT_TD.mat, SHA-256 6062b7e8dacdf46f7eee465bd844ffe1fb3b7ce0793c34d3e4d106be357f4746. The source deposit carries a CC0 1.0 public-domain dedication. The article describes the original experiment. [Dryad data and terms](https://doi.org/10.5061/dryad.nk98sf7q7) · [Area 2 of primary somatosensory cortex encodes kinematics of the whole arm](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.48198)

NeuroTranslation selected the first eligible example in each chronological workspace-1 split and in the workspace-2 transfer split. The source examples are sequences 2, 410, 520 and 1 respectively. Each cutoff falls five 10-ms rows after a subsequent go cue. Twenty previous rows supply eight position coordinates, spike counts for 23 electrode/sort pairs and three cue fields (number of preceding go events, time since the last, and whether a prior go exists). At each history row only cues strictly preceding its beginning enter those three fields. The future position rows span 0 to 90 ms after cutoff, or 10 to 100 ms after the last history sample. Delayed spike counts occupy the following interval from 100 ms up to 200 ms. The chart places each 10-ms count at the bin centre.

The hand and near-elbow marker coordinates are shoulder-relative. The planar handle coordinates come from an encoder and have a separate origin and scale. Source markers were processed offline; exact visibility masks are unavailable. The recording includes offline spike sorting and bidirectional handle filtering. Some session conditions, including eventual reward and sufficient following recording, enter the sample selection. Both workspaces were visited by one animal on one day.

The supplied movement model fits future positions to the earlier pose, sorted spikes and task cues. A subsequent count model takes this predicted movement and the same history. Its comparison model takes direct history alone. The augmented model was fitted with movement forecasts made on later training pieces by a motor model trained on earlier pieces. The first training piece had no such forecasts. The three browser fits all use seed 17. The held results report losses averaged over the three fixed seeds 17, 41 and 73. Model predictions, hypothetical edits and averaged expectations are derived here. The source authors did not supply these models.
