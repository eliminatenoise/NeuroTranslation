Inside a reaching arm

As an elbow bends, the distance a muscle and its tendon span can change. Cameras tracked two points on this arm. A computer model used the original tracking to calculate 39 such paths. Can a forecast of the arm’s movement tell us how much each path will change in the next tenth of a second?

Where do these lengths come from?

The original study estimated muscle–tendon lengths by fitting an anatomical arm model in OpenSim to motion tracking. Here, a second model learns how changes in hand, elbow-marker and handle position relate to those estimated lengths. Its coefficients come from 7 September. The later examples are from 14 September, one week afterward.

A positive number means the path grew longer than at the last known position. A negative number means it grew shorter. These distances tell us how the modeled arm changes shape. The recording contains no direct measurement of muscle activation or force.
