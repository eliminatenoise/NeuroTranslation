Tail-variation forecasts across two zebrafish recording dates

A Transformer fitted to earlier camera measurements and logged visual commands gave the lowest mean absolute error on the 12 later recordings, 0.020223 radians. The camera-only Transformer scored 0.020686 and carrying forward recent movement scored 0.023516. Adding command history reduced the camera-only error by 0.000463 radians. The current-command model improved on each comparator in nine of 12 recordings. Carry-forward had the lowest aggregate error on the four earlier development recordings.

Running the model


The runnable example supplies one invented input row to seven methods. Each result is the predicted population standard deviation of signed tail bend, in radians, during the 100 ms starting at issue. Use Calculate forecast after changing the example or entering a row. Restore example restores the original input and recalculates.


The camera scale multiplies the four bin means, the four bin spreads and the recent spread. The two reversal controls multiply the signs of the ten display entries in the selected history. The controls preserve each sample and confirmation time. Edit input JSON shows the exact row. Once you edit this field, calculate the edited row or restore the example to use its controls again.

Opening the offline package


Extract the ZIP and keep its directory together. Open index.html, or run Linux.sh, macOS.command, or Windows.cmd on the matching system. Both editions open the same full double-precision weights in the browser. The command line uses the identical model when Node.js is installed. From the extracted folder, run node model/cli.cjs model/example-input.json and read the seven values in radians from the JSON output.


Two dates define the comparison


Ryosuke Tanaka and Ruben Portugues describe the Figure 4 protocol as head-restrained larval zebrafish viewing a visual pattern whose direction was programmed externally. The archived embedded flag is a software setting and cannot establish physical restraint for an individual file. We used all 29 date-qualified Figure 4 recordings, assigning each whole recording to one role. The fitting group comprised 17 recordings from 13 April 2023, including the previously analysed engineering recording. The test group comprised 12 from 16 May 2023. Swimming frequency and forecast performance played no part in selection.


The date and local fish number identify a recording, with numbers 1 to 12 reused across dates. May recordings f7 and f8 have distinct files but share a session label. The archive does not resolve physical identity across dates or independence within a session. Age and sex for individual records, apparatus equivalence and the separate contributions of fish, preparation and date remain uncertain.


Camera measurements and logged commands


The camera field tail_sum records signed cumulative tail bend in radians. The program field LDPS_drift_direction records the commanded pattern direction as −1 or +1. On a 320 ms grid starting at zero on the software clock, forecast the population standard deviation of signed camera tail angle over [t, t + 100 ms), where t is the issue time. The target has units of radians and describes dispersion about the window mean. A sustained bend can have a large angle and little dispersion.


We retained native temporal order. Duplicate timestamp groups were ineligible. For a historical input, a strictly later timestamp in the same stream had to close the selected group by t. Each of the four preceding 50 ms camera bins and the 100 ms target needed at least two eligible readings. Internal gaps and gaps to either edge of each interval could be at most twice that recording’s median positive camera sampling interval. The gap check applied within each earlier bin. At every command cutoff, we used the latest eligible value at or before the cutoff, with age at most twice the command log’s median positive interval. Feature and target camera rows were disjoint throughout each recording. These tests concern stored software times. Physical screen onset, camera exposure and live arrival time were unmeasured.


Earlier recordings set the model


Carry-forward used the pooled standard deviation over the two most recent 50 ms bins. Three linear ridge models learned corrections to it. All used the four bin means and population standard deviations from the preceding 200 ms. The current-command model added the logged direction at 5, 4, 3, 2, 1, 0.5, 0.2, 0.1, 0.05 and 0 s before issue. The control shifted all ten cutoffs six seconds earlier, retaining real history potentially informative about later movement. Each fitting recording had equal total weight, divided equally among its usable windows. The ridge models disabled features that were constant in the fitting data. Each varying feature was centered at its fitting-only weighted mean and divided by its fitting-only weighted population standard deviation, using these recording weights. The response for regression was the measured future spread minus the recent-variation baseline. The objective minimized the weighted mean squared error of the fitted correction to that residual plus 0.1 times the sum of squared slopes, with an unpenalized intercept. At issue, each ridge model added its correction to the baseline and set a negative sum to zero.


Three matched Transformers represented camera summaries and, in the two command variants, direction readings and their observed ages as inputs to an attention model. Attention allows the influence of one reading to depend on the others. Each Transformer estimated a signed correction to the same recent-variation baseline. Its output layer began with zero weights and bias, so the initial correction was zero. During fitting, the squared error compared the unclipped sum of baseline and correction with the measured future spread, with the same equal total weight per recording. Each shuffled batch’s weighted sum of squared errors was multiplied by the number of batches in a full pass. Each variant was trained separately with the same Adam optimizer settings and Float64 arithmetic. At forecast issue, the frozen model added its correction to the baseline and applied a lower bound of zero to the sum.


The first 13 earlier recordings supplied 83,258 development-fit windows. The last four in numeric label order (f14, f15, f17 and f18) supplied 25,438 check windows. The width choice minimized the mean of the three Transformer variants’ equally recording-weighted check errors. Width 24 gave 0.037345 radians and width 32 gave 0.037672. An exact tie favored 24. The two candidates used four attention heads, two layers, respectively 48 and 64 feed-forward units, six epochs, batches of 128, seed 104729 and learning rate 0.001. The earlier check ranked the carry-forward baseline first at 0.023542 radians. The rule selected the Transformer architecture and preserved every comparator for the later test.


Of 191,233 earlier grid times, 109,005 passed the clock rules and 108,814 supplied finite inputs. A further 118 lacked a finite target, leaving 108,696 windows for the final fit on all 17 earlier recordings. No later inputs or outcomes entered selection or fitting.


Error on the later date


Of 134,988 later grid times, 90,357 passed the clock rules. Insufficient finite input support removed 130 before issue. Independent review reopened all 631,589 nonnegative predictions across seven methods and 90,227 windows and verified 2,103,065 requested later tail-angle rows before their camera values were read. Zero of those native camera rows occurred among the earlier feature rows for the same recording. A further 78 windows had nonfinite targets. Every method was scored on the same 90,149 windows.


Within each recording, we averaged the absolute difference between forecast and measured spread over its scored windows. The cohort mean gives equal weight to each recording. For a paired comparison, subtract the reference method’s absolute error from the comparison method’s absolute error on each window, average within recordings, then across recordings. Across 720 populated one-minute blocks of stored time, the current-command Transformer improved on camera alone in 470, on carry-forward in 424 and on the older-command Transformer in 718. These are descriptive subdivisions of the same recordings. The recording-level means and win counts carry no population confidence interval.


An independent check compared 9,529 numerical fields using the frozen predictions and saved target spreads. The largest absolute discrepancy was 3.33 × 10⁻¹⁶ radians. That check covered case matching, finite masks and recording, minute and paired scores. It did not repeat extraction of angle values from the original camera files. The experiment tests transfer to the held date within this source. Sensory exposure, neural activity and transfer to another laboratory remain unmeasured.
