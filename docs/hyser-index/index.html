    What the forecast measures

    Hyser recorded five finger-force sensors as adults pressed in self-chosen or prescribed combinations on two separate days. This forecast uses the index sensor’s voltage. The two recording systems shared a start trigger. Their drift and the processing delay in the released force trace remain uncertain.

    Does seeing the other fingers help the forecast? When fitted models are included, compare the index-only forecast with one that reads all five histories and one that adds just the latest voltage and fitted slope of each of the other fingers. All three predict the same index reading from samples through the same cutoff. To judge any gain, compare errors at matched cutoffs across people.

    The finger-context forecasts learned from the Random task. Across the same four development participants scored in the earlier study, all five histories averaged 14.161 mV of absolute error on that task. The index history alone averaged 14.355 mV. Both scored the same 18,000 cutoffs over two days. Two people improved, and the middle 95% of the paired resampled gains included zero. On cross-validation with training participants, the index history had lower mean absolute error. The four reserved participants remain unopened.

    In N-DoF, participants followed prescribed finger combinations and force trajectories. “Index cued” describes the instruction. The sensor can still record voltage when the index finger is uncued. The forecasts use voltage through the cutoff and receive no cue. A trial score here covers one selected recording. Read the prescribed-task study

    On four development participants, the force-history model (a straight-line fit on the index sensor’s recent readings) averaged 14.355 mV of absolute error. Adding aligned forearm EMG gave 14.457 mV, 0.10 mV higher, with a 95% range for that change that includes zero; EMG taken from 400 ms earlier gave 14.426 mV. Two people improved and two did worse. Both models scored the same 18,000 cutoffs across the two days.

    Across two days, each population forecast keeps its shared weights. Day 1 fits just an offset and a change in gain. Day 2 can show a lower error, a higher error or no change. The shared fit may already include the selected participant, so a local comparison alone does not tell you how the method fares on new people.

    In the cross-day study, the population force model averaged 14.912 mV of absolute error on Day 2. Fitting on the same person’s Day 1 raised the error to 15.091 mV. Fitting on another person’s Day 1 raised it to 15.401 mV. With surface EMG, the corresponding errors were 14.903, 15.122 and 15.496 mV. The same four development participants had already been scored in the original study. They formed two donor pairs. The four reserved participants remain unopened. Read the cross-day study

    The forecast uses the released force samples at 100 Hz. Optional surface EMG runs at 2,048 Hz on its own clock. The reader subtracts each channel’s recorded baseline, then divides by its gain to give volts. It keeps the opened files and Day 1 coefficients in browser memory for this page.
