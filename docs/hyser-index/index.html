    What the forecast measures

    Hyser recorded five finger-force sensors as adults pressed in self-chosen combinations on two separate days. This forecast uses the index sensor’s voltage. The two recording systems shared a start trigger. Their drift and the processing delay in the released force trace have not been established.

    On four development participants, the force-history model averaged 14.35 mV of absolute error. Adding aligned forearm EMG gave 14.46 mV. Two people improved and two did worse. Both models scored the same 18,000 cutoffs across the two days.

    Across two days, each population forecast keeps its shared weights. Day 1 fits just an offset and a change in gain. Day 2 can show a lower error, a higher error or no change. The shared fit may already include the selected participant, so a local comparison alone does not tell you how the method fares on new people.

    In the cross-day study, the population force model averaged 14.912 mV of absolute error on Day 2. Fitting on the same person’s Day 1 raised the error to 15.091 mV. Fitting on another person’s Day 1 raised it to 15.401 mV. With surface EMG, the corresponding errors were 14.903, 15.122 and 15.496 mV. The same four development participants had already been scored in the original study. They formed two donor pairs. The four reserved participants remain unopened. Read the cross-day study

    The forecast uses the released force samples at 100 Hz. Optional surface EMG runs at 2,048 Hz on its own clock. The reader subtracts each channel’s recorded baseline, then divides by its gain to give volts. It keeps the opened files and Day 1 coefficients in browser memory for this page.
