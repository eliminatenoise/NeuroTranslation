Where did each wrist land?

An endpoint is a position (x, y) in the original image. Error is the straight-line distance to the later wrist. Divide that distance by the median width between the shoulders in the 15 earlier frames to compare camera scales.

Which arm is which?

In the recordings, a wrist movement identified afterwards sets the cutoff. “Contralateral” means the wrist opposite the side of the brain implant. “Ipsilateral” means the wrist on the implant side. Swapping sides lets the same model follow the annotated wrist from one person to the next. The implant supplies a side label. The model receives camera positions alone.

What can the other arm tell us?

The Transformer can give weight to earlier frames from either arm. Block its view across arms, or substitute the other arm from an earlier event. Every version still uses both shoulders to orient the camera frame. The matched versions learned from the same set of events with an eligible earlier partner.
