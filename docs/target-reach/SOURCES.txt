Target reach • NeuroTranslation experimental reader, v1

Extract the entire target-reach folder. Open index.html in a web browser, or use
the dedicated launcher for Windows, macOS or Linux. Local computation uses
files beside the page. The atlas, paper, citations and download link go online.

Use the hand selector and the assigned target to choose a recording. The clock
starts at the first usable optical measurement, or at the third consecutive
250 Hz camera frame at least 15 mm from that starting position. Five measurements
over the next 0.200 s enter the forecast. Future coordinates are displacements
from the last input. Their clocks read 0.400, 0.600 and 0.800 s from the anchor.
Controls work with a keyboard. Select a target button or type “target 5” and
press Enter. Forecasts run on this device.

Each displayed hand/target example is the first eligible held-out participant,
then recording, under the original ordering. Selection uses input availability.
Missing endpoints stay missing. Separate clock models use development-only
parameters. The motion-only arm excludes the target and the target-only arm
excludes the optical coordinates; both retain hand and time. The source study
presented visual destinations and an acoustic go signal. Typed commands are
reader controls. An edited target is a conditional simulation with no matched
measurement; the three endpoints are discrete predictions at three times.

This adaptation selects and averages source optical object markers, samples
original world-frame positions and takes their differences in metres. A missing
member of a trial’s fixed marker set makes a frame unavailable. Published
optical data can contain fills made during the authors' optical reconstruction.
The original camera axes are preserved. Object motion cannot identify joint
angles, muscle signals, neural activity or the meaning of a novel sentence.
