What will the pedal read 100 samples later?

A seated volunteer tries to lift their forefoot against a fixed pedal. One sensor reads the load. Electrodes pick up electrical activity from the shin muscle. Can that muscle signal tell us more than the pedal’s recent history already does?

What happened on the recordings?

Each forecast had an answer on the lab recording. We subtracted the pedal reading at the cutoff from the reading 100 samples later. An error of zero means the forecast got that signed change exactly right.

Two people were reserved. Only one had a complete set of usable trials.

The planned two-person test of the muscle signal could not be scored.

One neutral trial stored two columns for the right load. The record did not establish which column to use or provide a verified common 1 kHz grid. This left the other neutral trial without its paired muscle trace.

The following comparisons keep each person and setting on its own available support. Seven usable trials supplied 446 forecast windows. A matched other-trial signal existed for 388. A lower mean absolute error means a closer forecast.

Complete held person · both settings

Four trials at the two pedal settings. The mean gives the settings equal weight, with equal weight for each trial within a setting. This group supplies 272 forecast windows.

Mean absolute error was 0.0545 with load, setting and muscle, and 0.0626 with load and setting. The same model scored 0.0625 after the muscle traces were swapped between trials.

Development comparison

Two people used in development

Each development volunteer was forecast by models fitted on the other volunteer. The final browser models were then fitted on both. The comparison used 624 windows from eight trials, two pedal settings per person. Each person and setting carried equal weight.

The matching muscle signal fell short of the planned test for an improvement in these two people.

Mean absolute error was 0.0749 with load, setting and muscle, 0.0840 with load and setting, and 0.0804 when the muscle trace came from the other trial. The best comparison without muscle input was “Repeat the recent change” at 0.0615.

How the improvement test worked
  • At least 5% lower error than the best comparison without muscle input. Missed.
  • At least 2% lower error than the swapped muscle trace. Passed.
  • Lower error than load with setting and the swapped trace for each person. Missed.
  • No higher error than either of those two methods at either setting, averaging across people. Passed.

Adjacent histories can share readings. The test counts two participants, regardless of how many windows they supplied. These are descriptive thresholds for this task.

What the model sees

Two histories on one clock

The model uses a nominal grid of 1,000 sample positions per second. Some recordings have a stored time vector. Others use the reported rate to label successive rows. Three usable trials from one held volunteer include the authors’ 1 kHz export, which matches every tenth reading of the 10 kHz source. The clocks cannot verify the physical interval between every pair of readings. The model divides the last 200 sample intervals into ten intervals. From each it takes the ending load, how far load has moved, the muscle signal’s average and spread, and how quickly that signal changes. The full model also combines muscle-signal spread with the recent change in load.

The second pedal setting is the archive category “angle5.” Its toe-up or toe-down direction remains unverified. The setting is fixed for each trial. A small Transformer learns which intervals to give more weight. Its output is the signed change in the sensor channel labelled “right.” A positive change means the recorded number rose. Anatomical side and the direction of physical force do not follow from that label or sign.

Does the muscle signal have to match?

In a retrospective control, we keep the original pedal history and setting and substitute the muscle signal from the same person’s other trial at that setting, at the same sample index. The recorded trial pair is available after collection. The browser lets you make that swap with two invented signals.

The electrodes record skin-level muscle activity (surface EMG). Neither nerve activity nor the force of an individual muscle was measured here. The measured gain needed to convert this load channel into force or joint torque was unavailable. These forecasts describe a seated ankle task. They establish no clinical effect or causal response to changing muscle activity.
