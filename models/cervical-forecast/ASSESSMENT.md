# How six new people tested the forecast

Fit the Transformer variants, linear comparisons and robust readout on the four development participants. For each new person in the same study, forecast that person's first 80 qualifying events. Each person has equal weight in the mean absolute error and mean of the person-level root mean squared errors, in microvolts. In each comparison, gain is the control's mean absolute error minus the matching-arm forecast's error.

For the Transformer, compare with the matched architecture trained without the axillary channel and with the **same fitted full model** given the early arm voltage from 37 events ahead in the same run, wrapping at 80. Each of the two average gains must be positive, and at least five of six people must improve against both controls. The separate mismatched model in development uses a different control. It was trained and evaluated with an arm signal rotated 37 places in the opposite direction. `transformer_deranged` identifies that separate model, and `transformer_same_fit_wrong` identifies the six-person test of the unchanged full Transformer.

The robust readout starts with the most recent one-millisecond neck-voltage bin. Its control swaps all eight arm bins with those 37 events ahead. Its average error with matching arm readings must be at least 5% lower than both controls, and all six people must improve on both. Each two-sided sign test drops exact ties. Within each model family, the two gain lists use the same anonymous order, sorted on the first comparison and then the second to resolve ties. This preserves each person’s pair of gains for checking the count who improved on both. The two model families have separate orders. Neither model met its criterion.
