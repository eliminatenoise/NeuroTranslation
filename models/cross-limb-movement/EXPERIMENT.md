# What the two fits share

One study follows a wrist, the other the base of the spine. In each, electrodes record the contraction-associated electrical activity of named muscles. The input covers the 625 milliseconds up to the forecast. Each of twenty bins ends with a measured marker position in millimetres, expressed relative to the cutoff. A second number gives the age of that position at bin end. For each available muscle, the bin also holds the root mean square of its recorded voltage. Eight released samples supply a leg bin and 125 supply an arm bin. The 32 future marker positions are 1/256 second apart. The numerical sign of a WAY axis does not carry anatomical meaning in the other study's optical frame.

The forecasts use 3,924 learned parameters. Within each fitting split, each muscle's scale is the median of its available last-125-ms RMS values across fitting events, floored at 1e−8. Marker scale is the median of the event means of the twenty historical distances to cutoff, floored at 0.1 mm. The histories alone supply those scales. Each muscle's available patch value enters as log(1 + RMS / scale). A learned name embedding and shared amplitude slope yield an eight-dimensional vector through a rectified linear unit. Its mean across available names is concatenated with scaled marker XYZ, marker age at 500 times its value in seconds, and study, posture and rotation direction. The hand contributes its fixed grasp-and-lift context. Series and participant identities never enter the model.

A linear layer embeds each patch in 16 dimensions. Add a learned patch-position vector and segment embedding. Both studies update the same two-head temporal attention block, 32-unit feedforward layer and layer normalizations. Every input precedes or coincides with cutoff. The final state combines with each available muscle name to predict a scaled correction to the preceding 125 ms RMS. Physical amplitude is clamped at zero. The path decoder queries that same state at 32 learned offset embeddings. At each offset it adds a time-scaled correction to a straight-line extrapolation from the two latest causal marker knots, using their observed time difference. The result is displacement in the study frame. No dropouts or early stopping are used.

## What 192 updates buy

Participants stay together. Sort the fitting participants within each study, assign successive ranks modulo three, fit on two folds and score the third. Repeat across the three folds, then refit once on all 18 lower and six upper development people. Each domain draws 48 events per batch with replacement. A participant has equal probability to every other participant in the same study. Within a balance participant, weight postures equally and rotation directions equally within posture. Within an arm participant, weight numbered series equally. Events within a cell have equal probability. Every fold recomputes its own scales. RMS loss averages only the usable channels in each event. Path loss averages the 32 offsets and three coordinates. Both are smooth L1 losses on scaled outcomes with a unit transition. The two losses receive equal weight.

AdamW uses a learning rate of .002, weight decay .0001, default betas and epsilon, and a global gradient-norm cap of 1. The seed is 1641. The joint fit alternates lower and upper batches, lower first. Its 384 updates expose it to 9,216 sampled events per study. A 192-update single-study fit sees exactly the same 9,216 target draws, in the same order. A 384-update specialist continues from that same state with its optimizer intact to see 18,432 target draws. All three networks have the same parameter capacity, optimizer and event-batch size. For the joint network, half the total examples come from the other task. Report scores for both specialists. Equal numbers of updates need not take identical wall time.

For each transfer direction, start from the 192-update source fit, reset the optimizer, then adapt every weight with the exact 192 target batches used by the scratch comparison. The frozen foreign diagnostic has no supervised target updates. It still uses target-fitted history scales and retrieves target name/segment embeddings that received no target gradients. AdamW can decay unused entries, and the common coordinate decoder has learned the other study's geometry. Neither condition establishes a strict zero-target calibration result. For an additional label-scrambling check, permute paired muscle and marker labels together across distinct, nonoverlapping source trials within person, series/posture, cue, availability pattern and the original lower-series third. Pretrain with or without that permutation on the identical qualified source rows and draws. Adapt on unchanged target examples in both cases.

## What counts as an error

Score every method on the same eligible historical events. Whole-path error averages Euclidean distance across all 32 points. Endpoint error uses only the last point. Muscle error takes the mean absolute difference in native RMS across each event's available muscles. Balance cells receive equal weight within person, WAY series equal weight within person, and people equal weight within study. For signed channel errors, re-average over cells and participants where that channel is available. Include lower participants with both postures and both directions. Future data quality contributes to event eligibility, so reported errors are conditional on passing the full signal-quality test. [Source preparation](source/README.md)

To claim a joint advantage, all four joint-minus-specialist primary means and all four one-sided 98.75th-percentile paired upper bounds must be below zero. Each bootstrap samples whole people within their study 5,000 times with seed 1641. Report two-sided 95% intervals and the number of people with smaller error as well. Three arm participants allow only 27 ordered bootstrap draws, or at most ten distinct means before ties. The extreme order statistic can determine a reported upper bound.

For the wrong-trial control, keep the recipient's marker and task context. Move the full muscle history and its RMS baseline together from another valid event of that person. Use the same WAY series, or the same lower posture, visual direction and original series third. Require distinct events more than .752 seconds apart for WAY or more than 207 samples apart for balance. In cutoff order, choose the smallest common cyclic offset giving the most valid pairs. Compare on the intersection of the recipient and donor muscle sets, with the original minimum channel count. The second control keeps the recipient's true RMS baseline and swaps only the patch history. A third allows each event's full original availability at input and scores on the common set. These interventions test dependence on the recorded input, without changing a person's actual muscle activity.

The final joint fit drew 4,296 distinct balance events out of 5,179 and 1,756 distinct lifts out of 1,764. Those same rows and frequencies appear in the two 192-update specialist fits. The shared fit took 0.720 s of warm optimizer time, versus 0.360 s and 0.362 s for the short balance and arm fits. The second target-only blocks took 0.361 s and 0.363 s respectively. Across all three folds and the final fits, training, prediction, scoring, serialization and exports took 27.26 s in one invocation on the study machine. The complete 16-update actual-data benchmark took 2.07 s with one CPU thread, 1.91 s with three, and 2.45 s on the Radeon 8060S with ROCm, including startup and export. All three used the same updates and examples. These single-trial timings describe this machine and workload.
