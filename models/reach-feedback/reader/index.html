Lower error is better. The score is Poisson deviance per sorted unit for the whole 100-ms count. We averaged errors from three independently initialized fits and gave each original sequence equal weight. The intervals come from 5,000 resamples of blocks of eight consecutive sequences.

What did the other comparisons show?

Each matched reach had an earlier reach in the same workspace and block at the same go-cue number. Swapping in that reach’s forecast increased error in workspace 1. The correct path minus the borrowed path gave −0.01940 (95% interval −0.03747 to −0.00476). Workspace 2 gave −0.00329 (−0.00802 to +0.00241). The planned test required the correct path to beat both borrowed motion and direct history in both workspaces. It fell short.

Adding earlier spikes to the movement model helped forecast the hand endpoint in workspace 2 by 0.964 mm on average. Elbow-marker error rose by 0.305 mm. The test required improvement at both markers. The matched count comparisons used 113 of 116 eligible workspace-1 windows and 822 of 825 in workspace 2. Their full rosters contained 43 and 321 original sequences respectively.

What the instruments recorded

Researchers tracked a hand marker and a marker near the elbow in three dimensions relative to the shoulder. A robot encoder tracked the handle in its own plane. Electrodes recorded activity in area 2 of the primary somatosensory cortex. A “sort” distinguishes spike waveforms grouped as a unit on a given electrode. An average forecast of 0.3 spikes can make sense across repeats even though one measured bin contains a whole number of spikes.

Each recording here is the first eligible example in its split. Seed 17 identifies the fitted Transformers used in the reader. The motor model takes 20 earlier samples. The count models read those samples either directly or alongside ten forecast displacements. Their training used movement forecasts made from earlier training sequences. The first training block supplied that initial history and has no count-model prediction in this study.

The task controller marked go cues. Its exported data omit the random target coordinates. Both workspaces were visited on the same day by the same animal, and the trials interleave in time. Prior smoothing, interpolation, bidirectional handle filtering and offline spike sorting limit claims about live timing. These comparisons test whether one forecast helps another. Establishing that movement caused the later firing would take a different experiment.
