# Can a short arm movement predict the next one?

A participant sees a cue to move the supported right arm or hand, or to rest. Four consecutive bins record the elbow, forearm and index finger from before the cue through a quarter-second after it. Can an attention model trained on other people use those bins and the known cue to forecast the next three readings?

## People and clocks

The seven fitting participants are S1–S7 of the 2017 Ofner release. Leave each person out once for seven development folds. Fit one final model to all seven. Freeze weights, scoring code and evaluation rules before opening S8–S12, who supply one prospective assessment. S13–S15 remain in reserve. Only the first three motor-execution runs per included participant enter. The numbered split follows the published file order. Each participant used the right arm. S1's first three runs have previously supported within-person analyses. This is a new model trained from scratch. [Original data](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.834976) · [Study](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0182578)

Cue codes 0x600–0x606 indicate elbow flexion, elbow extension, supination, pronation, hand close, hand open and rest. Convert GDF event positions to zero-based ticks at 512 Hz. A trial needs exactly one matching end marker before or at the next cue, at least 896 ticks after onset. At the last cue the recording end bounds the search. Require complete, finite samples throughout each required window. A missing channel or unverified file layout prevents use of the run and must be reported. Report all omissions. A participant must supply eligible trials in each scheduled run and each cue needs representation within each run for a confirmatory result. Keep flat signals, unusual directions, large valid excursions and unbalanced counts. Report flat target coordinates per run and the spread across each participant's instrument readings. A flat glove may indicate a sensor issue, little movement or both.

Channel numbers in the authors' table begin at one. Read 93 (elbow), 94 (forearm rotation) and 68 (proximal index-finger glove) at their native 64 Hz cadence, in that order. Define cutoff tick as cue tick + 128. Let L be floor(cutoff/8). The four eight-sample bins cover L−31…L−24, L−23…L−16, L−15…L−8 and L−7…L. For each bin retain the three medians followed by the three last-four minus first-four median differences. For each horizon 256, 512 or 768 ticks beyond cutoff, read the eight-sample median ending at floor((cutoff+horizon)/8). Subtract the corresponding last input-bin median to get the target. No target enters prediction or any assessment-person normalizer. File clocks leave up to 7/512 seconds between a requested time and the preceding mechanical sample. Sensor latency, physical units, anatomical signs and inter-person calibration remain unresolved.

## Fit

Weight each person equally, then each run within person, cue within run and trial within cue. Fit scalers on fitting people only. For each history token subtract the three last-bin levels from its first three coordinates. Estimate a common weighted mean and SD over all four tokens for each of the six coordinates. Standardize the separate three last-bin anchor levels across trials. Replace input SD below 1e−8 by one. Bound standardized mechanical coordinates at ±8 for every learned method. Keep out-of-training-range trials, flagging each when any of the 24 raw values lies outside its corresponding fitting min/max. Do not recenter, flip signs or calibrate on the new person. Standardize each of nine output coordinates by its weighted mean and SD on fitting trials. An effectively constant fitting target (SD below 1e−8) receives scale one for fitting and native predictions, with the nine-coordinate score and resulting superiority decision withheld.

The Transformer has five eight-wide tokens. Four come from a learned projection of mechanical bins. One combines a learned task embedding and the projected anchor. All five have trainable position embeddings. A single two-head scaled dot-product block attends over all available inputs, followed by residual addition and layer normalization, a width-12 tanh feed-forward block, residual addition and layer normalization. The task token feeds a linear nine-output head. All weights train. A no-cue fit replaces every cue by the same learned token in training and inference. Positional order is always chronological.

The matched MLP has one tanh hidden layer of width 18. It receives the same flattened 24 token values, three anchors and seven cue indicators. Its no-cue version receives seven zeros. Neural models use PyTorch default linear/embedding initialization, position embeddings drawn from N(0,0.02²), CPU initialization seed 20713 for each fit, no dropout, AdamW with learning rate 0.01, weight decay 0.01, default betas, 80 full-batch updates and equal-weight mean squared standardized target loss. Fixed choices apply in every fold. No hyperparameter or checkpoint search. The cue-only fit predicts the weighted training mean for each cue. The ridge fits take the same clipped inputs with and without the cue, standardize individual design columns, center the outputs and penalize coefficients by 0.20 in the weighted squared-loss objective. The intercept is unpenalized. Persistence predicts zero change. For each cue-conditioned method also score the six incorrect labels on exactly the same histories, averaging six absolute errors per trial. The corresponding no-cue control for the cue-only prescription is the unconditional weighted mean.

## Score

The primary error is mean absolute error divided by each of the nine training target SDs, averaged across coordinates, trials inside cue, cues inside run, runs inside person and finally people. Publish the per-person differences, all nine native MAEs, per-cue summaries and trial counts. Compare the Transformer against persistence, cue only, cue-plus-history ridge, matched MLP, separately fitted no-cue Transformer and all six incorrect labels. A consistent advantage requires at least 5% lower overall error and lower error for **every** assessment person against each relevant comparator. Report these gates separately for (a) cue contribution against the two cue controls, (b) attention architecture against ridge and MLP and (c) net practical benefit over cue alone and persistence. All three must pass to claim an overall advantage. Other contrasts are descriptive. No superiority claim follows from seven development folds.

For each prespecified paired comparison show the mean difference (model minus comparator), individual differences and an exploratory paired 95% percentile interval from 10,000 resamples of whole people, PCG64 seed 71293. Rebuild both means from the same resampled people. Overlapping training sets make development intervals descriptive. Five assessment people give the interval little precision, and the study supports only the sampled tasks, clocks and instruments. Quantitative results and null findings remain part of the release.
