A seated volunteer lifts the front of the foot while an ultrasound probe looks into the shin. In each image, the study tracks a bundle of muscle fibres, a fascicle. It estimates the length of that bundle in millimetres and the angle it makes with a sheet of connective tissue in degrees. The data come from Qiang Zhang, Noor Hakam, Oluwasegun Akinniyi, Ashwin Iyer, Xuefeng Bao and Nitin Sharma, *AnkleImage*, BioImage Archive S-BIAD1482, Task 3. This derivative selects two trials per person from the original tracking tables, takes the released estimates in their original order, adds row numbers and marks gaps in the tracking labels. The two measurements can share errors because the same software follows the same tissue. The scale comes from the authors’ published units. An independent physical calibration for each trial is unavailable here.

The extraction reads the geometry fields needed for each file. The first development volunteer has paired tables named A and B, along with separate length and angle vectors. The second has 9,920 actual tracker rows per trial, ten times as many as the other three development volunteers. The third has one or two numbered fascicle tables. The published length and angle curves reproduce that person’s first tracked fascicle. Its angle relative to the connective tissue is available, although the tissue sheet’s own orientation cannot be independently recovered. Elsewhere, the stored geometry agrees with either addition or subtraction of the two saved orientations. Each trial's source receipt identifies the formula that reproduces the released angle.

The saved TIME is the tracking software’s label. A development trial may advance by 0.002, 0.01 or 0.02 on its own scale. Ten successive rows have a clear place in the sequence. Their actual elapsed time would require the acquisition timing record.
