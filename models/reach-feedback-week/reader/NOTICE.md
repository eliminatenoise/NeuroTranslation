# Recording, models and reuse

Raeed H. Chowdhury, Joshua I. Glaser and Lee E. Miller published the recordings and the original account of area 2 during reaching. Rhesus macaque C guided the robot handle to targets in two workspaces. This reader uses the September 7 and 14, 2017 two-workspace reaching sessions. The Dryad deposit carries the CC0 1.0 public-domain dedication. The authors did not produce the models in this reader. [Dataset and terms](https://doi.org/10.5061/dryad.nk98sf7q7) · [Chowdhury, Glaser & Miller, eLife 9, e48198 (2020)](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.48198)

September 7 supplied 519 windows from 198 original sequences, all in workspace 1. Model fitting, feature scaling and the choice of later date preceded access to numerical measurements from September 14. Eligible sequences had a recorded reward and a sufficient interval around a subsequent go cue. The go cue numbered two or three on the later day. A workspace, reward or eventual end of a sequence informs eligibility only. The counts and positions in the test date kept their original axes and units. Each block has an independent source clock. The example selector orders eligible windows within each workspace and block, and takes the first window and the first with an earlier distinct sequence at the same ordinal. An absent earlier sequence has no donor forecast.

The hand (Marker_1) and near-elbow marker (Pronation_Pt1) have three optical coordinates relative to the shoulder, in metres. The robot encoder gives handle X and Y in centimetres in its planar frame. Sample k falls five 10-ms bins after the chosen go cue. For each axis the early velocity forecast extends the slope between k−5 and k−1. At the second forecast, the residual is the measured change from k−1 to k+9 minus ten times that slope. For each contact population, a separate pair of original-weighted log-link Poisson regressions predicts event totals from k+10 through k+19. Both receive last position, old slope, past 200-ms totals on the 37 primary contacts, number of prior go cues and elapsed seconds since the last cue. The updated model also takes the eight scaled movement residuals. Only the loss calculation floors expected counts at one millionth. The matched other-reach control substitutes a residual from an earlier eligible sequence at the same go ordinal, block and workspace. Full contact sums include positive and unsorted columns once each. The positive- and unsorted-only comparisons keep their own fitted weights.

The separately frozen positive-sort model uses its own contact's older and intervening spike totals, last go state, earlier motion and displacement observed at k+4 and k+9. The stale-position comparison also sees the intervening spikes. Its 17 shared contacts have at least one positive sort in every block. Original positive-sort numbers are session-local. The contact-grouping approach provides no evidence of stable individual neurons, stable detection threshold, or unchanged sorting. A third model averages three movement forecasts from a frozen pose-and-cue Transformer with a fitted residual informed by earlier contact counts. This trajectory stops at k+9. It cannot serve as evidence about cortical counts in the following ten bins.

The cortical recordings, reconstructed optical positions and filtered encoder traces came from an offline release. This comparison documents forecasting on a saved time grid in one animal on one held date. It cannot identify a causal feedback pathway or establish live sensor latency. The physical interval entered the count model as a measurement, independently of the movement forecast. Edited paths are hypothetical inputs and have no corresponding recorded response.

The movement and spike excerpts retain the original data dedication. The project dedicates the original reader software and its trained count models to the public domain to the extent of its rights.
