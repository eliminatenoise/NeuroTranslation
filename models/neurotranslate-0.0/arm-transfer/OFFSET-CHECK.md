# What if a sensor starts from a different zero?

Before opening assessment, a training-only inspection of the first three people showed that the resting level sampled at the cue cutoff varied across recordings. A constant added to every reading can change the absolute level without changing the movement between the cutoff and the endpoint. The pooled fit includes absolute levels and is free to use them. This additional experiment tests the consequence of removing those three inputs. It does not replace any comparison in the original protocol.

Fit two additional readouts on the same S1–S7 rows and with the same hierarchical training weights and ridge penalty. Keep the cue indicators, the 24 ordered motion features formed by subtracting the last level within each history and by taking half-bin differences, and, for the shared version, the identical frozen encoder summary. Omit only the three cutoff levels. Fit every scale and output on fitting participants only. Store both linear and shared versions, and report their native and standardized errors on the same S8–S12 primary support. Separately compare each against its otherwise matched original linear or shared version. Consider removal a consistent gain for that version only if the original participant-paired 5%, upper-interval and strict-majority conditions are met. These are additional comparisons motivated by the training inspection. A gain here would not establish differences in physical calibration or their causal role.

Numerically, adding [10, −5, 0.1] native units to the three median-level columns in all four input bins, leaving within-bin differences fixed, should preserve the offset-free outputs to floating-point precision. Check this property in the distributed model and retain all original results. Without measurements that establish the mapping into physical angles, the raw instruments and the forecasts remain in native units.
