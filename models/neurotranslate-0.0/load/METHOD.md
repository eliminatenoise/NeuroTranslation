# Can the recent walk predict a knee's turning moment?

## Question and original recordings

Use the 100 Hz optical joint-angle and inverse-dynamics knee-moment channels in Reznick and colleagues’ published Streaming.mat (CC0, Figshare 14558181 v1). The original analysis used camera reconstructions and external forces to calculate net internal moments about a joint. The signed sagittal knee moment in N·m/kg is positive for flexion. It measures neither an individual muscle’s force nor a direct reading inside the joint. The archive hash, nominal 0.8 m/s level segments and frame interpretation are already recorded in the external optical knee assessment. That earlier study looked at knee *angles* for all ten people. No held-person *moment* outcomes have been examined for this model before this design.

## Fixed sources and cutoffs

Fit once on AB01 through AB07. Seal the fitted model before loading the AB08 through AB10 moment samples, then score those three people once. There is no test-based tuning or person substitution. Use only the previously identified s0x8 steady segment under Streaming/ABxx/Tread/i0, conservatively trimmed inside both possible event frame conventions. Use index 0 of each source 3×N joint matrix for sagittal values. Six angles in degrees are ordered right/left knee, right/left hip, then right/left ankle. All 400 consecutive angle samples at indices t−399 through t must be finite and nonzero for every channel. Place t on the segment-local grid 399,404,409,…, provided t+25 is in the segment. The target is both knee moments in the same 100 Hz file at t+25. Both target components must be finite and nonzero. The paper reports exact-zero gaps in its stride data; this is a conservative exclusion in the continuous export, potentially also excluding real zero crossings. Count losses separately. Force plate file rates are not used. Source reconstruction and filtering latency is unknown. The task predicts later stored moments, not a live ground-truth deadline.

## Inputs and controls

Match the latest 100 samples of the bilateral knee angle to earlier samples for each integer period p from 75 through 300. Minimize summed squared error across those 100 pairs, resolving ties to the smaller p. The joint angles at t+25−p and t+20−p come from a completed earlier part of the walk. For a raw linear input concatenate the six angles at offsets −40,−35,−30,−25,−20,−15,−10,−5,0, the six at t+25−p, the six at t+20−p, the six differences between t and t−p, the period in frames, and the means of the six angles in each of four chronological 25-frame bins ending at t. No forecast sees a future angle or a moment measured from the target person.

Reference predictions are the person-equal training mean, a ridge readout on the raw input, and motion matching to training walkers. For the last reference standardize the 54 recent snapshot features with the training mean and standard deviation. Find the nearest row in squared Euclidean distance separately within each training person, shortest index on a tie. Predict the componentwise median of their seven matched later moments. No source windows will be shipped with the model; the comparator is reproducible from the original deposit.

Two additional readouts use the same raw inputs plus all four 24-wide hidden states of the published frozen shared movement encoder. Standardize the six bin means from training, project with the released spatial input layer and run the original attention block and final normalization. One control shuffles the entries of each sorted used weight tensor independently (NumPy default_rng 4281). For all ridge heads standardize design columns with equal total training weight per person; standard deviations below 1e−8 become 1. Fit in float64 with unpenalized intercept and lambda 0.03 on mean squared error. Include all eligible training rows. The trained baseline is common to all held-out subjects.

A separate task-trained Transformer starts from the frozen original block parameters, then adjusts its projection, position, block, normalization and a zero-initialized linear readout. It learns to correct the raw ridge prediction from the four standardized bins. Do not change the shared trunk for any other task. Use equal sampling across training people and then windows within each person, fixed seed 4281, float32 AdamW (learning rate 0.001, weight decay 0.001), batch 96 for 400 updates, no dropout, no clipping of output, no early stopping. Scale correction targets by the equal-person training standard deviation of the target (floor 1e−8 → 1). Fit against squared error in those scaled units. Export the separate updated block and readout for finite-history inference. Use a fixed single-process CPU route with limited math threads.

## Evaluation

Mean absolute error (N·m/kg) is averaged across right and left knees, cutoffs within each participant, then the three participants with equal weight. Report every method, each person's right/left errors and paired differences for the main comparisons. Descriptive support for the task-trained model requires lower error than both the raw ridge and training-walker matching in all three people. For a benefit of the original encoder require the frozen shared head to beat both raw and scrambled heads in all three. These thresholds describe three people, not statistical confirmation. Do not remove a difficult person or change the endpoint after scoring.

Two extra controls may use past actual moments, so report them on a separate common subset. Compare holding the current moment, replaying the moment at t+25−p, and the motion-only models on exactly the same rows where both earlier pairs are finite and nonzero. None of the learned model inputs contain those moments. Record future-mutation and portable inference parity, checksums, training cutoffs and past/target provenance. Keep both the previous independent optical angle result and this distinct mechanics outcome visible.
