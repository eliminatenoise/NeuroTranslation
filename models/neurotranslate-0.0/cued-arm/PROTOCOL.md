# From an arm instruction to three later instrument readings

Can knowing which action was cued improve a forecast of the supported arm and hand? Choose one of the seven source task categories, then provide four bins from three mechanical sensors. The model estimates the change in all three sensors half a second, one second and one and a half seconds after the last input. The cue is an investigator-defined category attached to a visual instruction, not a transcript. This experiment uses no EEG. A separate elbow experiment tests whether scalp measurements add information after the category is already known.

## Data selection fixed before reading new target windows

Ofner, Schwarz, Pereira and Müller-Putz recorded the right upper limb during visually cued tasks. Use only motor execution, participant S1, runs 1–3 of version 1 (Zenodo record 834976, CC BY 4.0). These are all designated development runs of one person. Hold each entire run out once, fit on the other two and publish all three comparisons. No other participants, later runs or imagined actions enter here. An earlier development check opened one flexion window in the first run. The model and evaluation below were fixed before new target windows were decoded. [Original data](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.834976) · [Study](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0182578)

Identify the visual cue from the seven original event codes 0x600–0x606 in source order: elbow flexion, elbow extension, supination, pronation, hand close, hand open and rest. Require a unique matching end event and at least 1.75 seconds of the trial after its onset. Retain all trials satisfying these rules and the source quality checks, whatever their eventual direction or amount of movement. The three channels in fixed order are 93 (exoskeleton elbow), 94 (exoskeleton forearm rotation), and 68 (proximal index-finger glove sensor). Use the authors’ sensor table and the native GDF header. Neither the sign nor the units supply a calibrated anatomical angle here. Inputs and outputs use each instrument’s native values.

## Measurements and fits

Work on zero-based integer recording ticks. The cue uses the 512 Hz event grid. Each mechanical channel is sampled at 64 Hz. The cutoff is the cue tick plus 128 ticks. The last available mechanical sample index is the cutoff tick divided by eight, rounded down. Divide the 32 most recent samples, up to and including that index, into four successive eight-sample bins. The first three columns of a bin are the three channel medians. The last three are the median of its last four samples minus the median of its first four samples for the same channels. Every target is the median of the last eight samples available at the cutoff plus 256, 512 or 768 event ticks, minus the same channel’s cutoff-bin median. The target matrix has three rows of three channels. Reject incomplete or nonfinite required histories or targets. Do not substitute zero for missing data.

The public model receives a source action category and/or a 4-by-6 history according to its selected method. In each bin, subtract the cutoff level from the three median levels and keep the three within-bin changes. The resulting 24 numbers plus the cutoff’s three levels form the motion features. The seven possible cues form seven indicator columns. Fit an intercept with weighted mean squared loss and ridge coefficient 0.20 after training-only centring and population standard deviation scaling of every column. Replace standard deviations below 1e-8 by one for input features. Give each run, then each available task in that run, then each trial equal weight. Learn the nine output coordinates jointly using the same design. Use all three development runs for the final downloadable weights only after evaluating the three fixed folds.

Compare no change, a cue-only fitted readout, motion-only ridge, cue plus motion ridge, and two readouts that also receive the output of the existing frozen 24-wide one-block NeuroTranslate encoder: with and without the cue. For the encoder, scale each of the six types of token coordinate on training bins only, averaging over time bins, then apply the original six-to-24 projection and four-token time encoder from the published shared checkpoint. The cue enters the final readout. For a matched control, train one further cue-and-motion readout after shuffling every participating encoder/projection tensor’s scalar values in sorted name order with PCG64 seed 20495. Keep the shuffled encoder fixed across folds.

## Tests

Report mean absolute error for every instrument and horizon in its native units. To average nine heterogeneous coordinates, divide each absolute error by the corresponding training-fold standard deviation of target change; abstain from the pooled comparison if a target is effectively constant (SD < 1e-8) in any fitting fold. Average over the nine coordinates, then trials within task, tasks within run and finally runs. Show all three run means and task-specific results. No population confidence interval is justified from one participant.

Test cue dependence by comparing the shared cue-and-motion fit with its cues withheld during fitting and prediction. Separately give the same cue-conditioned model each of the six incorrect task labels while retaining the original recorded motion history. Average the six losses, never their forecasts. Compute the wrong-label contrast on identical targets. A consistent cue advantage needs at least a 5% reduction relative to both the no-cue shared fit and the wrong-label result, and lower error than both in every held run. An advantage from the pretrained encoder additionally requires at least a 5% reduction versus the cue-plus-motion ridge and the shuffled encoder, and improvement over each in all three runs. The capability tested is prediction from known task categories and past instrument values in these recordings. No text-only inference of intent, anatomical calibration, biological control, or generalization to other people follows.
