# Does learning from walking improve the standing forecast?

The same muscle and motion predictor can be fitted on standing, walking or both. Does a walking fit help when only a small amount of standing data is available? This is a regularized linear benchmark alongside the separate joint Transformer. Its output is eight surface EMG RMS amplitudes in microvolts for the future 125 ms and 32 positions of the sacrum relative to cutoff in millimetres. The 104 uncompressed output columns minimize independent squared losses in the same feature space; the linear model makes no claim that joint training improves either family. Use the registered source, eligibility and temporal clock. One developmental person's first two sessions were inspected for feasibility. No new multi-person arrays were viewed by this fitter before setting these choices.

Motion features: last position relative to cutoff and change within each of eight consecutive 20-sample bins; recent signed movement over 8, 16, 32 and 64 samples; include the visual cue. Muscle features: the signed mean and log(1+true RMS in µV) for each of eight muscles in each of the eight bins, plus log(1+RMS) separately for the trailing and preceding 32-sample intervals. Use only measured history through the cutoff. The common unadjusted forecast for muscle RMS is its latest 32-sample amplitude. The common baseline for the full path is to repeat the preceding 32-sample path, using the anchored start p(c−32) and past trajectory p(c−31)…p(c). Fitted corrections predict the 8 muscle-amplitude differences directly and the 96 path-coordinate residuals in native units. After adding the muscle correction, bound predicted RMS below by zero (record any pre-bound negatives). Keep trajectory unconstrained. Baseline forecasts include the full persistence pair, last 16-sample velocity extrapolated at each of 32 times, constant position, and trained mean outcomes indexed by posture and cue. Test a linearly changing muscle envelope extending the latest difference between previous and current 32-sample RMS, bounded below at zero. No floor-force/contraction/cortical measurement inferred.

Use participant/posture/direction-balanced training weights with equal event weight within each cell. Fit ridge coefficients of each target on centered, training-standardized features (tiny SD <1e−8 set to one). Use a penalty of 0.1 with weights summing to one and an unpenalized output bias. No hyperparameter search. Fit one model on all qualifying postures with a signed posture indicator and its interaction with the common feature vector; another pooled model lacks posture. Fit movement-only and muscle-only signal controls (with posture, cue and the baseline for each outcome: the past RMS and repeat-path baseline are still available). Fit standalone walking and standing models with the same common design and without the posture column. Report walk-only and stand-only models on BOTH postures, declaring when evaluation is out of their training posture.

For the matched standing adaptation comparison, use just the first one-third of each original standing event log (precomputed block=0) from the eligible standing development participants. The other two-thirds are excluded from BOTH adaptation and matched low-data standing-only fitting. No adaptation to any evaluation individual. Starting from the walking fit, convert its raw slopes to the centered, standardized design of these standing rows exactly. Learn an update to its slopes with the same ridge penalty (0.1) around the converted walking slopes, with an unconstrained new standing-data intercept. The same standing-only fit, identical early rows/normalization/penalty and zero prior on slopes is the comparator. The full-data standing-only fit is the additional strong reference and has more standing training data. Report the three training counts and measured exposure/limitations honestly. Describe a standing improvement only if the adapted model improves BOTH EMG and path over the matched low-data standing-only method, with both paired 97.5-percentile upper bounds <0, on standing from outside the fit. Also report its comparison to full-data standing-only and to frozen walk-only, retaining failures. Never choose individual predictions using future outcomes. This experiment tests a walking prior under a limited amount of standing fitting data; it is distinct from the joint-vs-specialist claim.

Use people with both postures and both directions in each for primary scoring. Keep one-posture support separately for evaluation. Partition the sorted paired development people into five outer folds by index modulo five (min(5,n)); every fold keeps both postures of an individual outside its fitting set. Refit on all paired development people once. Report all models on specified scopes, and the full pooled contextual model with another same-person, same-posture/time-third/direction event's entire muscle history, retaining all original recipient motion, cue, posture and future target. Define the wrong-history control as a changed muscle observation including the derived RMS baseline, with eligible >207-sample distance; also report wrong-history effect retaining the correct recent RMS baseline to distinguish baseline substitution from the learned correction. Use deterministic next eligible chronological event, following the common study. Report 8 per-muscle MAEs, aggregate muscle MAE, mean 3D path error, 3D endpoint error, signed amplitude and endpoint biases; average in the prescribed hierarchy. Paired uncertainty draws resample whole people (seed 1419; 5,000 draws) and the registered joint criteria remain unaltered.
