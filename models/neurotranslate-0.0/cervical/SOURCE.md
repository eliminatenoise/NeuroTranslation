# What eight events show at the neck

The first eight median-nerve events in participant 001’s run 03 gave a mean change of **+1.70225 µV** between electrodes over the sixth cervical and sixth thoracic vertebrae (SC6–TH6). For each event, the mean from 12 to just before 14 ms after the trigger was compared with the mean from −15 to just before −13 ms. The positive sign means the neck-to-back voltage was higher in the later window. Neither a peak nor its latency was measured. [OpenNeuro recording](https://doi.org/10.18112/openneuro.ds004388.v1.0.0)

Nierula and colleagues measured the **N13**, a negative deflection around 13 ms, at each person’s peak in a cleaned, trial-averaged waveform. Across 36 people, their thoracic-reference result was **−0.85 ± 0.05 µV at 13.25 ± 0.18 ms** (group means ± standard error). After correction for stimulus and cardiac artifacts, filtering and trial rejection, their median-nerve averages contained 1,210–2,000 trials per person and used a −110 to −10 ms baseline. Figure 3A shows the cleaned SC6–TH6 trace in red, standardized before group averaging. Figure 2A uses a different reference on the front of the neck. [Table 1](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.3002828.t001) · [Figure 3A](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.3002828.g003) · [Figure 2A](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.3002828.g002) · [Study methods](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.3002828)

The eight-event result preserves the recorded polarity. A changing field at either electrode changes the difference, and the contribution from the heartbeat or nearby muscles is unknown. The study’s evidence for a spinal contribution included the location of the response across the neck and back, which changed with the stimulated limb. One pair of electrodes in one person supplies neither that spatial comparison nor an independent replication sample. The N13 amplitude, its source and any direction of transmission remain unresolved by these eight events. [Figure 3B and 3F](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.3002828.g003) · [Bailey et al., cardiac-noise study using the same dataset](https://doi.org/10.1162/IMAG.a.938)

## Which electrodes formed each voltage?

| Stored channel | Recording reference |
|---|---|
| CP4, scalp EEG | Right mastoid, behind the ear (RM). The authors later used an average reference for cleaned cortical EEG. |
| SC6, neck-surface voltage | TH6, over the sixth thoracic vertebra. For the ventral-reference trace, the authors subtracted the recorded front-of-neck channel AC from the recorded SC6 channel. Both had been recorded against TH6. |
| Biceps, surface recording of left median-nerve activity at the axilla | BicepsN, approximately 3 cm proximal to the active electrode. The channel table labels Biceps as MISC. |
| Thumb, surface muscle recording | Electrode proximal to the recording site over the belly of the abductor pollicis brevis. The Methods give this position. The run metadata omit a separate EMG reference label. |

The four channels share an acquisition clock and have different voltage references. Recording the scalp site Fz against both references allowed the authors to combine scalp and spinal recordings in some participants for a separate brainstem analysis. The authors report that the spinal-reference copy, Fz–TH6, was missing for participants 001–006. It is absent from this run’s channel list.
