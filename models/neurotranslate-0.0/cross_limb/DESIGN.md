# Do simple timing rules carry across studies?

Test a small model with a common set of coefficients across different muscles and marker frames. It has no learned muscle names or study labels, maps each observed muscle through the same rule, and combines only observed channels. It predicts a future 125 ms muscle RMS (in that source's native units) and a 32-point displacement curve (in the source's axes, mm). This complementary baseline is separate from the trainable cross-study Transformer. No new expanded development signals were inspected by this fitter before specifying these methods.

Normalize each available muscle's 20 RMS bins by the root mean square of those 20 values (exact whole-history RMS for equal-length bins). No target data or per-study scale is needed at inference. A zero-scale channel has zero relative inputs, predicts zero, remains eligible for native scoring, and contributes no target to fitting the relative-amplitude rule. Never include missing channels in a fit or error average. The RMS prediction starts with that channel's hold baseline (last four bins). Its correction features are an intercept, all 20 per-channel ratios, 20 within-event averages of the ratios over available channels, and five dimensionless movement descriptors. Predict a dimensionless correction to the hold divided by the channel's past scale. Restore native units and clamp negative RMS values at zero, counting each. With no history signal a positive prediction cannot be identified by this design.

For movement, let motion scale S be four times the root mean square of the norm of the 19 consecutive bin-end position increments. Use increments in their own source frame. Where S<1e−8 mm, predict continued zero change for the learned correction (baseline remains). Fit the target as the residual relative to repeating the last 125 ms trajectory, divided by S. For each event the vector basis uses cutoff-minus-history displacements over the last 1, 2, 4, 8 and 16 bin intervals, divided by S. The five scalar movement descriptors are the norms of those basis vectors. Multiply each basis vector by three scalar features: one, the across-available-muscles average of (last four bins' RMS / respective channel scale), and the corresponding average over the preceding four bins. No unconstrained XYZ intercept. Fit 15 coefficients per future timepoint by weighted penalized least squares, treating the three source coordinates as rows with one-third of the event weight. A change of source-frame axes rotates the output with its own input; no anatomically defined arm axis is equated to a sacral axis. Shared muscle decoder is similarly insensitive to a rigid rotation. Both tasks are separately fitted linear control rules, not an additional multitask neural network. Patch ages are audited in source and exposed; the model uses the explicitly available latest bin-end knot, never an interpolated future observation.

Penalty 0.05 on the mean-weighted ridge problem, including the muscle intercept and all vector basis coefficients. No additional fit-derived coordinate normalization. Equal study weights when pooling; within each study weight people equally, balance posture then cue then events for the leg task and sessions then trials for the arm task. Per-channel regression rows divide each event weight equally among its available, non-silent fit channels; silent or dropped channels are reported. For a single-domain fit use exactly the same optimizer and feature scales. Fit independent full arm and leg coefficients, and a pooled set. The frozen leg fit on arm outcomes and the frozen arm fit on leg outcomes are strict signal-history-only transfers with NO target-domain scales or weights. Also fit each target on the first third in source event order of each development session (block=0). Fit it two ways with identical target rows: ridge toward zero, and a penalty toward the foreign-domain learned slopes (including the dimensionless muscle intercept). These are calibrated, target-supervised transfers. Compare the adapted model to low-data target-only, full target-only and frozen foreign-domain reference. Report actual training counts, no budget/quality tuning from assessment.

Use three synchronized folds by sorted participant ordinal modulo three *within each study* for auxiliary OOF. Do not mix people between train and validation, and refit on all eligible training people once. Test on historically designated people only after Root seals two fitting families and authorizes the assessment reader. The same-person wrong-muscle control rotates histories within source series (upper) or posture/direction/recording third (lower). For lower reject donor cutoffs within 207 samples; for WAY events from distinct lifts are already disjoint original records. Compare each matched correct and wrong prediction on the common set of available channels (>=3 upper); recompute features with that common mask for both. Split out a variant retaining correct recipient RMS persistence baseline while using the donor's learned correction. No across-study pairing. Report per-study native data scores, conditional per-muscle errors and denominators, final endpoint and mean path error, signed biases, historical upper strict-five support even if zero and incomplete. Use 5,000 paired whole-person bootstrap resamples seeded 1641 independently for each domain. Secondary declared calibrated-transfer criterion: adapted-minus-equally limited target-only must have all four one-sided 98.75th-percentile upper bounds below zero over both modalities in both studies before claiming general benefit; otherwise describe domain-specific effects and uncertainty. Three arm test people do not support a population-wide conclusion. Results with no eligible rows are unavailable.
