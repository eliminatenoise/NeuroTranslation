# Can one movement rule follow a new body location?

Take nine positions of a single reflective skin marker, one every 50 milliseconds. Where will it be 200 milliseconds after its last position? The outcome is its change in laboratory x, y and z coordinates, in millimetres. The model receives no marker name, side, anatomical measurements, other marker, body mass, muscle voltage, future gait phase or force. Each prediction concerns one marker at a time.

## Source and split

Use Céline Schreiber and Florent Moissenet’s version 8 overground walking archive (CC BY 4.0), the C2 barefoot 0.4–0.8 m/s condition. The previously fixed roster takes the three lowest available numeric C2 repeats from each of 50 sorted participant directories. The first 30 people supply fitting, the next 10 supply assessment after model sealing, and ten people remain in reserve. Assessment participants also appeared in earlier, distinct walking experiments. The source reconstructed missing positions and filtered the marker trajectories in both directions at 6 Hz. Thus the lead is defined on the stored signals. [Data](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.7734767.v8) · [Study](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-019-0124-4)

Use only six anatomical sites in fitting. On each side they are FAL (outer ankle), FLE (outer knee) and RSP (thumb side of the wrist). Use the identified 52 skin-marker names from the separate stride reference. The primary test uses only the 46 other sites in the designated assessment participants. Score the original six sites separately in those participants. The term transfer refers to anatomical locations withheld from this fit within this study and walking condition. The earlier shared encoder comes from unrelated movement tasks. Its prior exposure or that of other project studies is not a claim of global novelty. Do not open reserve records, use assessment for model selection or retrain after the assessment.

## Coordinates and windows

Use the stored 100 Hz optical clock, millimetres and laboratory axes. For each trial place cutoffs at zero-based frames 40, 50, 60 and so on while frame t+20 exists. History uses t−40, −35, −30, −25, −20, −15, −10, −5 and t. Keep rows where the nine positions and the target position at t+20 each have a nonnegative source residual, finite 3D coordinates, and are not the zero vector. Apply these rules to the sampled endpoints; the intervening frames are not needed. Never require a force plate or muscle channel, and do not consult later observations to choose an input coordinate frame.

Within a window rotate horizontal differences so their first axis follows the net horizontal displacement over the input. The second axis points left relative to that direction and the third points up. Below 1 mm net horizontal movement, use the original laboratory axes. The target and all forecasts are returned to the laboratory axes for evaluation. The basic forecast carries the most recent 50 ms change forward four times. The learned models predict a correction to this vector.

## Models fixed before assessment

Compare zero displacement, the last 50 ms velocity, repeating the previous 200 ms displacement, raw linear ridge, nonlinear Gaussian radial basis ridge and two otherwise identical linear readouts with encoded features. Form four ordered six-number tokens from the two consecutive 50 ms displacement vectors in each 100 ms segment. After a feature-wise training-only scale and centre, project them into the fixed 24-wide, four-head, one-block NeuroTranslate temporal encoder. Use its final normalized token together with the 24 unencoded inputs for one readout. For the matched control permute the scalars within each participating projection and encoder parameter array once, in sorted parameter order, with PCG64 seed 3691. Keep the same input and output scalings, time tokens and fitting opportunity. The raw linear control uses only the 24 inputs. The nonlinear control adds 64 Gaussian features centred on fitting windows drawn without replacement using the training row weights, seed 3692. Use Euclidean distances in the standardized 24-dimensional raw coordinates. Set the squared radial bandwidth to the median positive pairwise squared distance among those centres. Scale and centre each readout column on fitting rows. Replace training standard deviations below 1e−6 by 1. Use weighted squared error on the three velocity-correction components with ridge coefficient 0.01 and an unpenalized intercept. All four fits use the same rows and weight each walker equally, then each available fitting site equally within walker, each available walk equally within site, then each window equally within walk.

## Evaluation and claims

The error per window is the Euclidean length of the difference between predicted and observed displacement vectors, in millimetres. Within each person's site, average windows within walk then walks. Average sites equally within each person and people equally in each of the two named test sets. Report coverage per site, and report the six sites and the other 46 separately. Also report prediction-minus-observation in each laboratory coordinate. Show uncertainty in paired model differences with 10,000 participant bootstrap draws using PCG64 seed 3693. A robust advantage for the frozen encoder requires at least 5% lower average error than each of the velocity, 200 ms repeat, raw ridge, nonlinear ridge and shuffled-encoder controls, a strictly negative upper 95% paired interval for every comparison, and at least eight contributing people in each set. Preserve failed comparisons and scientifically distinct cohorts. An accurate estimate of skin-marker displacement would not, by itself, tell us the muscle force, joint centre or neural cause of that movement.
