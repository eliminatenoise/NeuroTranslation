# Activity, loading and the next pedal reading

This design was fixed before the analyst inspected any Task 1 signal other than the already declared A01 neutral trial 1. Does the earlier electrical signal recorded over the shin improve a forecast of the right load-cell channel 100 milliseconds later, after recent loading and the fixed pedal setting are available? The output is the signed change of the stored conditioner-output channel. It has no verified multiplier to newtons or net joint moment.

Source and split. AnkleImage S-BIAD1482, CC BY 4.0, Task 1 only. Use A01 and A02 for development, A03 and A04 only for assessment after the models, source handling and scoring are sealed. Use trials 1 and 2 at neutral and the distinct angle5 source setting. Verify source identifiers, native paired time and unprocessed EMG/load channels for every file. The file label is context; preserve any unverified physical direction as unknown. No replacements. Repeated subject codes in Task 3 do not imply synchronized measurements across tasks. No ultrasound, input from later samples, healthy-subject physical control, stimulation, or arbitrary action from language is part of these data.

Support. At the original approximate 1 kHz clock select cutoffs i=200,300,400,... so i+100 exists in the same trial. Require continuous monotonic nominal 1 ms steps across i−200 through i+100 (absolute spacing error at most 1e−8 s), finite load and EMG throughout the 201 past samples, and finite target load. Do not interpolate or fill from other channels. Change y = load[i+100] − load[i]. All comparisons use common physical source-qualified support. Forecast target intervals share boundary samples; overlapping histories and trials are not independent people. The primary score weights the two people equally, the two conditions equally within a person, the two trials equally within each condition, and all admitted windows equally within a trial.

Baselines and causal electrical model. Compare unchanged load, repeat its last 100 ms change, and a training-only weighted median change for the current pedal setting. Also compare a fixed first-order response: over the next 100 ms, load moves a fraction 1−exp(−100/150) toward an estimated equilibrium. The 150 ms constant is a modelling choice. Fit the equilibrium through weighted ridge on the training target divided by that fraction plus current load. Inputs are the source setting alone for one fit, then setting, last fast and slow rectified EMG envelopes, and their respective changes over the past 100 ms for another fit. The full electrical history is rectified first; fast and slow envelopes are initialized to the first rectified sample and updated at each 1 ms sample with 1−exp(−1/25) and 1−exp(−1/80). All inputs stop at the forecast cutoff. These envelope and load relaxation constants are chosen filters, not recovered physiological constants or a muscle-force simulation.

Shared movement encoder. Each history has four 50 ms bins, each bounded by 51 original values including endpoints, with only the boundary shared. Per bin use the final raw load, end minus start load, mean load minus the midpoint of the two endpoint values, fast and slow EMG envelopes at its end, and mean rectified EMG over the 51 samples. Standardize the six features with balanced training-only means and standard deviations over bins and windows; SD below 1e−8 becomes one. For no-EMG models set the final three standardized columns to zero. Flatten the standardized bins and append the recent 100 ms load change and the binary setting. In the explicit context-ablation control mask the setting before output training, otherwise include it. Encoder models append the last hidden vector from the original 24-wide frozen shared movement Transformer; the linear controls omit it. The random-encoder control uses the same original initialization recipe and fixed seed 20261121. Original checkpoint SHA256 6a05e06ecbf0eadb4524369d9089847813763d0d2a3c1a49bdd1e4bf295e4864. Fit a linear correction to the recent load change with unpenalized intercept, weighted training-only feature standardization, and fixed ridge penalty 0.05. Use the same standardized ridge rule for the two equilibrium fits. Keep all outputs unbounded in recorded units and report bad numerical values.

Methods. Evaluate hold, recent, setting_median, relaxation_setting, relaxation_emg, linear_load, linear_both, shared_load_no_setting, shared_load, shared_both, random_both. For the wrong-EMG diagnostic, score the same shared_both fit, substituting the other entire EMG history from the other trial, same person, same setting and same normalized temporal position (donor index floor(rank × n_other / n_recipient)). Keep the recipient load and context. Recompute both EMG envelopes and all derived inputs. This matching uses both completed trial lengths and is retrospective. Do not use this control as an online prediction or per-sample physiological trace.

Fit two leave-one-person-out development folds and then train on both development people once. Score untouched reserved recordings with frozen weights. Report balanced MAE, RMSE, signed error, separate setting summaries, same-fit wrong EMG, original-transfer/random/linear comparisons and paired gains per person without a participant identifier. The practical matched-EMG criterion requires at least 5% lower balanced MAE than both shared_load and wrong_both, and positive gain over both in EACH of the two assessment people. Report head-to-head against the best simple and direct linear comparisons separately. Two people cannot establish a general population effect; no inferential significance threshold, endpoint rescue, or outcome-based settings switch. If any planned person or condition lacks a verified endpoint, preserve the incomplete test and disclose only defensible descriptive results.
