# Can early scalp readings correct an arm forecast?

The starting forecast is the typical recorded response to each of seven right-arm visual cues. The earlier group study fitted those nine cue-dependent values on seven people. Here we retain them exactly, then ask if early scalp readings reduce the errors that remain when past arm motion and measurements near the eyes are already available. The tests concern instrument readings. They do not establish the physiological origin of a scalp signal.

## Recording and separation

Use the Ofner et al. public execution recordings, Zenodo 834976. Use the first three execution runs from S1–S7 for fitting. The first three runs from S8–S12 were used in a prior mechanical experiment. Their fourth runs are the prespecified fresh assessment files and may not be accessed until the present models, criteria, scoring code and the separate trained-Transformer study are sealed. No outcome-based substitutions. S13–S15 remain unopened. No imagery data. Prior single-person results and the training-only scalp–eye qualification were known when this protocol was written. [Original study](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0182578) · [Recordings](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.834976)

The cue event is one of codes 0x600 through 0x606. Require a unique matching end event through the next cue and enough time to measure the last target. Keep eligible trials regardless of their response direction or size. Require finite samples in every required interval for every input and target, on identical primary support across methods. Document any omission. Do not reject a stationary movement or a finite electrical outlier.

The cutoff is 128 event ticks (0.25 seconds) after the cue at 512 Hz. Arm inputs and targets use the preceding group model’s timing. Channels 93, 94 and 68 are the elbow, forearm-rotation and index-near glove readings. The final 32 mechanical samples on the 64 Hz grid ending at the cutoff form four successive eight-sample bins, each with three medians and three changes (last four median minus first four median). Changes half a second, one second and one and a half seconds after the cutoff equal the corresponding future eight-sample median minus the last input median. Require at least two distinct finite values per mechanical channel somewhere in the recording, as in the group experiment. This file-level integrity check does not demonstrate live performance.

Scalp sites are FC3, FC4, C3, C4, CP3 and CP4 in that order (channels 13, 17, 28, 32, 43 and 47). Eye inputs are channels 62, 63 and 64, by index: the left/right descriptions disagree across some files and the codebook. Average native electrical samples in the four inclusive event-relative tick intervals [−127,−64], [−63,0], [1,64] and [65,128]. Within each trial and channel subtract the average of its first two bin values from all four. This reference includes the cue tick. Do not filter, interpolate or use post-cutoff samples. Earlier source checks found common trial-specific variation in scalp and eye recordings. Neither instrument has verified physical units in these files.

## Fitted corrections

Keep the original cue median, shared 24-wide four-token encoder, and six-coordinate mechanical token scaling from the published seven-person checkpoint. Subtract cutoff levels from each bin’s three median levels and retain its within-bin changes. The mechanical correction receives the flattened 24 motion coordinates and the original final encoder token, with no absolute starting levels. All learned predictions add their corrections to the frozen cue median. Compare:

- The unadjusted cue median and the previous shared cue-and-motion model, both frozen. Also report no change.
- A mechanical correction; the same design with six scalp sites; and the same design with three eye channels.
- A mechanical correction using the part of the scalp signal left after a training-only linear regression on the eye channels.
- A joint model using mechanical features, the three eye channels and that residual scalp signal.

For the ocular regression, use all fitting bins, centre and scale the three eye and six scalp columns using training weights, and fit the six standardized scalp columns from the three standardized eye columns by ridge regression (penalty 0.10, unpenalized mean). The residual is actual standardized scalp minus that fitted ocular projection. For each forecast correction, use the same eligible trials and the same loss. Standardize design columns with weighted training means and population standard deviations, replacing SD below 1e-8 by one. Divide each target error against the cue median by its fixed original group training target SD. Fit a separate Huber regression for each of the nine outputs, threshold 0.25 in those scaled units, ridge coefficient 0.05, zero intercept after design centring. Use iteratively reweighted least squares, initialized at zero, up to 100 iterations and a max coefficient change below 1e-10. No model selection or assessment-based tuning. Give equal weight to each person, each run within person, each cue within run and each trial within cue.

## Evaluation fixed before assessment

Score native mean absolute error per instrument and horizon and an equal-coordinate mean absolute error standardized by the original group fitting target SD. Retain person/run/cue balancing for the held scores. Report the entire eligible sample, coverage and per-cue summaries. All primary comparisons use the same observations. Report paired whole-person mean differences, the number improved, and 95% percentile intervals from 10,000 draws of participant indices with replacement (PCG64 seed 66381). At least four participants are required for an advantage claim; intervals from five people are descriptive and unstable.

For the scalp-only correction, replace scalp readings with those of the next eligible repeat of the same cue in the same run and participant, wrapping once; the original arm history and target remain. For the joint model, substitute the donor’s scalp residual calculated from that donor’s own eye and scalp readings, leaving the recipient's explicit eye and arm features intact. Do not use a trial as its own control. Compare aligned and shifted joint predictions on identical paired support. The practical scalp-information criterion requires the raw-scalp correction to reduce error by at least 5% versus mechanical correction, original cue median and shifted scalp. The more specific conditional criterion requires the joint model to do the same versus eye-only correction, original cue median and shifted residuals. Each criterion must have a strictly negative upper paired interval and benefit a strict majority for every required comparison. A failed test remains failed even if one comparison or one sensor improves.

As a prespecified sensitivity check, use the strictly pre-cue electrical samples at ticks [−128,−1] to calculate each channel's within-trial population SD. Mark a trial if any of the nine SDs exceeds that channel’s 99th percentile over fitting trials (NumPy linear quantile, no held-set fitting). Report identical-support scores for the remaining rows, including a paired wrong-trial subset requiring both trials to pass the screen. This check is descriptive and cannot confer a primary success. Detailed traces and individual model corrections remain local; publish fitted weights and cohort comparisons with attribution.
