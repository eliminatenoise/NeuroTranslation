# Did voltages from the back improve the forecast?

The task-trained Transformer and the shared movement encoder each received a tenth of a second of motion, the standing or walking instruction, and the voltage features allowed by that fit. The target is the unsigned angle between the strapped-on sensor's orientation at the cutoff and 100 ms later. The source stores 2,000 samples per second. The orientation unit updates internally at about 100 Hz.

Four people supplied the development data. Both model families had fixed weights, preprocessing, scoring rules and controls before records from four other people were opened. Both trials for one of them labeled the four orientation channels as components 2, 3, 4 and a buffer. Without a verified first component there is no measured rotation target for that person. Neither four-person test can be evaluated. The remaining three people supplied six trials and 1,152 standing and 1,152 walking windows across the group. These windows share recordings and people. The person is the unit of comparison.

| Three people with verified orientation labels | Motion alone | Both voltage grids | Unrelated voltage |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Task-trained Transformer | 0.361° | 0.382° | 0.381° |
| Shared movement encoder | 0.376° | 0.385° | 0.385° |

**Specialist Transformer.** Combine each person's trials within each task, calculate the mean absolute error per task, then average over tasks and people with equal weight. The full fit had to beat the best motion-only or simpler forecast by at least 5% and the unrelated-voltage control by at least 2%. It also had to beat both the separately trained motion-only model and the unrelated-voltage control in at least three people, with no increase in either task's mean error against either control. These conditions apply to the original four-person test. The follow-up reports the three eligible people's errors separately. The four-person development report includes the prespecified regional comparisons and descriptive paired intervals. [Fixed design](protocol.json) · [All assessment results](assessment.json) · [Earlier development results](development.json)

**Shared movement encoder.** Give each trial equal weight within person and task, then weight tasks and people equally. All voltage and motion features come from four 25 ms bins. The electrode control replaces both regions with readings from the same task in the person's other trial. The full model had to reduce error by at least 5% against both motion alone and unrelated voltage, improve against both in at least three people and avoid increasing either task's error against motion alone. The three-person comparison cannot decide the original four-person test. The shared checkpoint is identical to the one used for earlier movement forecasts. The readouts and associated transformations were fitted to the development recordings.
