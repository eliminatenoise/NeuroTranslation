# Can a decoder follow a finger it has never seen move in the evaluation trials?

A word on a screen names a finger. The participant chooses when to bend it. A glove records how far its sensor reading changes, and clinical electrodes record voltage at the brain surface. The word alone gives a model a considerable head start. The question is whether the current electrical recording adds information about movement, including differences between two repetitions of the same instruction.

## Who contributes what

Use the five previously designated Stanford FingerFlex development recordings bp, cc, ht, jc and jp, in that order. Plan one leave-one-person-out fit for each eligible person. Each person's early recording supplies calibration for that person only. Each held person's later eligible targets enter scoring only. The other eligible people's later trials train that fold. No fitted decoder, pretrained representation, calibration choice or trial selection can use the held person's scored targets. This tests transfer with a short voltage **and glove** calibration. It does not establish zero-calibration transfer. One person, bp, contributed to the earlier, single-person design. Reuse only that study's old inner-fit interval [0,236000) for bp. All its old inner assessment and everything beyond stay closed here. The choice of study design has prior exposure to bp, so even its held fold is development evidence.

For a recording of N nominal 1 kHz rows the maximum permitted prefix ends at 40 floor(3N/200) − 4000, exclusive. Use the smaller bp limit above. No other people, later 40% or guard rows are eligible. Calibrate inside [0,80000), drop [80000,88000), use [88000,prefix end) for whole trials. Exclude trials touching a truncated phase or crossing a boundary. An eligible trial contains a complete positive named-digit run immediately followed by a complete zero-cue run with an observed end. Digits 1–5 mean thumb, index, middle, ring, little. Exclude the first run of the file even if positive. At each phase, anchor on the first inclusive nominal 25 Hz grid tick at or after its onset. Use offsets 9 through 42 inclusive from that anchor. Require all four past voltage windows and all four future glove samples inside the phase, otherwise drop the whole trial. Both phases then have 34 cutoffs. Use one occurrence of each trial. Duplicated zero-order-held millisecond rows cannot supply extra observations.

At each cutoff t, take glove samples at t+40, +80, +120 and +160 ms, compute three absolute first differences in floating point and average them per digit. A repeated sensor reading contributes zero. Units are sensor counts per nominal 40 ms step. The target is unsigned. The nominal timestamps come from the shared release grid. Individual hardware timestamps, lag correction, physical joint angles and anatomical laterality transfer are unresolved. Source verification must establish five columns, the nominal original sampling grid and valid source electrode labels. Report any incompatible recording and reason before fitting. Never substitute a person or change the grid to improve performance. The original publication describes glove measurement for participants 1–8, while the task documentation identifies jc as participant 9. Prospectively exclude any of the five without a verified aligned five-digit glove, five supported cues, documented voltage and contact map, at least ten complete calibration trials, or at least one matched quartet. Report the source-specific reason and original denominator. Fit leaving out each eligible person if at least three remain. If fewer than five are eligible, the five-person performance claim is unavailable. The same descriptive gain criterion may be applied to the named eligible subset only. Fewer than six quartets spanning at least three cues in any eligible person prevents a gain claim but does not erase their data.

## How one person's electrodes can be compared with another's

Use release region codes in the fixed order 1 (dorsal primary motor), 2 (rolandic in the depositor's processing script; the task README omits it), 3 (dorsal primary somatosensory), 4 (ventral sensorimotor), 6 (other frontal), 7 (other parietal), 8 (temporal), 9 (occipital). Align groups by their code. A column number has meaning only in its original recording. Unknown and undocumented codes, including 0, contribute to the common reference and one unknown-present indicator, but supply no anatomical power feature.

At each of t−120, −80, −40 and t ms use the 160 preceding 1 kHz samples, ending inclusively. Convert counts to microvolts with factor 0.0298, subtract the instantaneous mean over all released contacts, demean each window/contact, and apply a symmetric Hann taper. For each interior positive FFT bin use squared magnitude × 2 / (160 × sum of squared taper). Sum the bins in 12–32 and 70–150 Hz inclusive, omitting 113.75–126.25 Hz in the high band. Take log(1+power/1 µV²). For each contact and band, subtract that person's calibration mean and divide by its population standard deviation (values below 1e−8 use 1). Compute these moments on the unique voltage token endpoints used by complete calibration trials, giving each endpoint one vote. Average the standardized contacts in each region separately. Write zeros for an absent region, append eight binary presence indicators and one unknown-present indicator. The 25 cortical values are eight low-band means, eight high-band means and nine flags. Masks can expose differences in clinical coverage. The program never uses geometry or assumes equal grids across people.

For each digit divide the movement target by max(1 sensor count, RMS movement target across all 68 cutoffs of every complete calibration trial). No target centering. These five fixed gains put movements on comparable within-person scales. They can convert an estimate back to that person's sensor units. No updates after calibration. The decoder takes cue and/or regional voltage features only. It receives no past or future glove values, target spread, patient identifier or channel count. Applying the five output gains requires the person's saved calibration.

## Models and comparisons

Encode digit × display/blank as ten cells with six numbers in the active cell: 1, elapsed seconds since phase onset, and positive-part hinges at 0.4, 0.8, 1.2 and 1.6 seconds. Use the four true token times. The blank retains the preceding named finger. Concatenate the 60 cue values and 25 cortical features. For each person and cue, the first eligible post-guard trial is donor only. Every subsequent trial is a recipient, with its previous complete eligible same-cue trial as the wrong cortical donor at matched phase and tick. Preserve the real current cue values. All comparison arms score the same recipients. No wrap-around or cross-person donor.

Fit separate ridge and Transformer models with cue alone, cortex alone, and their combination. Give people equal total weight, trials equal weight within person, each of 68 cutoffs equal weight in a trial and each digit equal weight in the loss. Ridge flattens available tokens, standardizes columns with training-person-weighted mean and population SD (SD < 1e−8 uses 1), uses an unpenalized intercept and penalty 0.05 in the mean squared objective. A further strong cue control predicts the training mean for the named cue, phase and exact tick, averaging available people's individual cell means equally. A missing cell falls back to the equally person-weighted overall five-digit mean. This control can learn a different 68-point profile for every named finger, without electrical inputs.

The Transformer standardizes available columns over training tokens with the same person weights. Unused modalities are zero after scaling. Project 85 inputs to width 16, add four learned position vectors initialized at zero, then apply one 2-head post-norm encoder block, width 32 GELU feed-forward layer, zero dropout. Read five normalized movements from the last token. Train in CPU float32 with seed 319 for every fit, AdamW at 0.001, weight decay 0.01, eight epochs, deterministically shuffled pooled rows, batches of 512 including the final partial batch. Multiply each per-row loss by total rows / (number of people × that person's rows) before averaging the batch. Use the final epoch. All readouts floor predictions at zero before evaluation. No architecture search, early stopping or alternate target. Train a separate all-eligible-person deployment fit of the three arms in each family and the cue table only after completed folds. Its weights support reproduction and examples. The fit has no new evaluation set.

## What counts as evidence

For each held person compute the mean absolute error in that person's calibration units, averaging equally over digits, cutoffs and complete recipient trials. Also report per-person, per-digit and raw-count errors. For each model family calculate comparator error minus aligned error against its cue-only fit, the exact-tick cue table, the aligned fit receiving the wrong donor, and the cortex-only fit. Report ridge aligned minus Transformer aligned. The primary proposed gain concerns the Transformer versus its cue-only fit, cue table and wrong donor.

A second comparison asks whether the model captures differences between two movements made under the same instruction. Within each held person and cue, partition the chronological complete eligible trials into disjoint groups of four. Trials 2 and 4 of each quartet are recipients; trials 1 and 3 supply their respective wrong donors. No source trial appears in two quartets. Subtract the first recipient's 68 × 5 observations from the second's at matched phase and tick, do the same for predictions, then measure absolute difference error. Compare aligned predictions with zero difference, the actual cue-only model's difference and the wrong-donor difference. Average equally over quartets within each person and people. The table produces exactly zero difference here. Report support by cue, leftover trials and digit results. All people contribute once to each primary and conditional contrast.

For each contrast present its individual person means, overall equal-person mean and a descriptive 95% percentile interval over all n^n ordered resamples of the n eligible people with replacement. A person is the unit. Folds share training data, and five clinical participants limit population inference. The interval describes dispersion under person resampling and is not a validated population confidence interval. Declare provisional positive transfer only if every eligible person meets the support requirement and shows strictly positive gains for **each** of the three primary and three repeat comparisons, with positive lower interval bounds for all six contrasts. Apply the same criteria to ridge separately. An average improvement alone cannot pass. Report negative or mixed findings without changing these rules. No claim about clinical utility, conscious intent, physical joint angles, neural causation or external people follows.

Release only code, group and person-level summaries, all-eligible model weights, an invented example and source identification. Keep clinical voltages, glove values, dates, coordinates, per-trial features and participant-specific calibration arrays out of the public package. Obtain the original licensed recordings separately.
