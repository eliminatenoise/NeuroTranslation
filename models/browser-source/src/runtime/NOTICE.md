# Source credits

Jorge German Perez Blanco and Javier Mauricio Anteliz Ortiz published the **EEG-EMG-WristMotion Dataset**, version 3. Their encoders and electrical recordings come from cued, constrained right-wrist pointing. We used the six named participants, formed sixteen bins of earlier wrist angles and voltages, calculated four bipolar forearm amplitudes, and fitted and compared angular forecasts. The source, derived training scales, fitted weights and numerical results carry the original attribution and CC BY 4.0 terms wherever applicable. The publisher hosts the original recordings. [Version 3 deposit](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.29666735.v3) · [Study descriptor](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-026-07287-z) · [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)

Xin Wang, Taein Kwon, Mahdi Rad, Bowen Pan, Ishani Chakraborty, Sean Andrist, Dan Bohus, Ashley Feniello, Bugra Tekin, Felipe Vieira Frujeri, Neel Joshi and Marc Pollefeys published *HoloAssist*, ICCV 2023. The original spatial study converted wrist tracking to centimetres and used successive positions to calculate distances, elapsed times and changes in direction. HoloAssist's data carry the Community Data License Agreement – Permissive 2.0. [Dataset](https://holoassist.github.io/) · [Included agreement](CDLA-Permissive-2.0.txt)

Justin Shin and Shantanu Jadhav published *Single Day W-Track Learning*, DANDI version 0.240511.0307 (2024), under CC BY 4.0. The spatial study used tracked positions from JS14, session 01, running epoch 5, to derive movement histories and future distances. [Versioned dataset](https://doi.org/10.48324/dandi.000978/0.240511.0307) · [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)

The spatial weights are redistributed under CC BY 4.0. Their numerical values are preserved in the new checkpoint. For this browser edition, NeuroTranslation packed the original float32 parameter and float64 scale bytes into the JavaScript predictor. It computes each forecast locally using the shared encoder and the selected fitted input and output weights. The wrist examples are invented. The labelled rat examples use recorded W-track observations. Keep the source credits, accompanying CDLA agreement and model attribution with redistributed copies. [Detailed spatial-data credits](DATA-CREDITS.md) · [Tensor inventory](model.json)

The two optional angular corrections use the same WristMotion source. NeuroTranslation fitted one additive correction to the earlier angles and a second to those angles plus the forearm signals. The downloadable coefficients and scales come from the pooled six-person fit. Fifteen additional four-person base fits supported the separate development comparison. This edition carries the six new arrays in their original 64-bit representation. The wrist-derived coefficients, scales and reported errors retain the source attribution and CC BY 4.0 terms above. The forearm example uses invented curves. [Example](forearm-example.json) · [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)

The population readout uses five camera frames and four chronological pairs of dorsal CA1 and prefrontal spike counts from the Shin and Jadhav recordings. NeuroTranslation grouped spikes into quarter-second bins, normalized by recorded units, trained movement-only and spike-conditioned readouts on ER1, JS15 and JS17, and compared them on JS21, JS34 and KL8. The JS21 example is a measured excerpt. The derived data, fitted coefficients and reported results carry CC BY 4.0 attribution. The browser includes the eight fitted population arrays in their original float64 representation. [Versioned dataset](https://doi.org/10.48324/dandi.000978/0.240511.0307) · [Licence](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)

Rateb Katmah, Aamna AlShehhi, Doua Kosaji, Nour Al-Rahmani, Muhammad Abdullah, Abdul Aziz Vaqar Hulleck and Kinda Khalaf (2026), *A multimodal gait dataset of brain activity, muscle activity, kinematics and ground forces in young adults*, version 1.0.0, PhysioNet. NeuroTranslation used the reconstructed right and left knee angles from the 0.5 m/s walk to fit three corrections to a prior-stride forecast.
