# Two papers, each written twice

Each paper has a Technical edition, for readers who want the method, and a Simple English edition, which explains the same things in ordinary words and takes more pages to do it.

| | Technical | Simple English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *NeuroTranslation: A New Paradigm In Brain-Body Mapping.* | [PDF, 153 pages](current/neurotranslation-technical.pdf) | [PDF, 188 pages](current/neurotranslation-explained.pdf) |
| *Brain2Body: The Next Generation In Body-Brain Interfacing.* | [PDF, 136 pages](current/brain2body-article.pdf) | [PDF, 152 pages](current/brain2body-simple-english.pdf) |

**NeuroTranslation** asks what a recording adds once a model already knows the movement. It reports a macaque study in which two networks lowered the average error and still failed the pass rule set for them, and 19 of the 25 studies on people that are listed on [the studies page](../benchmarks/README.md). Then it proposes the larger model and the recording study that would test it.

**Brain2Body** starts somewhere else: let a computer guide a hand through a tune, then stop the guidance. What can the person play now? The paper is a proposal for telling help from learning. It reports no experiment on that question.

## Work published alongside the drafts

*How precisely do words identify a recorded movement?* is a four-page supplement, written to go with the NeuroTranslation paper but not part of it: neither paper's current draft contains it. It reports two studies in which a person wrote a sentence after watching a movement, and a model had to find that movement among recordings, one of the whole body and one of the hand. It explains why the timing of an action can be easier to find than the shape of the hand, and which question about guiding a hand, the subject of the Brain2Body paper, the studies leave open. [Read it online](https://eliminatenoise.github.io/NeuroTranslation/papers/language-and-observed-movement/language-and-movement.html) · [Download the four-page PDF](../docs/papers/language-and-observed-movement/language-and-movement.pdf) · [Editable source and evidence](../docs/papers/language-and-observed-movement/)

Two newer forecasting studies are not in the drafts either. Each asks what a forecast gains from information available before the movement, against the body's own recent movement: [an instructor's finished words and the wrist one second later](../benchmarks/preceding-instructions/), and [earlier brain-cell firing and a rat's position a quarter of a second later](../benchmarks/wtrack-neural-motion/). In both, the extra information did not help. [The studies page](../benchmarks/README.md) lists the studies published after its numbered table.

Nor is the general model's newest wrist result. Its forecast of the two wrist angles was given a correction from four forearm signals, scored on six people the wrist studies had already used, each in turn by a version fitted on the other five: the error fell from 4.175° with a correction from the wrist's movement alone to 4.054° with the forearm signals, and an earlier model made for the wrist still did better (3.549°). A figure shows these errors with the controls, in which the forearm signals come from the wrong trial or run backwards. [Comparison figure (PDF)](../models/figures/wrist-correction.pdf) · [Methods, paired comparisons and source](../models/neurotranslate-0.0/forearm/)

A later experiment gives two words a movement of their own. Enter **walk** or **run** and the model generates a short stride from that word alone. On two held-out walks and one run, the average error of its body points was 89.82 mm, against 89.88 mm for the average recorded action from training. The experiment appeared after these drafts. [Generate a stride](https://eliminatenoise.github.io/NeuroTranslation/word-motion/) · [Recordings, method and results](../models/word-motion/)

A two-page supplement asks where the body goes next. One experiment follows a human stride for 600 milliseconds. The other uses the movement and brain-cell firing of a rat to forecast how far it will move in a quarter of a second. The walk or run label helped locate body points relative to the waist. Correctly timed firing improved on both comparisons in only one of three rats. These results came after the two paper drafts. [Read the supplement](https://eliminatenoise.github.io/NeuroTranslation/papers/forecasting-movement/) · [Download the two-page PDF](../docs/papers/forecasting-movement/movement-forecast.pdf) · [Editable source and figures](../docs/papers/forecasting-movement/)

A four-page supplement follows two treadmill experiments. The first predicts where the knees were 15 stored frames later. The second asks whether leg-muscle and knee-angle recordings agree on a walking rhythm when the clocks cannot establish the timing of individual steps. [Read the supplement](https://eliminatenoise.github.io/NeuroTranslation/papers/walking-rhythm/) · [Download the PDF](../docs/papers/walking-rhythm/gait-clocks.pdf) · [Editable source and results](../docs/papers/walking-rhythm/)

## Nobody outside the project has reviewed them

"Working Draft — Review Pending" is printed on each title page, and it is true.

The four files were last built on 19 November 2026. Where a paper and a results file disagree, the results file is right: [the studies page](../benchmarks/README.md) is written from the results files.

## Source, checksums and licence

- **Source.** [One ZIP](downloads/NeuroTranslation-Brain2Body-Papers-Source.zip) of 77 LaTeX and BibTeX files. It is not yet enough to rebuild the PDFs: the figures and two input files are missing from it, and its own README names them. The manifest inside the ZIP names two folders by the paths they had before the papers moved: `papers/experimental/cemhsey-pinch-carry-2026-11-19` is now `current/`, and `papers/experimental/r11-source` is only in the repository's history.
- **Checksums.** [`current.json`](current.json) names the edition and gives the checksum of each ZIP ([papers](downloads/NeuroTranslation-Brain2Body-Papers.zip.sha256), [source](downloads/NeuroTranslation-Brain2Body-Papers-Source.zip.sha256)). [`current/manifest.json`](current/manifest.json) gives the checksum and page count of each PDF.
- **Licence.** The project's own text is dedicated to the public domain under CC0 1.0. The fonts inside the PDFs and the recordings behind the studies have their own terms: [fonts and paper notices](current/NOTICE.md), [dataset credits](../docs/RIGHTS.md).

Each new edition replaces these files in place. Earlier editions are in the repository's history.
