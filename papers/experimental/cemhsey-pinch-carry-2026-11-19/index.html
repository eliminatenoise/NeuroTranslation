Does yesterday’s pinch correction travel?

Four people returned for another day of pinch-force recordings. At 50% of maximum force, yesterday’s five-grid correction beat a fresh practice squeeze for three people overall. It improved the forecast for all four as the cue rose and worsened it for all four during release. The current gauge reading plus the upcoming cue change gave the smallest average miss.

What changed on the second day?

Four adults performed the same pinch on the next recording day. At each of three targets, only their second squeeze supplied scores. The two models could use one correction from the preceding day or fit a new one from that day’s first squeeze. At 50%, adding the upcoming cue change to the current gauge reading gave the smallest average miss, 0.005835579 of same-day maximum pinch force. It beat the carried five-grid model for three of four people. Within the five-grid model, carry reduced the average miss by 0.000022612 against target adjustment alone and by 0.000006520 against a fresh correction. Three people improved in each comparison. Against fresh practice, carrying the five-grid correction cut the average miss during the rise by 0.000736032 and increased it during release by 0.000698594. All four showed both directions. In the force-history model, carry reduced the miss against target adjustment alone by 0.000018664 on average, although just one person improved. At 10%, both carried models beat their target-adjusted counterparts in all four people. At 30%, where adjusting to the original target changes nothing, carry increased average error in both models. The earlier day’s results were known. The 12 second repeats describe these four people and one grasp. The forecasts ran offline and clock alignment and live latency were unmeasured.
