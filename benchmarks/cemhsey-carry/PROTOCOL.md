# Does yesterday’s pinch correction work today?

Ask someone to follow the same force cue on two recording days. On the first day, one practice pinch lets us estimate a correction to the forecast. On the next day we can reuse it, take another practice pinch, or skip the correction altogether. Which choice comes closest to the gauge? Each forecast gets exactly the same second pinch to answer that question.

## Two recording days, one force gauge

The CEMHSEY GRASP study recorded adults pressing the pads of two digits against a P200 dynamometer. The gauge measures the force between the digits. Each day’s released force is scaled to that person’s maximum voluntary contraction (MVC) for the grasp that day. The forearm grids record surface electromyography (EMG), electrical activity measured through the skin. We compare forecasts of the gauge, in fractions of same-day MVC. Identical fractions on two days can correspond to different physical forces. ## Eleven forecasts of one reading

The unchanged point models use the most recent force history and the scheduled phase. One model also uses forearm summaries and contrasts across the recorded EMG grid. Issue a forecast at force ticks 60, 70, …, 5970 and score the gauge 24 ticks later, a nominal lead of 120 ms at 200 Hz. Force features end at the issue tick. EMG windows cover [i−60,i−40) and [i−40,i−20) on the force clock, with boundary b mapped to ceil(2048b/200) on the EMG clock. Both released records start at nominal index zero. These clocks give no measured online latency, physiological delay or synchronization error bound. [Frozen point models and timing](https://github.com/eliminatenoise/NeuroTranslation/tree/d66fc10251fb3e04e6dcd8a9d2cda8c927ade408)

At target q = 0.10, 0.30 or 0.50, let g be the unit cue. It starts at zero, rises linearly from tick 1000 to 2000, holds at one through 4000, then falls linearly to zero at 5000 and stays there. Write u = g(i+24) − g(i). The upcoming cue change is known at forecast time. The controls use current force f[i], the future scheduled cue q·g(i+24), and current force plus cue change f[i] + q·u.

For each point model keep its unmodified forecast p₀. Target adjustment gives p = p₀ + (q−0.30)·u. Carrying the practice correction forward gives p + b₉·u. Practising again gives p + b₁₀·u. Each b comes from one first repeat for this person, target and model, using sum(u·(y−p))/sum(u²), where y is the gauge reading at i+24. Use the original unrounded b₉. Fit b₁₀ only on the new first repeat and hold it fixed. Fit neither an intercept nor new point-model weights. Use every issue and leave the forecast unclipped. The 30% target makes p and p₀ identical. Either practice correction can still change the forecast where u differs from zero.

## Who gained, by how much, and when?

At 50% MVC, the primary outcome is mean absolute error (MAE) across the whole second repeat. For each model ask whether yesterday’s correction beats target adjustment alone, then whether it beats a fresh correction. The 10% trial is the companion comparison. At 30% check the target-adjustment identity exactly. Report the complete set even when the forecasts lose to a simple control.

Compute MAE, root mean squared error (RMSE) and mean signed error (forecast minus gauge) in fractions of MVC for each participant. Divide MAE by q for error as a fraction of the prescribed target. Average each metric over four people with equal weight. Repeat for each target and each phase by issue tick. The half-open phases use edges 0, 1000, 2000, 4000, 5000, 6000 for initial rest, ramp up, hold, release and final rest. The counts per trial are 94, 100, 200, 100 and 98, giving 592 overall. Keep issues whose forecast crosses a phase edge in the phase where it was issued. The second repeats supply 2,368 issues per target and 7,104 in total.

For each model, compare carried with adjusted, carried with same-day, same-day with adjusted, and adjusted with frozen. Compare each of adjusted, carried and same-day with current force plus cue change. At each of these three correction levels compare the grid model with the force-history model. These 17 comparisons accompany all eleven arm summaries at every target and phase (overall included). Subtract the candidate’s MAE from its comparator’s MAE within each person. Positive favours the candidate. Report the mean, observed minimum and maximum of the four unrounded differences, plus counts of strictly positive, exactly zero and strictly negative differences. Round published numerical summaries to nine decimal places only after computing signs and aggregates.

Overlapping issues share many of the same measurements. Four people on one pair of indexed days give four paired differences per comparison. Their range describes what happened to those four people. The comparison measures offline transfer of a task-specific correction on one grasp. Real-time operation, transfer to another population and clinical or causal effects would each need evidence of their own. Release only independently reproduced aggregate scores, counts and contrasts. Individual measurements, participant identities, forecasts, fitted weights, coefficients and person-level scores stay private.
