# Reusing a pinch correction on another day

How long does one practice pinch buy you? The earlier study fitted a correction on the first of two attempts, then checked it on the second. Here we bring that correction back on the next indexed recording day. The forearm electrodes and the gauge measure a new session. The target on the screen follows the same schedule.

At each of three force targets, the earlier correction faces two alternatives. We can fit it again from the new day’s first pinch, or use the scheduled cue without a personal adjustment. All eleven forecasts face the same second pinch. We compare them first at 50% of that day’s maximum voluntary contraction. [Prespecified methods](PROTOCOL.md) · [Study design](DESIGN.json) · [Acquisition study](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-025-05733-y)

## The second attempt on the new day

At 50% of maximum force, the five-grid forecast made a mean absolute error of 0.70578 percentage points of MVC with the earlier correction. Fitting a fresh correction gave 0.70643. Across the four people, the paired mean gain from reusing the earlier correction was +0.00065 percentage points, with individual gains from −0.00249 to +0.00391. In person-by-person comparisons, the earlier correction had 3 lower, 0 equal, 1 higher errors.

The comparison also has a simple control. Just carry the last gauge reading forward and add the change scheduled on the screen. At 50% its error was 0.58356 percentage points. Against this control, the five-grid forecast with the earlier correction had 1 lower, 0 equal, 3 higher errors. Against the same five-grid forecast with cue adjustment alone, the mean gain from reusing the earlier correction was +0.00226 percentage points.

All eleven forecasts were issued at 592 cutoffs on each of twelve second attempts. At 30% MVC the target adjustment equals zero by construction. During final rest, both practice corrections multiply a cue change of zero. The 7,104 overlapping forecasts come from four adults at three targets. The people and their earlier study results were known before the new day’s membership was fixed. We observed a single transition between indexed days with a fixed repeat order. The experiment cannot establish how long a correction lasts, or whether it will carry to a new person or task.
