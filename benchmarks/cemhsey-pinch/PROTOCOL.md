# Can surface EMG improve an offline pinch-force forecast?

A person pinches a dynamometer while following a repeated cue. The cue already tells us a great deal about what their next reading will be. We ask whether four amplitude features from two short windows of forearm surface EMG improve a forecast of the measured reading after the model has seen the recent force and the cue phase. The comparison uses a separate acquisition from the project's Hyser experiment. The P200 gives one reading for the force between the opposing digits. Its released scale is dimensionless, normalized to the participant's maximum voluntary contraction for that day and grasp. We retain that scale. The measurement cannot identify the force of one digit or establish the performance of a five-digit predictor. ## Which samples the model may see

Index the first released force sample as zero at the nominal 200 Hz rate and the first released EMG sample as zero at the nominal 2,048 Hz rate. The paper says the modalities were collected simultaneously within each trial. The source evidence establishes the within-trial pair and nominal rates. It supplies no bound on cross-device start offset, drift, delivery or preprocessing phase. The EMG comparison therefore treats the two first indices as a common **nominal release coordinate** by convention. No force/EMG cross-correlation, length stretching, endpoint alignment, resampling or data-driven shift selects this coordinate. The 100 ms guard below separates written release indices. It provides no bound on which signals were physically available at a forecast instant. Use this comparison only after source acceptance of a trial-relative index convention. If the EMG identity or convention remains unresolved but the normalized native force series qualifies, use the force-only model and leave the EMG question open. If force identity, units or its native rate fail, stop the experiment.

Issue indices are $i = 60,70,\ldots,5970$ on the force stream. Their 592 paired targets per trial are `f[i+24]`, inside the first 6,000 force indices. Twenty-four intervals at the nominal rate are 120 ms **on the force record**. Features may use force through `f[i]`. Force history comprises `f[i]` and `f[i]-f[i-k]` for `k=(5,10,20,40)`. No waveform filtering, clipping, calibration to newtons or full-trial amplitude normalization is fitted.

Issue-only phase changes at 0, 5, 10, 20 and 25 seconds (initial rest, ramp up, hold, release and final rest), with the endpoint at 30 seconds. At issue tick `i`, use five indicators and five associated fractions of that phase elapsed, inactive entries set to zero. Derive them from the fixed schedule of this one condition. Measured force or future cue values do not determine phase. The same schedule holds for both measured and control trials.

EMG uses two half-open nominal intervals, `[i-60,i-40)` and `[i-40,i-20)`, expressed here in force ticks. Convert each integer boundary `b` to the exclusive or inclusive index implied by `(b × 2048 + 199) // 200`. For each 100 ms interval calculate RMS separately over each of the 320 channels. Summarize those 320 values by their median and 90th percentile using linear interpolation at positions `(320−1) × p` in ascending zero-based order. Transform each summary by `log1p(RMS / 1 mV)`. Features are older median, older 90th percentile, recent median and recent 90th percentile, in that order. Channel ordering does not change these features. No channel selection, trial-specific scaling or model-side filtering is applied. The study reports hardware/acquisition filtering. The last possible EMG sample has nominal index strictly earlier than the issue-100 ms boundary. These four numbers test one amplitude representation of surface muscle electrical activity.

## What counts as evidence

The endpoint is DEV mean absolute error (MAE) in released normalized force. Report root mean squared error (RMSE) second, taking the square root after equal weighting of squared errors. Use one common set of issues for all models and split all scores by issue phase as a secondary readout. The main comparisons are MAE of force-history/phase minus MAE of paired EMG, and MAE of other-repeat EMG minus MAE of paired EMG. Positive values favor correctly paired EMG. Aggregate the paired differences at the person level and report the average, the count of positive person differences without labels, and the separate day-average contrasts without person-specific values. Enumerate the `4^4` resamples of four DEV person clusters with replacement and give the linear 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles of the mean contrast as a descriptive sensitivity range conditional on these two indexed days. Four people and two indexed days cannot establish reliable population coverage. There is no confirmatory p-value, equivalence claim, or criterion for production readiness. Call a lower mean in both primary comparisons an observed advantage on this panel and give its size, day variation and uncertainty. If either comparison is zero or adverse, the panel does not show the paired advantage asked for.

These comparisons test a later release-index value from a dynamometer in a fixed task. Physical latency, a real-time interface and the general value of surface EMG require other evidence.
