# Does the layout of forearm activity help forecast a pinch?

Two people can put the same force on a pinch gauge while their forearms give different electrical readings. Even within one trial, two snapshots can have the same median amplitude while the stronger readings move from one electrode grid to another. The earlier CEMHSEY pinch forecast kept four summaries of amplitude and discarded the grid layout. Here we keep both and ask a narrower question. Does the distribution measured across five grids help estimate what the pinch gauge records next, after recent force, cue phase and overall EMG amplitude are already in the model? [Earlier pinch protocol](https://github.com/eliminatenoise/NeuroTranslation/blob/4cb63aa0a88be1d1a05d1201d7fed9b374a86ca3/benchmarks/cemhsey-pinch/PROTOCOL.md) · [CEMHSEY GRASP source study](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-025-05733-y)

## The pinch we measure

Use the adult human two-finger pad-to-pad pinch at 30% of maximum voluntary contraction (MVC), Session 5, Task 2, repeats 1 and 2. The separately measured P200 dynamometer value is one reading between the opposing digits, in fractions of the participant’s same-day and grasp MVC. Keep the released normalized force. It cannot resolve individual digit forces. The five forearm surface electromyography (EMG) grids record electrical activity through the skin. A grid index identifies part of the acquisition layout. Each grid’s readings can reflect overlapping sources, electrode contact and recording conditions. This forecast concerns normalized force in one specified task. It provides no inference about an individual muscle, peripheral nerve activity, intention or causal control. [CEMHSEY GRASP, Methods and Data Records](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-025-05733-y)

Train on the same three people and indexed days 1 and 2 as the earlier fit (12 files). Score once on four entirely unused people on indexed days 5 and 6 (16 files). The people in the earlier evaluation are excluded. Private source membership and role grants bind the exact member names and accepted body hashes before a stage can reach the arrays. Missing or invalid recordings hold the affected stage. There are no substitutions, dropped rows, added days, or waveform-dependent inclusion decisions. Seal the exact model and TRAIN fit before authorizing new-panel scores. Subjects and days both change, so this design cannot isolate either effect. [GRASP Part I v2](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.15077957) · [GRASP Part II v2](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.15070187)

## Two windows, five grids

Retain the earlier forecast’s support. The 592 issue indices are 60, 70, …, 5970 on the nominal 200 Hz force record. The target at issue index *i* is the independently measured value *f[i+24]*, 120 ms later on that record. Its latest index is 5994. Use force through *f[i]* and differences over 5, 10, 20 and 40 force ticks. Take the five phase indicators and their elapsed fractions from the fixed issue-time cue schedule, with phase boundaries at 0, 5, 10, 20, 25 and 30 seconds. Force readings and later cues cannot move those boundaries. Every arm uses the same 592 issues.

EMG comes from two consecutive nominal 100 ms intervals, *[i−60,i−40)* and *[i−40,i−20)*, written in force ticks. Each integer boundary *b* becomes the EMG index *ceil(2048b/200)*. Compute the root mean square (RMS) of each channel’s values in each interval. Window starts 0, 10, …, 5930 give 594 distinct RMS vectors as issue indices advance. Window *j* and window *j+2* give the older and recent values for issue number *j*, with zero-based numbering. The last eligible nominal EMG index lies before issue minus 100 ms. The release describes a paired recording with nominal rates. We treat first released sample as zero on each record. This convention supplies no bound on physical synchronization, drift, processing phase, transmission or device latency. No resampling, realignment, waveform filtering, clipping or trial-scale adjustment affects this calculation. [Earlier pinch protocol and timing convention](https://github.com/eliminatenoise/NeuroTranslation/blob/4cb63aa0a88be1d1a05d1201d7fed9b374a86ca3/benchmarks/cemhsey-pinch/PROTOCOL.md)

The four retained amplitude features are log1p of the median and 90th percentile of the 320 channel RMS values over the older window, then the recent window, with RMS divided by 1 mV. Quantiles use linear interpolation at sorted zero-based position *(320−1)p*. The recorded MATLAB rows map to grids in five contiguous blocks of 64. In each window, calculate log1p of each grid’s median channel RMS divided by 1 mV, then subtract the mean of the five transformed medians. Keep the resulting five grid values in acquisition order for the older window, then the five for the recent window. Each set of five sums to zero up to rounding. The transformation isolates contrasts among the five pooled amplitudes relative to a common level in log space. It does not eliminate all effects of a gain change. [CEMHSEY GRASP, Fig. 1 and Data Records](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-025-05733-y) · [Reader channel map](./channel-map.json)

For the same-size grouping control, calculate the ten features the same way after grouping raw channel RMS values by zero-based row index modulo 5. This interleaves the native grids into five sets of 64 before pooling. The grouping rule and channel count stay fixed. Evaluation can distinguish these exact physical and interleaved pools. It cannot establish that one electrode arrangement would win against every possible grouping.

The separately specified rank model adds one value to the main grid model. Rank the 320 RMS readings in the older and recent windows independently, averaging tied ranks. Center the two rank vectors and take their correlation by matching the same channel indices. A flat vector gets zero correlation by convention. A value near 1 means the channels kept a similar relative ordering across the two windows. For the broken-correspondence control, take the SHA-256 of the ASCII string `cemhsey-pinch-channel-derangement-v1:<zero-based decimal index>` for each index from 0 through 319. Sort indices by digest into a ring and map each to the next index in that ring, wrapping at the end. Use the mapping only on the recent ranks. It moves every index while preserving the complete set of recent ranks and all amplitude and grid features. It gives one fixed corruption, independent of the signal values.

## What the model has to beat

| Comparison | Inputs beyond force history and phase |
| --- | --- |
| Historical amplitude | Same-trial global EMG summaries |
| Historical other-repeat amplitude | Other-repeat global EMG summaries |
| Native grids | Same-trial global summaries and native grids |
| Interleaved grids | Same-trial global summaries and interleaved pools |
| Other-repeat grid profile | Same-trial global summaries and other-repeat native grids |
| Full other-repeat grid | Other-repeat global summaries and native grids |
| Native grids and rank | Native-grid inputs plus same-trial channel rank persistence |
| Deranged channel rank | Native-grid inputs plus rank persistence with deranged recent correspondence |
| Other-repeat channel rank | Native-grid inputs plus other-repeat rank persistence |

The original persistence, phase-only, force-history and force-history-plus-phase comparisons remain. A donor is the other repeat from the same person, indexed day, grasp and load at the same issue tick. Both directions of each pair are used. Donated EMG never changes the force history, target or issue phase. Refit each comparator on its corresponding TRAIN inputs. Separately report what happens when each fitted same-trial model receives its corrupted inputs from the table at score time. Those perturbations measure reliance of a given fit and cannot substitute for the refitted comparisons.

All fitted arms use the earlier ridge objective. Center and scale each feature by its TRAIN mean and population standard deviation. A constant column gets scale 1. Fit an unpenalized intercept with equal weight per participant, day, repeat and issue and a squared penalty of 0.01 times the sum of squared standardized slopes. The phase-only arm predicts the later force directly. The other fitted arms predict the change from current force and add current force back. Persistence returns current force. Feature order, regularization, clocks and denominators are fixed before any study scoring.

## How to read the result

The primary endpoint is mean absolute error (MAE) in fractions of MVC. Report root mean squared error and issue-phase errors as secondary readouts. A positive contrast is the comparator’s MAE minus the candidate’s MAE on the identical issue support. The native-grid candidate needs positive mean contrasts against all four refits: same-trial global amplitude, interleaved pools, other-repeat grid profile and full other-repeat grid. The prespecified rank extension needs positive mean contrasts against the native-grid fit, deranged channel rank and other-repeat rank. Assess them separately, with no decision to switch candidates or revise a result after TEST. The earlier same-trial amplitude contrasts versus force-history/phase and refitted other-repeat amplitude remain visible.

For each contrast, report the count of favorable person-level means, both indexed-day means, and the linear 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles over all 256 resamples of four person clusters with replacement, conditional on that panel’s two days. A positive mean across every required contrast gives a directional panel lead. To describe a lead as consistent on this panel, both observed day means and every descriptive range’s lower endpoint must also be positive. Four people and two days give limited evidence about new populations, new placements and new tasks. The ranges are sensitivity summaries. No confirmatory p-value, equivalence claim or real-time capability follows.

Keep the qualified original files, per-trial descriptors and genuine fitted weights private. Use invented packet and parameter examples in the public reader. The reader preserves release indices and lets a viewer inspect a grid and see a prediction drawn from the same unrounded channel RMS values. Any public observed score requires independent reproduction and an allowlisted non-identifying aggregate.
