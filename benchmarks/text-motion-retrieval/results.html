Which movement did the volunteer describe?

Volunteers watched recordings of a person moving and wrote down what they saw. Given a description, the models ranked 38 recordings. Each recording counts equally, even when several volunteers described it.

The attention model looked for patterns in the sequence of words and the sequence of movements. It ranked the right recording first less often than a simpler model that counted words and summarized the movement. Thirty-one of the 38 recordings showed walking. Several descriptions could fit more than one recording.

The models were fitted on 149 recordings and assessed on 90 descriptions of 38 recordings from six other KIT catalogue subject IDs. Another 31 recordings remained in reserve. This experiment matches words to recordings of movements someone has already made. It contains no brain or muscle signals.
