# A harder pinch changes the forecast. By how much?

A model trained on a 30% pinch gets a different assignment when the screen says 50%. It already sees the recent gauge readings. The question is whether the scheduled change in the cue, and then one practice repeat at the new target, can improve its next reading. We test the same question at 10%. At 30%, the cue adjustment is zero by construction, which gives us an identity check before we consider the practice repeat.

## What the gauge measures

Use the adult CEMHSEY GRASP pad-to-pad pinch. The released P200 dynamometer reading gives one force value between the opposing digits, normalized by the participant’s same-day maximum voluntary contraction (MVC) for this grasp. The forearm grids measure surface electromyography (EMG), electrical activity recorded through the skin. The forecast estimates one future gauge reading. [CEMHSEY GRASP study](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-025-05733-y) · [GRASP Part I v2](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.15077957) · [GRASP Part II v2](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.15070187)

Four adults contribute both repeats at each of three targets on one indexed day. Their recordings were absent from the original training cohort, and earlier studies have already used recordings from these people on different days. The first repeat at each person and target belongs to ADAPT (12 trials). The second belongs to EVAL (12 trials). The exact private roster was committed before new reads, SHA-256 `a4bbfd14736b08f1decdbcd64fc3110489b93a8fb413c580f7fc0a49d9ea4ea2`. The two roles receive separate grants and record seals. Seal and independently check the 24 ADAPT coefficients before opening EVAL. There are no substitutions, exclusions by waveform, retuning or split changes. Missing or invalid input blocks that role.

## Nine ways to forecast the same reading

Use the published point forecast’s 592 issue ticks per trial, `60, 70, …, 5970`, and score recorded force at `i+24`. The nominal 200 Hz force record makes that lead 120 ms. Force features end at `i`. The grid model uses two guarded EMG windows `[i−60,i−40)` and `[i−40,i−20)` in force ticks, with each boundary `b` mapped to `ceil(2048b/200)` and the unchanged amplitude and grid order. The acquisition clock is nominal, with the first released index taken as zero on both streams. It gives no measured online latency, synchronization bound or physiological delay. [Preceding point forecast and timing conventions](https://github.com/eliminatenoise/NeuroTranslation/tree/d66fc10251fb3e04e6dcd8a9d2cda8c927ade408)

Take the cue target `q` as `0.10`, `0.30` or `0.50` MVC. The unit cue `g` is zero through tick 1000, rises linearly to one at 2000, holds through 4000, falls linearly to zero at 5000 and stays there. Define `u = g(i+24) − g(i)`. The participant’s cue was scheduled in advance, so this term can cover a phase boundary without consulting later measured force. The three controls are current force `f[i]`, the future scheduled cue `q*g(i+24)`, and `f[i] + q*u`, which adds the cue’s signed upcoming change to current force.

Carry forward the saved training fits for force history and phase (`history_phase`) and the same inputs with forearm summaries and native grid contrasts (`history_phase_paired_grid`). For each fit take three forecasts on the same issues. The frozen forecast `p₀` uses the unchanged fitted weights. The target-adjusted forecast is `p = p₀ + (q−0.30)*u`. The adapted forecast is `p + b*u`. For each participant, intensity and fit, calculate only one coefficient `b = sum(u*(y−p))/sum(u²)` on the first repeat. Here `y` is the measured force 24 ticks after the issue. There is no intercept, clipping, weighting choice or parent refit. The second repeat supplies no fitted value, even when a coefficient makes the forecast worse. At 30%, the frozen and target-adjusted forecasts must agree exactly. Adaptation can still change a 30% forecast.

## What counts as an improvement

Primary is absolute mean error (MAE) at 50%, with 10% as the companion and 30% as the identity check. Report MAE, root mean squared error (RMSE) and mean signed error `forecast−actual` in fractions of MVC. Report target-relative MAE `MAE/q` in fractions of the prescribed target. Give all nine arms separately by target, overall within target and by phase at the issue tick. Initial rest, ramp up, hold, release and final rest use the half-open boundaries `0, 1000, 2000, 4000, 5000, 6000`. The five per-trial counts are `94, 100, 200, 100, 98`, including issues whose target crosses an edge. EVAL has 2,368 issues and four trials per target, or 7,104 issues and 12 trials total. Compute each error metric within a person and report the mean of those four metrics within each target and phase. In particular, pooled RMSE does not replace the mean of the four person-level RMSEs.

For each target and phase, and overall within target, take paired person-level MAE differences on the identical issues. Use comparator MAE minus candidate MAE, so a positive difference favours the candidate. For each parent, compare adjusted with frozen, adapted with adjusted, and both adjusted and adapted with persistence plus scheduled change. At each of the adjusted and adapted levels, compare the grid fit with the history fit. Report the balanced mean, observed minimum and maximum and the number of strictly positive, exactly equal and strictly negative person-level differences. Directions can reverse across target or phase. The range describes the four observed differences. The overlapping windows and repeated people give this panel no population, confidence or causal interpretation.

The files are offline recordings and the schedule is known in advance. Gauge force and EMG are measured, while forecasts, errors and practice coefficients are derived. The panel tests one grasp and three prescribed targets in one recording setting. It cannot establish individual digit force, nerve activity, intention, real-time control, clinical utility or transfer to other people and tasks. Public results require independent reproduction and a fixed allowlist of aggregate metrics. Keep individual readings, identifiers, model weights, coefficients and participant-level results private.
