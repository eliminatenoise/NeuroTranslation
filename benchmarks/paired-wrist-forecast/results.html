Adding the forearm signal made this wrist forecast worse.

People turned their right wrists to point at targets. Two angle sensors measured how far each wrist bent and tilted. Given half a second of earlier movement, a Transformer predicted the wrist angles a quarter-second ahead. It missed by 5.432° using motion alone and 5.866° when it also saw the forearm’s electrical activity.

EMG records voltage at the forearm. EEG records voltage at the scalp. Swapping a different movement’s voltage into a forecast barely changed the Transformer’s error. The same swap raised the linear model’s error by 0.766° for EMG and 0.252° for EEG.

The previous motion-only convolutional network, which detects short patterns in wrist movement, had the smallest error here at 4.053°. Repeating the last recorded angle gave 7.924°.

Six people supplied training data. Three previously examined people supplied 959 exploratory trials. With all three inputs, nominal 95% forecast ranges covered 89.3% of extension readings and 93.5% of sideways readings. Physical timing delays and performance on new participants remain open.

Run a forecast

With Python, NumPy and CPU PyTorch installed, run python -B predict.py from this folder. The example uses invented inputs and the three models fitted to recordings.
