# Fixed-checkpoint evaluation at two and four native passes

The expanded NeuroTranslate 0.1 Research Edition walking checkpoint had lower mean error at three passes than at two or four for both prespecified outcomes. The primary measure was mean absolute error in signed vertical impulse over the following 100 ms. The secondary measure was Brier error for the onset of new loading, the squared difference between the forecast probability and the observed zero-or-one outcome. Smaller values are better for both.

| Method | Impulse MAE (N·s) | New-load Brier |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Hold the last 20 ms mean force and use the fitting event rate | 3.23650 | 0.03994999 |
| Two passes | 3.03580 | 0.03880926 |
| Three passes, matched control | **2.89490** | **0.03862550** |
| Four passes | 3.03478 | 0.03865915 |

Compared with three passes, impulse MAE rose by 0.14090 N·s at two and 0.13988 N·s at four. Three beat two for all three walkers and beat four for two. Omitting each walker in turn left three ahead on impulse in all three remaining pairs. Brier error rose by 0.00018377 at two and 0.00003366 at four. The two-pass event score was lower than three for two walkers, but its disadvantage for the third outweighed those improvements. Four was lower than three for one walker. Both new depths beat the fitting event rate for all three walkers. [Aggregate scores and the omission check](results.json)

## Checkpoint and observations

The Research Edition uses Thinking Machines Lab’s TML-d24-varloop-research, a separately trained companion to Inkling 2 Mini. Each pass traverses the middle 12 of 24 decoder layers. Training ran three passes, one epoch and 547 updates on 9,586 plate rows from 36 walks by 12 people. Training updated 28,670,978 parameters across the numerical input and output layers, sensor messages between passes and selected original recurrent parameters. The CPU loader expanded the publisher’s packed expert weights to BF16 and retained its FP32 exceptions. The selected training values comprised 14,155,776 BF16 and 14,515,202 FP32 parameters. The present run used the same checkpoint, precision, input channels and output scaling at every depth. [Training protocol and checkpoint](https://github.com/eliminatenoise/NeuroTranslation/tree/476296338055624d63654579f0bd7e935468c925/benchmarks/expanded-walking-research)

Three of six assigned validation walkers contributed all six named surface-muscle voltage channels. Each provided three walks. At 216 issue times, a call received both plates’ histories through the same time, using ten 20 ms bins. Impulse summed the following 100 signed vertical force measurements at 1 kHz and multiplied by 0.001 seconds. New loading required an upward crossing of +20 N after 20 unloaded samples within ±10 N and at least 20 loaded samples including the crossing. Confirmation could use readings through 119 ms. There were 432 impulse outcomes and 427 usable event outcomes. Eighteen positive event windows represented eight distinct onsets. Errors were averaged over eligible plates at each issue, then within each walk, then equally across people. Restricting impulse to the 427 rows with both labels gave 2.84836, 2.71010 and 2.85332 N·s at two, three and four passes. The matched physical reference gave 3.07640 N·s. [Original recordings](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.24192480.v1) · [Recording methods](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-023-02767-y)

## Runtime and interpretation

A single deterministic four-thread CPU run computed all three depths from shared intermediate calculations. Its median forward time after the first issue was 1.033 seconds per two-plate issue for all three forecasts together. Loading the base took 62.05 seconds. The guarded run finished in 305.94 seconds and peaked at 49.72 GiB of sampled process memory. Separate model calls matched the shared calculation bit for bit at the first, middle and last issues at each depth. All 432 three-pass event and impulse forecasts matched the earlier saved results. The process monitor logged an unavailable process reading during shutdown after the forecasts had been written. The worker exited successfully. A separate check verified every saved forecast and its measurement time before scoring.

All nine walks had been scored previously. The two new pass counts and their comparison with three were fixed before running them, and forecasts were saved before reopening the measurements for scoring. In the earlier five-depth comparison on these walks, five passes gave the lowest native Brier score, 0.03859348. An earlier NeuroTranslate 0.0 comparison had Brier error 0.03831118 and much higher impulse MAE, 10.86727 N·s. A separate published treadmill transfer test included eight new walkers and only force inputs. It tested one, three, five, seven and nine passes. Linear force extrapolation gave lower impulse error than the best native result on all 384 impulse rows, 9.64445 versus 10.69183 N·s at three passes. One pass then gave the lowest native event error, 0.06997673 against a fitted-prior baseline of 0.07075751. Two and four passes have no treadmill result in either study. The example still uses three passes. The pass counts give no general depth recommendation. The study set no equivalence margin for declaring a plateau. Accuracy and end-to-end timing for live measurements remain untested. The separate final test cohort remained unopened.
