# Do the other fingers help predict the index sensor?

Press two fingers against separate transducers and the index reading is only one part of the record. Does seeing the other four readings help forecast the index voltage 120 ms later? We start with an earlier result from these Hyser records. The recent index readings made a strong baseline, and adding the surface EMG features we tested did not lower the average error. Here we change which voltages the model can see. We keep the index history, target, cutoffs and participant split.

## Four ways to see the same trial

Hyser’s Random task records voluntary combinations of isometric finger contractions in adults with intact fingers. The released transducer file has 2,500 frames at 100 Hz, with channels in this order: thumb, index, middle, ring, little. We convert each channel’s signed little-endian counts to volts using its own WFDB header, `(count − baseline) / gain`. A format-16 missing value becomes nonfinite. We add no filter or resampling. [Hyser 2.0.0 and acquisition methods](https://doi.org/10.13026/hxan-pe94) · [Original study](https://doi.org/10.1109/TNSRE.2021.3082551) · [WFDB formats](https://physionet.org/physiotools/wag/signal-5.htm) · [WFDB header fields](https://physionet.org/physiotools/wag/header-5.htm)

At each cutoff `t`, take 41 consecutive readings from `t−40` through `t`, oldest first. Forecast the release’s index voltage at `t+12`. The first cutoff is tick 140 and the last is 2,385, stepping by five. This gives 450 matched rows per trial. A missing or nonfinite input or target in any scored row stops the phase. Every real input comes from at or before the cutoff on the release’s 100 Hz grid.

| Input | What the ridge model sees | Values |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Index history | The 41 index readings. | 41 |
| Five aligned histories | Index, thumb, middle, ring and little. Each contributes its 41 readings through `t` in the same trial. | 205 |
| Other-trial control | The same index history and index target as above. The four other histories come together from the next trial, at the same elapsed time, on the same day for the same person. Trial 5 wraps to trial 1. | 205 |
| Five sensors, compact summary | The same 41 index readings. For each of thumb, middle, ring and little, take its voltage at `t` and its least-squares slope over 41 ticks in V/tick, `sum((i−20) × V[i]) / sum((i−20)²)` for positions `i = 0,…,40`. | 49 |

The other-trial model gives the extra features the same width, nominal phase and acquisition session. Its added values still come from that person, but from a different contraction. The trial assignment uses filenames alone, ignores values and keeps every donor inside the same participant and day. It tests whether the gain depends on alignment to the scored trial. This is a retrospective control. It can use a donor trial recorded later and has no deployable predictor. Index history, the compact summary and the full histories all use the same-trial recordings. The compact summary asks whether each added channel’s current level and fixed linear trend explain as much forecast error as its whole recent trajectory. It keeps the sensor count at five.

## Fit on people, score on people

For each arm the ridge target is `index[t+12] − index[t]`. Fit an unpenalized intercept and minimize mean squared residual plus `alpha × sum(coef²)` after standardization. Each fit estimates each feature’s mean and population standard deviation on its training people only. A scale at or below `10⁻¹⁴ × max(1, abs(mean))` becomes one. The forecast adds the predicted change to `index[t]`. Penalty candidates are `10⁻⁶, 10⁻⁵, 10⁻⁴, 10⁻³, 10⁻², 10⁻¹, 1, 10`. Select separately for each arm by average held-person absolute error across inner leave-one-person-out splits, with the largest penalty winning exact ties.

The split is the earlier index study’s hash ordering, ascending SHA-256 of `hyser-random-index-20261119-v1/SNN` for `S01` through `S20`. The first 12 people form TRAIN, the next four DEV and the final four the reserved FINAL set. Both days and all five trials per day stay with each person. Nested TRAIN leaves each person’s two days out of standardization, fitting and penalty selection. The full 12-person fits are selected by the fixed inner procedure and locked before DEV. DEV updates neither the coefficients nor the input scaling. These four DEV participants and trials have already been scored in the earlier index and calibration studies. The new inputs and analysis are specified prospectively while DEV remains a retrospective development check. FINAL stays unopened.

Report day 1, day 2 and their equal-weight average within each person, then give each person the same weight. Precedence rests with both-days mean absolute error (MAE). Root mean squared error (RMSE) provides a separate view of larger misses. TRAIN scores 120 trials and 54,000 rows per arm, and DEV scores 40 trials and 18,000 rows per arm. Day-by-day differences compare the same frozen method on recorded sessions. They do not measure transfer of a personal calibration. The two evaluation cohorts are distinct.

For each metric and day grouping, subtract the focus arm’s error from the comparator’s error, person by person. Positive deltas favor the focus arm. The primary contrasts compare aligned five histories with index history and with the other-trial control. The compact-summary contrasts compare full histories with the compact summary, and the compact summary with index history. Also report the other-trial control versus index history. Summarize each contrast with the paired mean, counts of positive and zero deltas, the range of leave-one-person-out paired means and the middle 95% of participant-resampled means. The TRAIN bootstrap uses 10,000 draws with NumPy’s seed 196233. DEV enumerates all 256 ordered draws of four people with replacement. Keep the two days together in each resampled person. The intervals describe the observed cohorts. Shared training sets and repeated windows limit inference, and the four-person development cohort cannot establish population performance.

An aligned-model gain that survives both primary comparisons would support trial-aligned predictive information in these released records. A gain only against index history would leave the alignment question open. Show losses and inconclusive comparisons alongside gains. Coupled contractions, shared task behaviour and instrument effects are all compatible with a predictive gain.

## What 120 ms means here

The horizon is 12 ticks of the released trace. The paper describes low-pass preprocessing, and the public benchmark applies a forward/reverse filter after loading the release. The upstream filtering and causal availability of the distributed readings are unverified. The forecast error is in transducer volts, without calibration to newtons or percent maximum voluntary contraction. This experiment measures offline sensor prediction for one task. It leaves device delay, underlying neural activity and live control unmeasured.
