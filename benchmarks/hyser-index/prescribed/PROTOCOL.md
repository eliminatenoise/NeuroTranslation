# What happens when the task changes?

The three [finger-sensor forecasts](../whole-hand/README.md) learned from voluntary finger combinations that participants chose for themselves. Here they face the Hyser N-DoF task. The same people followed prescribed combinations and force trajectories. The models still have to predict the **index transducer voltage 120 ms ahead**; the cue is never an input. We fix this comparison before scoring a prescribed-task recording. [Dataset](https://physionet.org/content/hd-semg/2.0.0/) · [Acquisition study, section II.C](https://doi.org/10.1109/TNSRE.2021.3082551)

## Who and which recordings

Keep the original participant assignment from the [Random-task protocol](../whole-hand/PROTOCOL.md). Report two cohorts separately. The first contains the 12 people whose Random-task trials trained the shared weights. The second contains the four people whose Random-task trials had previously been scored in development but never fitted those weights. The N-DoF recordings are new to this comparison in both cohorts. The second cohort tests the fixed forecasts in a different task and in people outside the fit. Those participants’ earlier results informed the research question. This study uses the same devices and experimental cohort as the original work. The four reserved final participants remain outside it.

For each included person take **sample 1 of each of the 15 prescribed combinations, on each of the two days**. Selecting the first numbered repetition gives each condition the same representation, independent of what the signals look like. The other repetition is outside this comparison. The 12-person cohort therefore contributes 360 trials and the four-person cohort 120. The first cohort must pass source-integrity and scoring checks before access to the second. The sign or size of a result has no bearing on that decision.

The acquisition study enumerates the prescribed combinations. The index finger is cued in combinations 1, 5, 6, 7, 9, 10, 11 and 15. It is absent from the instructions in combinations 2, 3, 4, 8, 12, 13 and 14. The transducer is measured in both groups. An uncued index finger may still move or exert tension. Within each cohort report the full set, the eight index-cued combinations, and the seven index-uncued combinations. The all-combination comparison is primary. The two cue groups explain whether the result depends on what the index finger was asked to do. No trial is selected on the basis of its measured amplitude.

## Forecasts and scores

Use the [published pooled weights](https://github.com/eliminatenoise/NeuroTranslation/blob/0e7555b2c1a05fa6deb6aeea97befa5636caab7b/benchmarks/hyser-index/whole-hand/WEIGHTS.json), SHA-256 `c237d6b59268c22d9e2844d32fe4ffd36dccf0584a8ad99b3465cc94cd2254d2`. Keep every coefficient, intercept, scaling factor and penalty fixed. There is no fitting, personal calibration, model selection or input from EMG, cue identity or trial number. The forecasts use the following inputs.

| Forecast | Available readings |
| --- | --- |
| Last reading | Repeat the index voltage at the cutoff. |
| Index history | 41 index readings from the cutoff minus 40 ticks through the cutoff. |
| Five histories | The same 41 readings from each finger. |
| Compact five-sensor model | Full index history plus the latest voltage and the 41-reading linear slope for each other finger. |

Every eligible trial is a source-identified pair of WFDB files with five force-sensor channels, a 100 Hz clock and 2,500 frames. Verify the publisher’s SHA-256 for both files, channel order and units, multiplexed format-16 layout, initial samples and per-channel checksums. Convert each channel to volts using its header gain and baseline. Stop the relevant cohort on a missing source file, integrity mismatch, unexpected layout or nonfinite value used by any forecast or target. Report the failure and its scope. Keep the same cutoffs for every method. Ticks 140, 145, …, 2,385 give 450 forecasts of the index reading 12 ticks later. This gives 162,000 forecasts per method in the first cohort and 54,000 in the second.

For each person and day, pool cutoffs from the selected combinations. Compute the mean absolute error (MAE) and the square root of the mean squared error (RMSE). Average the two day scores within each person, then give each person equal weight. Also report day-specific cohort means. MAE is the primary metric; we report the same paired differences for RMSE as a secondary metric. The primary contrast subtracts the five-history MAE from the index-history MAE within each person; positive values favor the five-history forecast. Secondary contrasts compare five histories with the compact forecast, compact with index, and index with the last reading, for both metrics. For each contrast report the mean paired difference, the count of people with a positive difference or tie, and the middle 95% of resampled participant means. Keep both days together. For 12 people use 10,000 bootstrap draws from NumPy’s default random generator with seed 196233, resetting the seed for each comparison. For four people enumerate all 256 ordered draws with replacement. Use linear interpolation between adjacent sorted means for the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles. These are descriptive ranges for small cohorts.

Report output voltages in mV in the reader-facing tables and in V in machine-readable scores. Neither the cue nor the transducer establishes muscle activation, physiological lead, force in newtons or clinical utility. The stored trace’s upstream processing leaves its live delay unresolved. A within-task score cannot isolate the effect of the task cue itself. The two cohorts differ in fit exposure, participant membership and sample size. The assessment does not interpret an absolute difference between these cohorts as a causal effect.
