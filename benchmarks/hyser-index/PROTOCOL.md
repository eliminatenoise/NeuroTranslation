# Does surface EMG help predict the next reading?

The index finger can press harder without moving any farther. In Hyser’s Random task, 20 adults with intact fingers made self-selected combinations of isometric finger contractions. Participants saw their measured forces on a screen. On two separate days, each completed five 25-second trials. This study asks whether forearm surface EMG improves a forecast of the index finger’s released sensor voltage 120 ms ahead, given its recent voltage history.

The apparatus acquired raw 256-channel surface EMG at 2,048 Hz and finger-force signals at 100 Hz. Both systems received a common trigger at task onset. The release provides ADC counts and channel-specific header scaling. The target is voltage from the named index force transducer, expressed in V. Read it as a released force-sensor signal. The paper describes an eighth-order 10 Hz low-pass filter for preprocessing. The benchmark toolbox applies forward/reverse filtering after loading the released force. The exact upstream filter and causal availability of the distributed force bytes are unverified. This experiment adds neither filtering nor resampling. The nominal grids give an elapsed time in the released traces. They provide no measured clock drift, processing delay or physiological lead time. Surface EMG measures muscle electrical activity.

## Forecast and comparisons

Use both days and all five trials per person. Allocate the released pseudonymous person IDs to 12 TRAIN, four DEV and four FINAL by lexicographic SHA-256 digest of ASCII `hyser-random-index-20261119-v1/SNN`. Roles are set from public metadata only. The authorized runs can use TRAIN and, under an independent later grant, DEV, with all FINAL files outside the licensed study’s execution scope.

Take score anchors every five force ticks from 140 through 2,385 inclusive. The force input spans ticks `t−40,…,t` inclusive, 41 values with a 400 ms difference from first to last. The target is the same released channel at `t+12`. The one-second guard keeps the entire force window and the target at least one second from the release edges on its nominal lattice. This guard controls the evaluation range and has no bearing on whether the source filtering was causal. Where the source reader exposes format-16 missing values, a nonfinite feature or target in the scored support blocks the entire phase. There is no imputation, window dropping or post-fit exclusion.

Use four half-open 100 ms EMG bins from `t−42` to `t−2` in 100 Hz force ticks, with the final edge 20 ms before the anchor. On the 2,048 Hz grid, the first sample of a boundary at force tick `k` is `ceil(512k/25)`. Each bin therefore contains 204 or 205 raw samples. This fixed-duration convention puts 819 or 820 samples in the full interval. Square in volts, average over samples and channels in each of the four consecutive 64-channel 2D electrode arrays and then across all 256 channels. Take the natural log of RMS with a 10⁻¹² V floor. Per source paper Section II.B and Fig. 1, the array regions are in acquisition order. The pooling makes no claim about which muscle caused a signal. Bin order is oldest first. Within each bin, order is the four arrays and then the combined value.

Fit these arms on the same rows:

| Arm | Forecast |
| --- | --- |
| Persistence | Released voltage at `t`. |
| Slope | Current voltage plus 12 times the least-squares slope across the last 11 readings, measured in V per tick. |
| Force | Ridge on 41 force readings. |
| Aligned | The same force input plus the twenty aligned EMG features. |
| Earlier | The same force input plus the identical EMG recipe shifted 400 ms earlier. Its support `[t−82,t−42)` is disjoint from `[t−42,t−2)`. |
| Same phase, other trial | The same force input plus the aligned EMG features from the next trial, wrapping trial 5 to trial 1 for the same person and day. The trial clock, task, sampling and acquisition session are held constant. The voluntary contraction trajectory may differ. This is an offline diagnostic. |

Ridge predicts `voltage[t+12] − voltage[t]` and then adds the last voltage back. Standardize features using population mean and standard deviation within the fit set. Replace scale with one when it is at or below 10⁻¹⁴ × max(1, abs(feature mean)). Optimize average squared residual plus `alpha × sum(coef²)` in standardized coordinates, with an unpenalized intercept. Ridge penalty candidates are `10⁻⁶, 10⁻⁵, 10⁻⁴, 10⁻³, 10⁻², 10⁻¹, 1, 10`. Select separately per fitted arm by inner leave-one-person-out macro MAE, choosing the highest penalty on exact ties. Person-level training, selection and scoring fold sets are disjoint by construction. Same-phase donors always remain within the corresponding person-day group.

## Where does it work?

- Primary TRAIN estimates use nested leave-one-person-out predictions. For each held person, both of their days stay outside standardization, fitting and penalty selection. Then choose the full TRAIN models using the same inner selection over 12 TRAIN persons and freeze them, including the offline control fits, for any separately authorized DEV run. DEV receives no fit or normalization update. The two predictive models may be exported as combined TRAIN coefficients with the source credit. Per-person fits and predictions stay local in memory.
- Show Day 1 and Day 2 within each evaluation. Each day value weights persons equally. The combined value weights days equally within each person. MAE is primary, with RMSE as a secondary check. Report the number of expected and observed trials, people and windows, with 450 windows per trial. Identical rows allow paired deltas in volts. Positive delta means lower error for aligned EMG.
- Alongside paired means, report deterministic 95% person-resampling percentile intervals, exact enumeration when there are at most four people, otherwise 10,000 seeded participant bootstrap draws, and the range of leave-one-person-out paired means. The same participant index is resampled across both days. These describe variability in the observed persons, with no strong inference from the four DEV persons and no independent-window sampling assumption. Nested TRAIN predictions share fit sets and their intervals do not constitute an external validation. Report aggregate numbers of positive person-level deltas. If the aligned arm shows a gain against force alone yet fails to separate from the time-displaced controls, the evidence for trial-specific last-window timing remains inconclusive.
- Supplemental known-person, same-day scores use only three other trials for each scored target trial. Hold out its next-trial phase donor together with the scored trial. The remaining three training trials use their own fixed cyclic donor order. Score each target trial once. The penalty is fixed at 0.01 for every fitted arm. This calibration result is a different setting from predicting in an unseen person. Day 1 versus Day 2 descriptive differences are not a same-person cross-day model transfer result.

Learning a predictor from these signals will not quantify sensor latency without an independently timed recording. Questions about muscle force in newtons, individual muscle sources, live control, brain activity, clinical use, causation and transfer to another task require evidence beyond this study. No private raw voltages, individual scores, wearable identifiers, participant assignments or predictions leave this run. Model weights and derived aggregate results require separate integration acceptance before distribution.
