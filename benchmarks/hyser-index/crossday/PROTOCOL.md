# Does yesterday’s calibration help today?

A person can use the same finger on two days and give the forearm electrodes a slightly different view each time. Ask whether five labeled trials from yesterday improve tomorrow’s forecast for the same person. Compare those labels with five trials from somebody else. If the two calibrations do about as well, the numbers give little reason to call the adjustment personal.

Use the published R106 Hyser 2.0.0 Random experiment. Keep its original index transducer voltage, in V, at force tick `t+12` and its score grid from `t=140` through `2385`, every five ticks. The force arm uses ticks `t−40…t` inclusive. The aligned arm adds the four 100 ms bins of forearm surface-EMG log RMS ending at `t−2`, with the original 2,048 Hz half-open ceiling rule and four order-preserving 64-channel groups plus the combined value per bin. Reuse the frozen reader and the full R106 feature path and missing-data checks. A fully decoded trial contributes 450 rows. Five trials make the 2,250 calibration rows per person on Day 1 and the 2,250 scoring rows per person on Day 2. Any missing data, role mismatch or replay mismatch aborts the phase. No row imputation or selective exclusion is allowed.

## The two coefficients

Given current voltage `c`, frozen forecast `p` and Day 1 label `y`, take `d=p−c`, `s=max(10⁻¹² V, sqrt(mean(d²)))`, `q=d/s` and `e=y−p`. Minimize mean squared error in `e − a − bq` plus `0.1(a²+b²)`; both coefficients in that objective are in volts. The penalty is fixed on the dimensionless forecast change scale. It shrinks offset and gain toward the unadjusted forecast without selecting a penalty or feature from this study’s outcomes.

```text
[ 1.1      mean(q)       ] [a] = [ mean(e)   ]
[ mean(q)  mean(q²)+0.1  ] [b]   [ mean(q·e) ]
forecastDay2 = p + a + (b/s)(p−c).
```

Each arm needs just an offset in volts and a dimensionless gain correction at inference. The calibration run needs labeled Day 1 rows and an already trained base. The Day 2 inferencing expression has no label argument. Day 2 labels are for scoring. Five complete Day 1 trials are supervised calibration. Day 2 is zero-update prediction. This is output-layer fine-tuning on the same task.

## Who supplies Day 1?

Within each R106 role, pair consecutive people in the frozen digest order. The two people in each pair supply one another’s wrong-person labels. Roles never mix. Both calibration variants use the exact same feature recipe, five labeled Day 1 trials and the same base within arm and pair. A wrong-person forecast uses that donor’s fitted offset and gain on the target person’s current Day 2 input. No one is assigned a donor using age, signal quality or model outcomes.

- **TRAIN**. Six reciprocal pairs. For each pair fit one base per arm on both days of the remaining ten people. Use R106 person moments and inner leave-one-person-out selection over the complete R106 alpha grid on those ten people only, with its original deterministic tie rule. Both excluded people remain outside standardization, penalty selection and shared weights. Score a target’s Day 2 with three forecasts from the same base: unadjusted, adapted on their own Day 1, and adapted on their paired donor’s Day 1. Each fold also uses Day 2 data from the ten training people for its shared base. The original R106 TRAIN result is a distinct Day 2 reference: it used outer leave-one-person-out with eleven base participants. It cannot stand in for the matched two-person-excluded base.
- **DEV reuse**. Two reciprocal pairs. Decode the exact R106 pooled twelve-TRAIN-person force and aligned predictors, score their unadjusted forecasts again and require agreement with the published Day 2 macro scores within absolute `1e-12 V` (no relative tolerance). Compute all DEV adapters from DEV Day 1 before opening the new DEV Day 2 reads. Neither DEV day updates the shared model or penalty. These four people’s Day 2 records were already scored in R106. This retrospective reuse provides descriptive evidence and leaves four reserved FINAL people fully unopened.

## Measurements

The primary error is mean absolute error (MAE) within person across all five Day 2 trials. Also compute within-person root mean squared error (RMSE). Average each metric equally by person. TRAIN has 12 scored people, 60 Day 1 and 60 Day 2 trials, with 27,000 rows per arm and stage. DEV has four people, 20 Day 1 and 20 Day 2 trials, with 9,000 rows per arm and stage. Donor reassignment duplicates neither biological people nor scoring windows. Day 1 rows provide calibration labels and yield no test score.

Report paired differences for same-person versus unadjusted and versus wrong-person separately by arm, then aligned versus force for the same-person predictions as a secondary descriptive comparison. A positive gain is comparator error minus the adapted error. Report paired group means, positive and zero counts among people, a 95% percentile range from resampling whole donor pairs, and leave-one-pair-out bounds. DEV uses all four two-pair resamples with replacement. TRAIN uses 10,000 draws with NumPy PCG64 seed 10720261119 and the same pair indices for every contrast and metric. Percentiles are linearly interpolated. Pair clusters respect reciprocal donor dependence. Shared TRAIN fit sets still overlap and two DEV pairs offer little resolution. These are descriptive ranges for this dataset and task, without independent-window inference or a predeclared success threshold. A gain against the shared base alone does not establish the usefulness of calibrating to that particular person. Inspection of the signed gains, matched control and their sensitivity governs later acceptance, with FINAL unchanged.

The experiment concerns the released index transducer voltage during the Hyser Random isometric task among adults with intact fingers. Surface EMG measures muscle electrical activity. The grids use the documented common trigger and give nominal timing on released traces. Upstream filtering and causal availability of the distributed force signal are unverified. Clock drift, end-to-end latency and physiological lead time were unmeasured. This work addresses a retrospective forecast of voltage on a single task. It establishes no claim about neural recording, clinical efficacy, causation, real-time control, decoding motion or generalization to another task.
